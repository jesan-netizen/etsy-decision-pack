0. The one paragraph that frames every number below#

The corpus is 73 shops, 40,275 listings, $7.07m gross sticker, and it is not one sample. It is two: 56 young winners (median listing age 4 months, a real 6-month unit window) and 17 master shops (median listing age 12 months, lifetime units only). shops17 contributes 35% of listings and 90% of revenue; its median listing is three times older. Shop age and corpus membership are the same partition (stats_analysis.md §2.1: "there is no shop that appears in both age regimes"). So the two cohorts cannot be added, and every claim in every direction doc was run twice. Where the two disagree, the 6-month young-winner basis wins, because that is the population a launch shop is actually in.

The rule is binding and it is now enforced. In v1 it was declared in this section and then broken in three of the winner's seven score cells. v2 applies it to every cell of the table in §1, and §1.1 audits every launch cell against it.

The yardstick for a new shop, used throughout: $21.56/listing, 2.35 units/listing, 17.5% sold, $6.00 median (56-shop, 6-month). The corpus-wide yardstick on the mixed basis is $175.63–$179.17/listing and that number is not available to anyone starting today.

Three structural facts dominate the whole debate:

  1. Launch-tenure yield is brutal and flat. 0–3 months: 88.0% of listings have never sold, median units 0, $10/listing. 4–6 months: 74.4%. 25–48 months: 21.4%. 49+ months: 0.14%. Revenue per listing runs $3.76 (0–1mo) → $15.23 → $40.56 → $88.64 → $337.74 → $1,467 (24mo+). A listing's winner status is not identifiable in month 1.
  2. The bundle is the largest measured effect in the corpus, and the headline multiple was overstated. Merged, bundles are 3.97% of listings and 32.89% of revenue at 9.32× a single on this pack's own stated 3-way bundle definition (1,397 listings; variant sweep 9.18×–9.50×; set-adjusted 10.27×). The previously published 16.19× is not reproducible at any listing count under any definition tested — classify.is_bundle returns 4.79×, a narrow bundle-plus-gallery-wall word rule returns 15.48×. The honest statement is a range: bundles earn roughly 4.8×–15.5× a single depending on where the class boundary is drawn. The direction and rough magnitude survive every definition. Within-shop median lift is 2.99×, 38 of 51 shops see it. At 0–1 months' tenure, bundle revenue per listing is $15.20 vs $2.70 for singles — 5.6× in the very first month, and bundle revenue share is flat at 28–35% in every age bucket. The 51–200 item bracket returns $4,986.50/listing at 47.30% zero on 241 listings. But launch bundle share has r = 0.009 with eventual shop success: it is necessary, not sufficient.
  3. The price band is the most misused lever in the folder, and the peak is a revenue peak, not a units peak. $3–5 listings are 23% of the catalogue and return $27 each. $15–19 is the peak band on merged revenue per listing at $1,538/listing — 3.17× the $485 corpus baseline, at 47.6% zero, the best sold-rate of any band. It is not the units peak: on the reviewer's re-derivation $20–24 (3.46×) and $25–29 both exceed it, and this pack's own YW arm already put $20–24 at 18.73 u/listing (6.65×) against $15–19's 6.71 u/listing (2.38×). $8–11.99 remains the worst cell in several lanes (nursery $48.86/listing at 84.2% zero; Direction 1's 27 young listings at $9–13 ran 92.6% zero; Direction 12's 31 listings at $20–50 sold zero). Nothing in the corpus says price low to start.

Conditionality, stated once and applying to everything below: every score in §1 is a conditional score. Each one answers "given that a shop reached a certain scale, what does its lane look like?" The decision is unconditional. §0.5 is about the gap.

0.5 The question this pack never asked: what is the base rate for an unselected new shop?#

This is the question that decides the launch, and no document in this folder answers it.

The question, stated precisely. Of 100 shops that open a digital wall-art shop on Etsy today, how many are still trading at month 12? What does the median one earn in its first year? What is the probability that a given attempt clears the kill threshold in §5?

Why the corpus cannot answer it. The 73 shops are 56 "young winners" plus 17 masters — shops already selected for succeeding — plus 4 dead shops that left no listings behind, which is why they cannot even be counted as failures. stats_analysis.md §4.2 puts it plainly: "The 73 shops contain no documented instance of a random shop's first year." There is no denominator of attempts anywhere in this folder. The 82.26% of young-winner listings that have never sold, the 88.0% of listings dead at 0–3 months, and the $3.76 median listing in month 1 all describe the survivors' catalogues, not the population of attempts.

Why that is fatal to the pack's central move. The scoring model has no selection term. Its weights are launch yield (20%), market depth (15%), competition openness (15%), doctrine fit (15%), risk profile (15%), production cost (10%) and IP risk (10%). Not one of them is "probability of surviving to month 12." The expected value of a launch is P(reach scale) × conditional lane yield, and this pack measures only the second factor. A direction can be the best possible bet conditional on getting to scale and still be the wrong thing to spend a month on.

The one score in the pack that acknowledges the gate is Direction 13's launch score of 3 — "it is not a launch" — and Direction 13 ranks 5th.

What would answer it, at three different costs.

Route What it buys What it costs
A fixed-week cohort of all new shops in one lane, tracked to month 12 The only true denominator. Survival, median first-year revenue, distribution not mean Weeks of passive tracking; needs a listing-level scrape at two dates
A seller panel reporting first-year revenue including zeroes The distribution of outcomes, which is the decision-relevant object Access, and self-selection into panels
Etsy-side counts: new-shop openings against shops with ≥1 sale at 12 months, for one lane A coarse survival rate Direction of causality is unresolved; sellers who never list cannot appear at all

Interim treatment, stated so the boss can hold it. Read §1 as an ordering of lanes conditional on execution, not as a probability of success. Price the gate separately: the cheapest tests in §5 cost 8–40 hours each, so the fundable question is "how many attempts can be funded, and what is the per-attempt probability of clearing the kill rule?" — not "which lane is best." Nothing in this pack answers the second half of that sentence either, and it should not be read as if it did.

4.3 Survivorship — the gap between what is scored and what is decided#

Restated here because it is the one caveat that cannot be mitigated by better analysis of this data:

The whole corpus is a winner's sample. 56 young winners + 17 masters, plus 4 dead shops that left no listings behind. Every score in §1 is a conditional probability — given that a shop reached scale, what does its lane look like? The decision is unconditional. The scoring model has no term for selection: launch yield (20%), depth (15%), competition (15%), doctrine (15%), risk (15%), production (10%), IP (10%) — none of them is "probability of surviving to month 12."

The base rate for an unselected new shop is unknown and unknowable from this data. §0.5 states the question, why the corpus cannot answer it, what it would cost to answer it three different ways, and how to hold the gap open while acting anyway.

5. Cheapest test per top-3 direction#

The top three are now 0.15 points apart in total. That is not a reason to test all three; it is a reason to test the cheapest one that discriminates between them. Each test is bounded by a kill rule written before launch.

5.1 D9 — Botanical vintage audit art (rank 1, 6.50)#

Claim to falsify: "count-anchored botanical sets work, they work on day one, and the keyword long tail is real."

The test: 12 listings, 6 masters, 38 hours. Three arms of 4 listings, deliberately matched:

Arm Listings Price What it isolates
A 4 count-anchored sets (3-set, 6-set) $12.99 The doctrine claim
B 4 plain-title sets of identical content $12.99 Whether the count anchor does anything — D9's own data says count anchors are 0.08× at 0–3 months
C 4 count-anchored sets $16.99 Whether the price ladder moves at this size

Budget: 6 × 38 min design = 3.8 h, plus 12 × 15 min listing packs = 3 h, plus 4 mockup composites = 1.3 h. ≈ 8 hours total, plus $4 of PSD conversion. No ads. No Kittl tokens.

The measurement that matters, and it is not revenue: impressions and CTR on the three head long-tails — botanical print set (1,723/73,347), vintage botanical print (1,990/165,743), sage green wall art (1,055/34,518). D9's own gate: three hero sets with fewer than 10 combined impressions at day 14 → swap mockups and re-test; still under 10 at day 21 → kill.

Kill rule (write before publishing): the benchmark is the corpus at 0–3 months' tenure — 0.45 u/listing and $3.33/listing — and D9's own lane rate is worse at 0.30 u/l and $2.11. So: bundles below 0.45 u/listing at day 90 → kill; between 0.30 and 0.45 → the lane is behaving exactly as its own document says it does at launch and the test is inconclusive, extend to day 120. Also kill if arm B outperforms arm A at day 60 — that means count-anchoring is inert here and the doctrine edit is real.

Cost of being wrong: ~8 hours and 12 listings.

5.2 D5 — Y2K / Swedish-Vogue poster (rank 2, 6.40)#

Claim to falsify: "the bundle is the majority of units here and the lane's terms are cheap to enter — so the mechanic is reproducible from outside PixeluxArt."

The test: 8 listings, one hero bundle, ~12 hours. D5's problem is depth, not demand (83% one operator; the 15-shop tail at $13.69/listing), so the test is not "does demand exist" — it is "does a small catalogue move". Build one 120-print bundle at $22.05 with a 15% launch discount, plus 4 $11.07 bundles, plus 3 singles at $6.77. That is the exact shape split the lane's own table identifies (bundle-led $233.97 vs single $53.67).

Budget: 8 prompts × ~30 min including the 1-in-4 reject = 4 h, 8 × 20 min listing packs = 2.7 h, mockups 1 h. ≈ 8 hours.

The measurement: D5's benchmark is precise — PosterluxPrint, 2 months old, 156 listings, 512 sales in 30 days, conversion 3.64%, 94.9% of its catalogue dead. So the test passes on one listing hitting 20 units in 30 days (≈$400 gross) and fails on a catalogue where the bundle sits at zero. Also measure the digital median you are being compared against: D5's recon puts leopard wall art set's digital median at $8.45, not the $14.70 blended.

Kill rule: if the $22.05 bundle has not moved in 30 days, that is the first thing to test, not the last. Switch to $13.16 (the ThatHipPrint bundle-price point) and re-run the same 30 days. If it moves at $13.16 and not at $22.05, the fee-leverage argument is wrong and price is a demand constraint, not a margin one.

Cost of being wrong: ~8 hours and one bundle.

5.3 D4 — Pure vintage bundle shop (rank 3, 6.35)#

Claim to falsify: "a small catalogue (~80 listings, 120 designs) can compete on a page won at 20,000–35,000 prints deep."

The test: 3 listings only, ~14 hours. This direction's uncertainty is not market size — it is whether catalogue depth is load-bearing. So the test is a depth ladder:

Listing Count claim Price Depth required
A 51+ prints $19.99 51 designs
B 120+ prints $19.99 120 designs (same assets as A, plus 69)
C 51+ prints $12.99 identical to A

A vs B isolates whether the count claim converts at all. A vs C isolates whether the $12–20 band clears against a $2.46 page.

Budget: 120 designs × 33 min = 66 h, minus the fact that A needs only 51 designs (28 h). ≈ 40 hours for A+B and ~6 h for C. A cheaper variant: build 51 designs only and list A at $19.99 and C at $12.99 — 28 h, 2 listings, answering the price question while leaving depth open.

The measurement: a silent first month is not a kill condition (88% of listings sell nothing at 0–3 months' tenure). Measure at day 60, not day 30. Target: A at or above the corpus 51–200 bracket rate — and note that the price peak is a revenue-per-listing peak ($15–19 at $1,538/listing), not a units peak, so the target is revenue, not unit count.

Kill rule: if the $19.99 listing holds zero sales at day 60 and the $12.99 version of the identical product holds zero, the price band is not the mechanism and the depth requirement is. Kill at 90 days.

Cost of being wrong: ~40 hours, or 28 in the reduced variant.

  5. A stated unit window for D4's 7.1× age-matched cut. If it is lifetime rather than 6-month, D4's launch cell loses its strongest number and D4's launch score should fall.

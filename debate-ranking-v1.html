0. The one paragraph that frames every number below#

The corpus is 73 shops, 40,275 listings, $7.07m gross sticker, and it is not one sample. It is two: 56 young winners (median listing age 4 months, a real 6-month unit window) and 17 master shops (median listing age 12 months, lifetime units only). shops17 contributes 35% of listings and 90% of revenue; its median listing is three times older. Shop age and corpus membership are the same partition (stats_analysis.md §2.1: "there is no shop that appears in both age regimes"). So the two cohorts cannot be added, and every claim in every direction doc was therefore run twice. Where the two disagree, the 6-month young-winner basis wins, because that is the population a launch shop is actually in.

The yardstick for a new shop, used throughout: $21.56/listing, 2.35 units/listing, 17.5% sold, $6.00 median (56-shop, 6-month). The corpus-wide yardstick on the mixed basis is $175.63–$179.17/listing and that number is not available to anyone starting today.

Three structural facts dominate the whole debate:

  1. Launch-tenure yield is brutal and flat. 0–3 months: 88.0% of listings have never sold, median units 0, $10/listing. 4–6 months: 74.4%. 25–48 months: 21.4%. 49+ months: 0.14%. A listing at 0–3 months delivers 0.26% of the units a 49+ month listing delivers — a factor of 390×. Revenue per listing runs $3.76 (0–1mo) → $15.23 → $40.56 → $88.64 → $337.74 → $1,467 (24mo+). A listing's winner status is not identifiable in month 1. Any direction whose case rests on a mature-shop number is being scored on a number the boss cannot access.
  2. The bundle is the largest measured effect in the corpus, and it is now the largest measured effect in month 1. Merged: bundles are 3.97% of listings and 32.89% of revenue at 16.19× a single. Within-shop median lift 2.99×, 38 of 51 shops see it. At 0–1 months' tenure, bundle revenue per listing is $15.20 vs $2.70 for singles — 5.6× in the very first month, and bundle revenue share is flat at 28–35% in every age bucket. The 51–200 item bracket returns $4,986.50/listing at 47.30% zero on 241 listings. But launch bundle share has r = 0.009 with eventual shop success: it is necessary, not sufficient.
  3. The price band is the most misused lever in the folder. $3–5 listings are 23% of the catalogue and return $27 each. Yield peaks at $13–20 ($464.35/listing merged, $1,306 mature). $8–11.99 is the worst cell in several lanes (nursery $48.86/listing at 84.2% zero; Direction 1's 27 young listings at $9–13 ran 92.6% zero; Direction 12's 31 listings at $20–50 sold zero). Nothing in the corpus says price low to start; Alfie says the opposite ("sometimes you're better off making 10 sales at $2 than zero sales at $5").

3. Head-to-head: where the top directions contradict each other#

3.1 D9 vs D4 — "the bundle works on day one" vs "the bundle needs a catalogue"#

This is the single most important contradiction in the pack, because it is a direct conflict between two corpus-verified claims and both are true at different tenures.

  • D4's position: Wave 1 is explicit that bundle-first launch is contradicted by Alfie's own doctrine — singles are the raw material, the mega bundle is what you build in September, and "a bundle with no catalogue has nothing to bundle." D4's plan therefore publishes 80 listings and 120 designs by day 30 and accepts a silent month 1 (88% zero at 0–3 months).
  • D9's position: bundles win at 0–3 months' tenure — $7.44/listing at 0.99 u/l and 5.9% sold, against $2.11 and 0.30 u/l for botanical singles and $3.33 for the entire corpus at that tenure. 3.5× singles and 2.2× the whole corpus on day one.

Adjudication. Both numbers are in the corpus and they are not the same measurement. shops17_analysis.md §6b measures bundle revenue per listing at 0–1 months: $15.20 vs $2.70 for singles, a 5.6× ratio in the very first month — that is corpus-wide, not lane-specific, and it directly contradicts the "silent month 1" reading of the bundle shape specifically. What is silent in month 1 is the singles catalogue, not the bundle. The two claims reconcile exactly: bundles are the only shape that pays before reviews exist; singles are what you need before the whole-shop bundle at month 3–4 has anything to bundle.

So the contradiction is real but it is a sequencing disagreement, not an empirical one — and it is worth noting that D9's own plan (46 listings in 30 days, 120 designs, ~53 h) is already the D4 sequencing. The directions are closer on this than their rhetoric suggests. What D9 claims that D4 does not is that its lane's bundle advantage is larger than the corpus baseline on day one (2.2× corpus, against a corpus-wide 5.6× bundle-to-single ratio that still leaves bundles at $15.20 vs $3.33 corpus). D9's advantage is real but it is 2.2× against a baseline of 4.6× — meaning D9's day-one bundles do not beat the corpus day-one bundles by as much as the doc's framing implies. That is a correction the doc does not make itself.

3.2 D4 vs D5 — "the lane is 2.71× the corpus" vs "the boundary is doing the work"#

  • D4: narrow vintage_gallery cut = 7,809 listings, $484.84/listing, 2.71× efficiency, 52.5% of revenue.
  • D4's own recomputation, wider marker set: 27,372 listings, $223.23/listing, 1.25×; excluding one $2,063.81 outlier, $193.68, 1.08×. Three shops supply 72.4%.
  • D5: the y2k aesthetic's yield is $113.93/listing against $144.97 for the no clear aesthetic bucket — below the uncommitted corpus median. Naming this aesthetic does not buy the premium.

Adjudication with numbers. The vintage lane's apparent superiority is between 1.08× and 2.71× depending entirely on where the keyword boundary is drawn — a 2.5× swing on an analyst's vocabulary choice. That is not a finding; it is a measurement artefact, and D4's own doc says so first and loudest. Strip the three dominant shops and the wide cut is roughly corpus parity. The durable finding in both lanes is not the lane yield — it is the bundle multiple. D4 has 16.19× (honest range 8.7–17.7×); D5 has 4.4×. On the shape, D4 wins by 2–4×, and that number is stable across both corpora, both classifiers, and matched age bands (p < 0.001 independently).

The second adjudication is about concentration, where D5 is worse and D4 is also bad but differently: D5's PixeluxArt + PosterluxPrint = 83.0% of lane revenue and 81.5% of units, and the folder's Phase 2 pack calls them near-clones on the same titles — so the apparent pool is half its size. D4's three dominant shops (NorthPrints, LoveDictionary, HarmonyPixels) hold 70.7% of master revenue and, critically, NorthPrints got to $1,423.84/listing with 41.7% bundle share, 1,792 listings and 73 months — i.e. without the doctrine. D4's own doc states the honest version: "NorthPrints proves that 73 months and 1,792 listings at one price point produce $1,423/listing in vintage, which is an argument about age, not about bundles."

3.3 D5 vs D2 — "the keyword long tail is real" vs "the long tail is an artefact"#

Both directions found a vol/comp outlier and both claim it is the strongest new evidence in the project. They cannot both be right about the mechanism, though both can be right about the number.

  • D2: bible verse wall art gingham vol 183 / comp 1,091 = 0.168, 29× better than the head term's 0.0057. Defence: Everbee scores on those terms are 170, 90 and 130 — an order of magnitude below the misspelling variants D2 discarded at 8,410, 8,560 and 12,020.
  • D5: leopard wall art set 0.072, vintage vogue poster 0.066, leopard poster 0.046. y2k aesthetic wall art 15% ads, fashion poster wall art 8% ads.
  • Against both: everbee_keywords.md warns that "suggestion lists are dominated by misspelling variants with inflated scores (Everbee score artefact), not usable demand signals", and D6's doc found that its own attractive ratios (artful line art at 2.39) came from exactly that family.

Adjudication. The distinguishing test is volume, not ratio, and on volume the two are not comparable. D2's terms carry 183, 181 and 120 monthly searches — real, but a combined market of roughly 1,979 monthly searches across three long-tail phrases. D5's head ratio of 0.072 sits on leopard wall art set, and D5's live recon of that exact term shows 25 of 60 cards digital at a $8.45 median, 35% ads — i.e. the term has real digital supply competing on it, which is what a genuine 0.072 ratio looks like, and is not what a 183-volume phrase looks like.

But the adjudication cuts back on volume too. A 183-a-month market with 1,091 competing listings is a market where the entire opportunity is roughly 180 sales a month across every shop on earth. D2's own month-one projection is $101, and its own doc says break-even is 4–8 months if the texture holds. So the honest ranking on competition openness is: D5's ratios sit on bigger, more contested markets; D2's ratios sit on markets too small to be a business. That is why D5 scores 8 and D2 scores 7 on this criterion despite D2's headline ratio being numerically higher — and why both lose the criterion to D9, whose four terms are all above 1,000 monthly volume with real ratios and which is corroborated by an independent live recon showing the download page at 79.7% digital, 16 Bestseller badges and the top shop holding only 1 of 64 slots.

3.4 D8 vs D3 — the same lane, two verdicts, and the corpus sides with neither#

D3 and D8 are the same lane (nursery/kids, 5,237–5,692 merged listings) built by two agents, and they contradict each other on the central question.

  • D3 says: the lane beats the rest of the corpus at every age a launch shop reaches, 1.6×–2.2× age-matched ($9.64 vs $5.87 at 1–3mo; $56.44 vs $28.94 at 4–6; $107.11 vs $61.33 at 7–12; $546.88 vs $250.81 at 13–24). The 51–200 bracket is the best cell in the lane at $2,658.79/listing and 42.3% zero, and 89.3% of the lane's listings sit in the 200+ bracket returning $132.95. "The lane's dominant format is its worst format. That is an opening, not a ceiling."
  • D8 says: the lane's yield is 0.98× the corpus blended average, 0.78× on units, and 5.4 points worse survival. "It is not a bad lane. It is not the best lane." And critically: the gift occasion is the weakest segment, not the strongest — baby-shower listings earn $81 against a $172 baseline, −47%.

Adjudication. D3's age-matched table is the better measurement — it holds tenure constant, which removes the exact confound that stats_analysis.md §2.1 warns dominates this corpus. But D3's advantage is a price effect, not a demand effect, and D8's objection is the one that bites: on units per listing the lane is 0.78× the corpus, and the corpus's own rank test puts nursery significantly below its complement (Cliff's delta −0.048, p < 0.001). D3 concedes this in its own §10b.2 — "the 2.2× revenue advantage is a price effect, and a price effect can be competed away."

Then the decisive number, and it comes from D3's own case AGAINST: two of the three shops in the corpus that run a majority-nursery catalogue at a young age are dead. ThePlumAtelier closed at 11 months on the highest revenue in the pack (827 of 983 listings at one price point, 90.4% zero); LinoaVintage closed at 93.6% zero. The lane's young-age survival rate is 0 for 2. D8 adds that the two pure-nursery shops that did scale are 38 and 46 months old, and that StylingHerLife — one of them — has conversion 1.58, the second lowest in the master set.

Resolution: the lane's ceiling is real and its launch curve is fatal. Both directions are correct and they are describing different halves of the same distribution. This is why D3 scores 7 on launch (the best modelled month one in the pack: $426 advertised on 16 listings) and 4 on risk, and D8 scores 5 on launch and 9 on doctrine. D3's "$546.88/listing at month 13" is the same fact as D8's "the two pure shops that scaled are 38 and 46 months old."

3.5 D13 vs everything — "physical is the endgame" vs "physical is 0.9% of the corpus"#

A 10.1× over-index on 0.9% of corpus revenue carried by 6 shops is not a market, it is a collection of six decisions that happened to work. The honest read: physical is a margin upgrade on an existing shop, not a market to enter. D13 says this itself ("it is a 10-shop behaviour", "keep as endgame"). That is why it scores 6th, not 1st, despite having the best competition openness score in the entire pack (wall art canvas at 2 ads of 66 cards is the softest head measured in this entire project) — the criterion is measuring an empty shelf in a shop's own future, not an entry point.

  3. Merged zero rate reverses depending on which question you ask. In yw alone, zero rate is not a differentiator (TOP10 84.41% vs REST 82.03%). Merged, it is 36.21% vs 77.10%. The reason is survivorship: the s17 shops that made the list have run 4+ years with continuously refreshed inventory. A young winner cannot copy "36% zero" — they have not had the four years of pruning that produced it.
  4. Non-monotonicity in the only within-corpus shop-age comparison available: 13–24 months (59.6% zero) is barely better than 25–48 months (60.8%), then 49–96 months drops to 20.3%. With 4 shops and 6 shops in those arms, that flat step is as likely composition as age.
  5. The 12-month listing spike is not a finding: only 239 listings reach 12 months, from 6 shops, concentrated in two high performers.
  6. Direction 13's premise is age-structural: 1 of 50 shops under 12 months runs a physical tier at ≥20% of revenue. That is not a market-entry signal; it is a statement about what happens after month 12.

Structural artefacts of the corpus itself.

  • The corpus is one vertical. 66.9% of titles contain "digital download", 36.5% "printable", 58.2% "wall art"/"print"/"poster". There is no meaningful clipart (2 titles), planner (4), sticker (1) or SVG (1) lane. Any plan built on this corpus is a digital-print playbook, not a general Etsy playbook.
  • Revenue is gross sticker, before fees, before discounts, before COGS. Direction 4's doc states the consequence plainly: "Every figure in this document is a gross sticker proxy before fees, before the ~25% discount that 60% of the live page carries, and before COGS."
  • The whole corpus is a winner's sample. 56 "young winners" + 17 masters — shops already selected for succeeding. The 73 shops contain no documented instance of a random shop's first year, because the dead ones (4 of them) left no listings behind. The base rate for an unselected new shop is unknown and unknowable from this data.
  • No time series per direction. Units are one window per shop. "Trajectory" per direction is not computable.
  • Lane rankings are shop rankings in disguise. stats_analysis.md §2.4: "Read them as 'this shop's assortment', never as 'this niche works'." The coastal/landscape result is one shop — remove NorthPrints and the lane ranking changes completely.
  • The three live-recon passes disagree materially. D3's CDP pass and its sibling agent's card-level pass were taken the same day and differ by 19 points on ad share (37.1% vs 17.8%) and 15 points on digital share (11.1% vs 26.5%). D3's own doc: "The reconciliation in §3c is an argument, not a measurement."
  • The 17-shop average is a NorthPrints statistic. Three shops hold 70.7% of master revenue; the top four hold 77.2%.

5. Cheapest test per top-3 direction#

Each test is designed to falsify the direction's central claim at the lowest possible cost, and each is bounded by a kill rule written before launch.

5.1 D9 — Botanical vintage audit art#

Claim to falsify: "count-anchored botanical sets work, they work on day one, and the keyword long tail is real."

The test: 12 listings, 6 masters, 38 hours. Three arms of 4 listings, deliberately matched:

Arm Listings Price What it isolates
A 4 count-anchored sets (3-set, 6-set) $12.99 The doctrine claim
B 4 plain-title sets of identical content $12.99 Whether the count anchor does anything — D9's own data says count anchors are 0.08× at 0–3 months
C 4 count-anchored sets $16.99 Whether the price ladder moves at this size

Two masters per arm, so 6 masters generate 54 files across 9 listings each (one plate → nine listings). Budget: 6 × 38 min design = 3.8 h, plus 12 × 15 min listing packs = 3 h, plus 4 mockup composites = 1.3 h. ≈ 8 hours total, plus $4 of PSD conversion. No ads. No Kittl tokens.

The measurement that matters, and it is not revenue: impressions and CTR on the three head long-tails — botanical print set (1,723/73,347), vintage botanical print (1,990/165,743), sage green wall art (1,055/34,518). D9's own gate: three hero sets with fewer than 10 combined impressions at day 14 → swap mockups and re-test; still under 10 at day 21 → kill.

Kill rule (write before publishing): bundles under 0.99 u/listing at day 90 → the lane's own measured 0–3-month rate, so this is the benchmark, not a target. Also kill if arm B outperforms arm A at day 60 — that means count-anchoring is inert here and the doctrine edit in §3.1 is real.

Cost of being wrong: ~8 hours and 12 listings.

5.2 D5 — Y2K / Swedish-Vogue poster#

Claim to falsify: "the bundle is the majority of units here and the lane's terms are cheap to enter — so the mechanic is reproducible from outside PixeluxArt."

The test: 8 listings, one hero bundle, ~12 hours. Because D5's problem is depth, not demand (83% one operator; the 15-shop tail at $13.69/listing), the test is not "does demand exist" — it is "does a small catalogue move". Build one 120-print bundle at $22.05 with a 15% launch discount (D5's own plan price), plus 4 $11.07 bundles, plus 3 singles at $6.77. That is the exact shape split the lane's own table identifies (bundle-led $233.97 vs single $53.67).

Budget: 8 prompts × ~30 min including the 1-in-4 reject = 4 h, 8 × 20 min listing packs = 2.7 h, mockups 1 h. ≈ 8 hours.

The measurement: D5's own benchmark is precise — PosterluxPrint, 2 months old, 156 listings, 512 sales in 30 days, conversion 3.64% (the highest of any shop in the corpus), 94.9% of its catalogue dead. So the test passes on one listing hitting 20 units in 30 days (≈$400 gross, D5's own arithmetic) and fails on a catalogue where the bundle sits at zero. Also measure the digital median you are being compared against: D5's recon puts leopard wall art set's digital median at $8.45, not the $14.70 blended.

Kill rule: if the $22.05 bundle has not moved in 30 days, that is the first thing to test, not the last (D5's own instruction). Specifically: switch to $13.16 (the ThatHipPrint bundle-price point) and re-run the same 30 days. If it moves at $13.16 and not at $22.05, the fee-leverage argument in D5's doctrine case is wrong and the price is a demand constraint, not a margin one.

Cost of being wrong: ~8 hours and one bundle.

5.3 D4 — Pure vintage bundle shop#

Claim to falsify: "a small catalogue (~80 listings, 120 designs) can compete on a page won at 20,000–35,000 prints deep."

The test: 3 listings only, ~14 hours. This direction's uncertainty is not market size — it is whether catalogue depth is load-bearing. So the test is a depth ladder, not a catalogue:

Listing Count claim Price Depth required
A 51+ prints $19.99 51 designs
B 120+ prints $19.99 120 designs (same assets as A, plus 69)
C 51+ prints $12.99 identical to A

A vs B isolates whether the count claim converts at all. A vs C isolates whether the $12–20 band clears against a $2.46 page. Together they answer the two things D4's own doc says "this corpus cannot answer": "Whether 120 designs can compete with 35,000" and "Whether a $19.99 tier clears against a $2.46 page."

Budget: 120 designs × 33 min = 66 h, minus the fact that A needs only 51 designs (28 h). ≈ 40 hours for A+B and ~6 h for C. That is the one test in this table that is genuinely expensive, and the expense is the question. A cheaper variant: build 51 designs only and list A at $19.99 and C at $12.99 — 28 h, 2 listings, and it answers the price question while leaving the depth question open.

The measurement: D4's own hard rule — a silent first month is not a kill condition (88% of listings sell nothing at 0–3 months' tenure, median zero units). Measure at day 60, not day 30. Target: A at or above the corpus 51–200 bracket rate of 89.35 u/listing — but at 60 days on a 51-design bundle, the realistic read is bundle revenue per listing ≥ $15.20, the corpus's 0–1-month bundle benchmark.

Kill rule: if the $19.99 listing holds zero sales at day 60 and the $12.99 version of the identical product holds zero, the price band is not the mechanism and the depth requirement is. Kill at 90 days.

Cost of being wrong: ~40 hours, or 28 in the reduced variant.

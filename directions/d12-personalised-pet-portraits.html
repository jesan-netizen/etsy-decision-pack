1. The direction in one paragraph#

A personalised pet portrait is a piece of wall art built from one customer's photograph of their own animal, sold as an instant digital download. The unit of sale is not a print; it is a commission executed to a fixed template. The lane's economics live in three places and nowhere else: the AOV (it is the only lane in the corpus where a $19.96 digital listing out-earns a $99.96 one is not the story — the story is that the bundle carries it), the repeat rate on one listing (CreaPawsCo's single hero did 413 units in six months off a $59.88 sticker), and the per-order labour the buyer is implicitly buying.

The aesthetic is not free. Unlike every other direction in this batch, the shop cannot be "one aesthetic, many niches" with the niches chosen freely — the customer's photo dictates the subject, so the shop's only real design decision is which room the pet is standing in. The corpus is unambiguous that the room is the product: funny dog bathroom art is the exact phrase on 68.6% of CreaPawsCo's titles, and Custom Pet Portrait Funny Dog Bathroom Art alone is 47.6% of that shop's units. The shape strategy is therefore one aesthetic (humour-gouache-pet-in-a-scene), many niches (the scenes), bundled backward — a set of 4 is the primary SKU, the B3G1 single is the traffic SKU, and the bare portrait is the price-anchor SKU that makes the set look cheap.

What makes this direction different from the other eleven: the customer supplies the raw material, so the product is half-made before the shop touches it. That is simultaneously the moat (a competitor cannot batch-produce your review moat) and the ceiling (nothing here scales without a human in the loop, and the human is paid per order, not per listing). Every number in this document is in service of answering one question: is the per-order human cheap enough at this AOV that the lane beats the catalogue-ladder lanes on contribution per hour of the founder's time?

3a. The single most important finding in this document#

The head terms are not a digital market. They are a handmade-commissions market.

On custom pet portrait, 14 of 37 organic results are physical craft — hand-painted oil on canvas ($136.30), stained-glass suncatchers ($22.33), 3D-sculpted resin ring dishes ($27.22), embroidery hoops ($11.99), ceramic fridge magnets ($14.24), laser-cut metal portraits ($42.99). On pet portrait wall art the physical-craft median is $66.11 against a whole-page median of $20.78. On custom pet portrait from photo zero of 36 organic results is a digital download.

Two consequences, both good:

  1. A digital-download shop is not competing for these slots head-on. The overlap is thin. On the two most digital-intent queries — custom pet portrait digital download (9 digital-looking results, median $20.00) and funny dog bathroom art (6, median $16.36) — the digital shops are already there, and those pages are the least defended in the whole set: median 11 and 26 reviews, 0 and 6 listings with 100+ reviews, zero-review listings 10/20 and 4/20.
  2. The incumbents' moat is deeper than in any other lane in this batch. Median page-one listing review count is 68–88 on the four head terms, against the corpus-wide live_recon.md finding of median 1 on christian wall art. 13–16 of 31–37 organic slots carry a Bestseller badge. 145 distinct shops appear across the seven pages and only 52 appear on more than one query — the top repeater, GoodSchmidtDesigns, takes 17 slots, and ArtTailCreations and onemooregift take 9 each. This lane is long-tail-shaped, not head-shaped: it is won by a catalogue that covers many scenes, not by winning one keyword.

3b. What a zero-review shop is up against here, specifically#

  • Review depth, not ad depth, is the gate. Ads are 38–42% of cards on all seven queries — unremarkable, same band as the faith lane's 36%. But personalized pet portrait has a page-one median of 88 reviews and custom pet portrait from photo has 76. There is no low-review headroom to hide in, the way there is in printable wall art (85% digital, a commodity race).
  • Bestseller is taken on roughly 40% of organic slots (13, 13, 12, 15, 16 across five queries) and Star Seller on zero — that badge is genuinely reachable for a new shop, because it is a service-history badge, and it is the single cheapest credibility signal available at launch.
  • Discounting is mandatory and deep. Across 119 live sale observations on page one, the median discount is 40% (mode 50%, 27/119). A $59.88 sticker is being shown to shoppers as roughly $36.
  • The room-niche pages are the open door. funny dog bathroom art: median 11 reviews, zero listings with 100+ reviews, 10 of 20 with no reviews at all, median $15.51, 42% ads. That is the closest thing to a page-one position available to a new zero-review shop anywhere in this lane, and it is precisely the phrase CreaPawsCo's $24,730 hero is built on.

Description approach#

Six blocks, in this order, because the buyer's questions arrive in this order:

  1. "Is this my dog?" — the sample gallery, 8–12 samples, all from the same scene style, captioned with the pet's actual name and a note that these are real customer submissions (or, at launch, honest samples). This block decides the sale; everything after it is logistics.
  2. "How do I do this?" — 3 numbered steps: order → reply with 3–5 clear photos in daylight → receive files within 24–48 hours. State the turnaround promise in the first 40 words. Turnaround speed is the one thing every case file flags as this lane's constraint.
  3. "What do I get?" — exact file list with pixel dimensions (the pipeline in §8c emits 9 ratio files), print-quality DPI, and colour profile.
  4. "How do I print it?" — named print shops, plus the free-printable-size list, plus the one-line "this is a digital file, no physical item ships" in bold. Getting this wrong is the top cause of 1-star reviews in this lane.
  5. "Photo tips" — the single highest-leverage block and the one no competitor on page one bothers with: shoot in daylight, square to the face, eyes visible, one close + one full-body if a scene needs the body. Better input photos mean fewer revisions, and fewer revisions is the labour control.
  6. Reviews / trust — Star Seller badge, review count, turnaround history, revision policy (one free revision, 48-hour window).

7. Concepts — the aesthetic bible#

The one-line spine#

The customer's pet, painted in a warm gouache, standing in a specific room of a real house, doing something that room is for.

Every design decision below exists to protect that sentence. If a design breaks it, it does not ship.

Palette#

Warm neutrals as the ground, saturated accent used once per composition at ≤15% of the frame.

Role Colours
Ground / walls #F6F1E7 cream · #EFE7D9 oat · #E3DDD0 putty
Warm mid #D8C4A4 sand · #C9A87C tan · #A8703F terracotta
Accent (one only) #6E8B6B sage · #B4694F clay · #5F7C8A slate blue · #C97B5A rust
Ink / line #3A322B warm charcoal, never pure black — pure black reads as clip-art
Memorial variant only #8E9AA6 mist grey · #C9C2B4 dove

Rule: the pet's own coat colour is the only saturated mass in the frame. If the scene competes with the pet for colour weight, the pet stops being the subject. This is why every scene background is a neutral.

Medium#

Opaque gouache with visible brush edge and a light ink contour on the face and paws. Not watercolour (reads as thin and cheap at thumbnail), not oil (too dark for a bathroom), not flat vector (reads as a $6 sticker, and the page-one digital median is $8–9). Gouache is the one medium that survives being shrunk to a 200px Etsy thumbnail, which is where the decision actually gets made.

Light#

Single soft source, upper-left, at roughly 30° — consistent across the entire catalogue. The light direction never changes between listings. One soft cast shadow on the floor plane, no drama. A bathroom print and a laundry print lit from different angles is the fastest way to destroy a one-aesthetic brand, and it is invisible to the buyer who only sees one listing but obvious in the shop grid.

Composition rules#

  1. Head occupies 45–55% of frame height. Measured on my own test output (d12_test/), a tight crop put the crown within a few pixels of the top edge and clipped both ears — sellable for one dog, unusable as a template for a dog with big ears.
  2. Three-quarter view, eyes on the upper third line. Eyes are the emotional anchor and the first thing the buyer checks.
  3. The room is named, not implied. A towel rail, a toilet roll, a washing machine drum, an oven mitt — one unmistakable prop per scene, readable at thumbnail size.
  4. Two sizes only for the pet: head-and-shoulders (default) and full-body (scene sets only). Full-body in a narrow portrait ratio fails; the pet goes small and the prop becomes the subject.
  5. Negative space at the top 15% for gallery-wall hanging and to survive varied uploads.
  6. Never more than two props. More than two and it reads as a gift-shop tea towel.

8. Production plan#

8a. What AI generation actually does — measured, not assumed#

I ran the workflow on a real CC-licensed photograph (d12_test/dog_src.jpg, a cream Golden Retriever, public domain CC-BY from Wikimedia Commons) with image-to-image generation. Three outputs, judged blind against the reference.

# Target style Output Verdict
1 Hand-painted watercolour + ink, faithful likeness t1_watercolour.png (1152×864) Style: strong. Likeness: FAILED. The style is a convincing digital gouache/watercolour hybrid at thumbnail — overlapping translucent washes, granulation, pigment pooling, untouched paper as highlight. But the likeness is genericised into "a retriever": coat markings simplified into decorative islands, ear construction inconsistent left-vs-right, asymmetric eyes, several doubled/ghosted contours, abrupt artificial wash edges. A customer would receive a well-made picture of a golden retriever, not their golden retriever. At the $46.13 AOV this is a 1-star and a refund, not a sale.
2 Minimalist single-weight black ink line drawing t2_lineart.png Style: PASSED. Likeness: acceptable. Clean continuous primary contours, 94% pure white background, 4.7% ink coverage, no unwanted shading, no stray marks, no malformed anatomy (ears, eyes, muzzle, nose all coherent). Line weight varies but reads as intentional fur texture. This is the style that survives AI production — because it carries less surface detail, so there is less detail to get wrong. This is the finding that decides the product: the cheaper-looking style is the safer style.
3 Humour scene composite (bathtub spa, gouache) t3_spa.png Style: PASSED. Full-scene narrative composition works well: prop, robe, mask, towel turban, tiled wall, cheerful palette, all clean. The scene carries the emotion; the pet is a recognisable dog in it. This is the commercially right format and it is also the format where genericisation matters least — because in the bathroom-spa scene the buyer is buying "my dog in a funny place", and scene accuracy is the promise, not facial micro-fidelity.

The honest workflow verdict, in three lines:

  1. Do not sell a bare photoreal-faithful likeness from AI. Output #1 proves it genericises. Either use the line-art style (#2), where the buyer accepts stylisation because the medium is stylised, or use the scene composite (#3), where the scene is the product.
  2. The scene composite is the CreaPawsCo/UnspirationalLiving mechanic and it works with AI. Their top six listings across both shops are all scene sets (spa, toilet, laundry, kitchen, bar, golf). This direction's entire corpus evidence already points at the one format AI can execute.
  3. The revision loop is where AI fails hardest. A customer who receives a genericised likeness will ask for a revision, and the revision is another prompt that genericises differently. This is why the pricing ladder must put the buyer in the scene format (where likeness matters less) and the price anchor ($70.00) must be explicitly labelled a stylised illustration.

10. The case, weighed#

10a. The strong case FOR, with numbers#

  1. The yield gap is the widest in the corpus and it survives every classifier. $340.08/listing (6-month) or $808.40 (lifetime) against $12.54 / $175.95 for the corpus minus this lane — 4.6x to 27x, never below 4x. Against the directions in this batch: the vault's own analogue is 53.5x for botanical bundles and 11.7x for vintage; this lane's numbers sit in the same order of magnitude and it is the only lane where the raw (unbundled) listing outperforms.

  2. The AOV ladder has a dead middle, which is a gift, not a risk. $12–20 and $50+ take 90.2% of lane units; the 31 listings priced $20–50 sold zero. A competitor who prices sanely in the middle is not competing with you.

  3. The benchmark shop is flat-and-up, and the vault's decay flag does not reproduce. Fresh Everbee pull: CreaPawsCo $6,089.68 in 30 days, within 0.3% of the 28 Sep six-month estimate of $6,074/month — 1.44x its own lifetime pace at month 13, conversion 3.12%, 132 orders. The 0.33x decay figure belongs to UnspirationalLiving, where only 42 of 253 listings are digital and its personalised-pet wing is 63; its dollar revenue held at 0.94x lifetime pace while its unit count halved. The premise in the brief that "the fresh 30-day evidence on the personalised lane is decay" is not supported once the physical/digital mix is separated. I am flagging this because it is the load-bearing objection to this direction and I could not reproduce it.

  4. The best keyword ratio ever measured in this project. pet portrait from photo: vol/comp 0.0744, 12x better than the best Wave 1 head. And the lane rewards specificity — dog wall art at 0.0006 is a trap, pet portrait from photo at 0.0744 is an opportunity.

  5. Per-order labour is $1.33–$6.67, not the blocker the vault implies. Contribution stays at 39–46% of sticker from zero labour to 20 minutes at $20/hr, because AOV is $46.13. 73–79 orders/month clears $2,000 of overhead. The vault's "per-order labour is the anti-scaling risk" is true as an accounting fact and false as a constraint at this AOV — it only bites if revisions are uncontrolled, and one clause controls them.

  6. The open door is the room niche, and it is the room CreaPawsCo's hero is built on. funny dog bathroom art: median 11 page-one reviews, 0 of 20 listings with 100+, median $15.51, 42% ads. Against median 68–88 reviews on the four head terms. The lane's head is defended by handmade commissions; its scenes are not defended at all.

  7. The format AI can actually execute is the format the corpus has already proven. Top six listings across both shops are all scene sets. AI's scene composite passed; AI's photoreal-faithful likeness failed on the same reference photo.

  8. It is a catalogue that scales without a catalogue. 24 scene templates × 4 compositions × 2 tints = 192 designs with zero new aesthetic decisions, and listing #16 (whole-house bundle) is the pinnable featured listing Wave 1 recommends.

10b. The honest case AGAINST, with numbers#

  1. 93.2% of the lane's revenue is one shop, and 88.6% of that shop's units are three listings, two of which are byte-identical titles. Six of the ten shops in the lane have sold zero. This is not a proven repeatable model; it is one shop's three listings. The single most important lesson in CreaPawsCo may be duplicate-listing arbitrage, not the concept — and a new shop cannot rely on Etsy continuing to surface two identical titles.

  2. 83.5% of personalised-pet listings sold nothing in six months (against 73–91% corpus-wide, so this lane is better than the corpus — but 96 of 115 listings have still to earn their keep). ArttHouses put 21 listings in and got $988 — $47 per listing, a third of CreaPawsCo's $1,918.

  3. The head terms are not this lane's market. custom pet portrait from photo and pet wall art returned zero digital-download results on page one. 14 of 37 organic slots on custom pet portrait are physical craft at a $66.11 median. A digital shop is buying the tail of a market whose head it cannot enter — which means volume comes from catalogue breadth, not from winning a term.

  4. The incumbents' moat is the deepest in this batch. Median page-one listing reviews of 68, 76, 88, 68 on the four head terms against the corpus's median of 1. Bestseller on 12–16 of 31–37 organic slots. Zero Star Seller badges on page one is the one genuine opening, and it is a service badge you earn over months.

  5. AI genericises likeness, and this lane's product is likeness. Test 1 on a real CC-licensed photo produced a well-made picture of a golden retriever rather than that retriever — simplified markings, mismatched ears, asymmetric eyes. The mitigation is to sell the scene, not the face — but that is a positioning retreat, and it means the customer is buying "my dog in a funny bathroom", which is a narrower and more trend-dependent promise than "my dog's portrait".

  6. Revenue is flat, not compounding. CreaPawsCo is at 0.91x its own 6-month pace and 1.44x lifetime pace — flat. 13 months, 20 listings, $6.1k/month, and it added listings (20 listings on a 13-month shop = 1.5/month) that mostly sell nothing: 35% dead, and the 6 listings that did sell sit in a $59.88/$19.96 two-price ladder. There is no evidence this lane compounds. The endgame thesis in the vault is "launch = bundle-led catalogue, endgame = personalised hero" (vault_synthesis.md §1 verdict) — the endgame has an entry price that is far above the launch price and a revenue shape ($6k flat) that is not obviously worth a second shop.

  7. It is a service business wearing a catalogue's clothes. Per-order work means: no weekend, no batching, a support inbox, a revision policy, and the founder is the production line. The vault's per-order-labour caution survives, just relocated — not to the margin (39–46% is fine) but to the founder's calendar. 180 orders/month = 12 hours/month of production, but 180 separate conversations, and one that goes wrong takes longer than ten that go well.

  8. Turnaround is the constraint every case file names, and it is a Star Seller dependency. CreaPawsCo's own prior comment: "turnaround speed is the constraint." At 24–48 hours with one free revision, a bad week (illness, a difficult photo, a model failure) directly damages the 21% review rate that is the lane's real conversion engine.

  9. Q4 timing is against it. Launching a personalised gift lane in late October means competing with established shops already discounting a seasonal category, and this lane's own seasonal wing (Series C) is the part that needs 60 days of lead time.

The evidence splits cleanly and the split is not a matter of interpretation.

If the question is "which lane has the highest revenue per listing", this is the answer and it is not close: $340/listing at 6 months, $808 at lifetime, 4.6x–27x the corpus baseline, with a proven shop at $6,090/month on 20 listings, 3.12% conversion and 21% review rate, holding flat and above its lifetime pace at month 13. Three things the boss should weigh that cut across the doc:

  1. The decay premise in the brief did not reproduce. Fresh pull: CreaPawsCo $6,089.68/30 days, flat-to-up, conversion 3.12%. The 0.33x belongs to a shop that is 83% physical by listing count.

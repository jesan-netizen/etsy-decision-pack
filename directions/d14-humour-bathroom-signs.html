14. Humour wall art with a bathroom-sign specialisation

A small, room-specific humour print shop whose entire catalogue is one joke per design, hung above one toilet. The lane is "funny bathroom wall art" and its three sub-heads — funny toilet signs, cheeky guest-WC prints, and Airbnb…

Compiled 2 Oct 2026. UK English. No listings created, no Kittl tokens spent.

Corpus: 73 shops (56 young winners + 17 masters), 40,275 listing rows, 662,217 lifetime units, 62,188 six-month units (the six-month column exists only on the 56 young-winner rows). 1. The direction in one paragraph#

A small, room-specific humour print shop whose entire catalogue is one joke per design, hung above one toilet. The lane is "funny bathroom wall art" and its three sub-heads — funny toilet signs, cheeky guest-WC prints, and Airbnb house-rules prints. The aesthetic is flat, hand-lettered or naive-illustration typography with one loud background colour and a sage/pink stripe frame, sold almost entirely as a digital download because the pocket's buying price on the live boards is $5.78–$6.95. The shape strategy is singles plus one 3-pack and one 9-pack, designed bundle-backward: all nine designs in the pack exist as individual listings first, so the pack is an inventory discount on assets already made, not a separate production run. The audience is three buyers in one: (a) the person buying a gift for a house move or a new bathroom, (b) the Airbnb host who wants a rules sign that is not a black plastic plaque from a hardware shop, and (c) the parent decorating a kid's or a guest WC. The thesis is that the pocket is a lane inside a humour shop, not a niche store, and the honest version of the direction is a humour shop that treats the bathroom as its best-converting room — which is exactly what the corpus says the one real incumbent does.

2.2 The headline is one shop, and it is decaying#

Bathroom pocket: 26.79 units per listing against a corpus of 16.44 is the number that launched this direction. It is not a property of the pocket.

Thelittleprintshopx holds 14,327 of the pocket's 21,943 lifetime units — 65.3% — across 117 of its 549 listings. Strip it and the pocket's yield falls from 26.79 to 10.85, i.e. below the corpus average of 16.44 and below humour-at-large (14.62). Concentration inside the pocket: top 1 listing = 17.3% of pocket units, top 5 = 34.7%, top 10 = 44.9%, top 25 = 64.8%, top 50 = 81.4%. Two facts sit side by side here and they are the crux of the direction.

The $12–15 band's 57.7% share of pocket units is TLPX's single $13.36 price point (428 of its 549 listings sit at $13.36). One shop's price is the band's demand curve.

In the young-winner digital shops, the curve inverts. Units per listing fall monotonically as price rises: 7.30 at under $4, 4.17 at $6–8, 3.01 at $8–10, 1.59 at $10–12, 0.88 at $12–15, and exactly zero at $15 and above. A new digital shop in this lane cannot price its way up. The corpus's own money shape — a physical $13.36 print — is not available to a download-only shop; the head terms are physical (§3).

  • CreaPawsCo is not a bathroom shop. It is a personalised pet-photo shop: 12 of its 20 listings are "Custom Pet Portrait Funny Dog Bathroom Art" at $19.96–$59.88. 7. Concepts — the aesthetic bible#

7a. The look#

The pocket's live aesthetic is not free-floating jokes on white. It is one loud flat background colour, one hand-lettered line, one sage or pink vertical stripe at the edge, generous margin, no border, no frame mockup inside the artwork.

  • Medium: flat vector-style digital illustration or hand-lettered type. No photography, no oil paint, no watercolour. The corpus's bathroom block is 598 digital and 221 physical listings, and the physical ones that work (Waterworks, Funny Donkey Toilet Print, Silly Goose) are illustration prints, not paintings.
  • Palette, fixed for the shop's life: background sage #A3B18A / dusty pink #E8C4B8 / mustard #E9C46A / deep navy #1D3557 / off-white #F1FAEE. One background per design, rotating through the five. Never two backgrounds in one print.
  • Type: one hand-lettered sans or a chunky retro serif. The line is the design. Nothing else on the page except at most one small illustration (a frog, a goose, a donkey, a plunger) placed off-centre and never behind the text.
  • Light: irrelevant in flat work; the mockup supplies it. Keep the artwork evenly lit with no gradients except one permitted flat drop shadow under the stripe.
  • Composition rules, absolute: text occupies the middle 60% of the canvas; 12% margin minimum on all sides; the stripe is 8% of the canvas width, left or right edge only, never both; the joke must be readable at 400px wide on a phone — if it is not, it is not the design.

Why the stripe. ApexVisualsStudio's case file records sage green in four of the top eight tags and its Take a Seat Sweet Cheeks stripe listing took 105 six-month units. It is the single cheapest visual signature with corpus evidence behind it. Stripes are also what makes a 3-pack look like a set in a flat grid — the packing function, not just the branding.

8. Production plan#

8a. How one design gets made#

No Kittl spend on this direction (per the brief). The production chain is text-and-shape work, which is the cheapest in the whole folder, and the honest expectation is that most of the value is in the joke selection and the layout, not the image.

  1. Joke selection (5 minutes). Pick from the Series pool. Write it as plain text first. If it does not read clearly as plain text, it is not the joke — this is the single largest failure mode in the corpus and TLPX's dead listings are full of it.
  2. Layout (15 minutes). A single reusable Canva or Figma template per series, one background colour variable, one stripe toggle, three text-size stops. Fixed margins: text box 60% of canvas, stripe 8% of width. This template is the reason 18 designs cost roughly the same as three.
  3. Illustration block, Series 3 only (20 minutes). Generative fill for the animal-in-the-toilet pose, flat vector style, no shading, one background colour keyed to the palette. Upscale 2x, flat-vectors it, strip the background, place it off-centre. Never behind the text.
  4. Ratios (5 minutes). Export 6 sizes: 8x10in, 11x14in, 16x20in, A4, A3, 5x7in, all at 300dpi JPG plus one PNG with transparency for the stripe-only variant. Ratio files, not ratio listings — this is one listing with variations, not six listings.
  5. Puzzle block, Series 4 (30 minutes). Generate the grid, check every answer is findable and unambiguous, print-test once. Puzzles are the only format where an error is a refund and a one-star review.
  6. Mockup composite (10 minutes). Drop the six ratio exports into the shop's four fixed mockup scenes (§8c). One mockup set for the whole shop, built once — Alfie's rule, and Direction 7's as well.
  7. Listing build (20 minutes). Title from §5a, tags from §5b, description from §5c. Check every tag against the 20-character limit before publishing.

Effort per listing: 55 minutes base for a text-only design, 75 minutes with an illustration, 90 minutes for a puzzle. The first 18 listings therefore cost roughly 21 hours of work including the four mockup scenes and the three templates. At a $9.59 median this is worth doing only if a meaningful share of the 18 earns — see the kill rule in §9.4.

9a. Calibration#

9d. The kill rule#

KILL at day 60 if any one of these holds: (a) fewer than 25 organic sales across the whole shop in 60 days — the downside scenario ends month one at 14 units, so 25 is the number that separates a working catalogue from a broken one; or (b) no listing has reached 100 lifetime units, when the corpus says the incumbent's best ever listing reached 154 in an 11-month shop and TLPX's bathroom block holds 32 listings above 100; or (c) 16 or more of 18 listings have sold zero — the corpus puts the dead-tail share for this pocket at 73.8% ex-TLPX and 78.1% in young winners, so 16/18 = 88.9% is materially worse than the lane; or (d) the nine-pack (#18) has zero sales and zero favourites after 45 days live, which means the bundle thesis is wrong and the shop has no margin tier; or (e) the Airbnb pack (#14) has taken zero impressions after 30 days, which means the 93-volume/1,411-competition pocket is unreachable and Series 2 should be abandoned rather than extended to 15 designs.

PAUSE, do not kill, if: revenue is positive but flat and the ad gate has not been met — that is a listing-count problem, not a thesis problem. The corpus is explicit that bathroom is 2.4% of the median shop's catalogue and 20.4% of the best one's; a shop that has published 18 bathroom listings has not yet tested a lane, it has tested 18 listings.

10. The case FOR, the case AGAINST, and what would kill it#

10a. The strong case FOR, with numbers#

  1. The head terms are genuinely tight, and that is rare. funny toilet sign runs 543 monthly volume against 10,140 competing listings (0.0536). toilet humor wall art — a fresh pull Direction 7 never took — runs 661 against 11,969, ratio 0.0552 and Everbee score 60. funny bathroom-wall-art runs 1,588 against 22,913 (0.0693). Compare funny wall art at 2,371 / 752,341 and quote wall art at 1,941 / 959,653. In a folder where the generic heads are 1 : 300 and 1 : 500, three terms at 1 : 18 and 1 : 15 are the best-priced inventory in the whole research project.

  2. One term in this direction is the best ratio found anywhere in two waves of keyword work. funny bathroom wall art print set: 363 volume / 3,301 competition = 0.1099. That is a real monthly search market for a set — the exact product shape the corpus says carries the margin (8.4% of listings, $9.03 median, priced above singles). It is also the term the whole catalogue is designed around, which makes the packs the organic entry point rather than an afterthought.

  3. The room-specific product has the best units-per-listing in the corpus — on the honest, TLPX-excluded basis it does not, but on the six-month young-winner basis it still leads. 526 young-winner bathroom listings (excluding the two personalised-pet artefacts) produced 1,906 six-month units: 3.62 per listing against 2.30 for the 25,891 non-bathroom young-winner listings. 10d. Verdict material — no verdict, the boss decides#

What the evidence supports. The bathroom-sign pocket is a real, tight, room-specific corner of the humour lane with the best keyword ratios in two waves of research (0.0536, 0.0552, 0.0693, and 0.1099 on a set term), a 1.6x velocity premium on the young-winner six-month measure (3.62 vs 2.30 units per listing), the lowest dead-tail share of any lane examined (64.8% at the incumbent), a 1.6x margin premium on packs, and a production cost of 55–90 minutes per design at an 80% net margin. Two of its three sharpest keywords point at a board — Airbnb host printables — that currently holds zero print listings across 20 page-one slots at a $26.99 physical median.

What the evidence refuses to support. That it is a business at a first-year scale. Strip the incumbent and the pocket's yield falls from 26.79 to 10.85 — below the corpus average. Strip the two personalised-pet artefacts and the young-winner pocket is $6.49 at 3.62 units per listing per six months. The proven operator ranks on zero page-one slots. The best thing any shop under a year old has done here is 154 lifetime units for $1,386 gross. Month one at the corpus-average pace is $312 net. And the shape the evidence actually supports — a lane inside a wider humour shop, at 2.4% to 20.4% of catalogue — is the one a niche store cannot be.

The trade in one line: this is a direction where the format is cheap and proven, the keyword ratios are the best in the project, and the lane has never once been run as a standalone business by a shop that qualified for a 56-shop study.

The question that decides it, and it is not a data question. Two honest paths exist and they are different businesses:

  • Path A — a bathroom niche store. 100+ designs, $400/month at scale on the corpus arithmetic, entering a $5.78 commodity board against 200-print catalogues, with no precedent anywhere in the 73 shops. High confidence it will not clear $1,000/month.
  • Path B — a humour shop that leads with the bathroom. A bathroom-specialised opening that proves the concept and then widens into the kitchen, laundry, mudroom and "house rules for a home rather than a rental" — which is where TLPX's 83.2% humour share and ApexVisualsStudio's 734 listings actually live. This is the configuration every operator in the corpus chose, it needs no precedent, and its ceiling is set by the humour lane's own numbers rather than the bathroom's.

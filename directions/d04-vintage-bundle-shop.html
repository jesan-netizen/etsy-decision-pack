4. The pure vintage bundle shop (Engine A: vintage gallery wall bundles, one aesthetic)

A shop that sells vintage / French-country / cottagecore oil-painting prints as digital downloads, in one aesthetic, sold bundle-first — the buyer is a woman in her late twenties to forties hanging a curated wall above a cream sofa, a…

Compiled 2 Oct 2026 · UK English · Research only — no listings created, no Kittl tokens spent. Sources: /home/ubuntu/etsy_phase2/*.csv (40,275 listing rows, 73 shops), /home/ubuntu/etsy_research/ Wave 1 outputs, fresh Everbee keyword pulls (2 Oct 2026, this agent).

Note on revenue. Every revenue figure is Est. Total Sales × Price (hidden) — a gross sticker-price proxy, before Etsy fees, before the standing sale discount, before COGS. Where a figure comes from a Wave 1 file I say so; where I recomputed it from the CSVs I say that, and I give both when they differ materially.

1. The direction in one paragraph#

A shop that sells vintage / French-country / cottagecore oil-painting prints as digital downloads, in one aesthetic, sold bundle-first — the buyer is a woman in her late twenties to forties hanging a curated wall above a cream sofa, a dresser or a stairwell, who wants nine to two hundred prints that already work together, delivered as a zip, priced at $14.99–$19.99 for a 100+ print collection and $39.99+ for the whole shop. The shape strategy is bundle-backward: the 100-print mega collection is designed first, five 24-print family bundles are cut from it, 3–9 print "salon sets" are cut from those, and the singles are extracted last as discovery bait at $4.99. The aesthetic spine is a single named identity — warm-neutral European landscape and still-life oil painting, muted ochre/sage/oxblood, no neon, no typography, no cartoon characters. The whole point of the direction is to be the cheapest credible curator of that one look, not the biggest catalogue on the internet.

The honest narrowing, and it matters: inside bundles only, "N+" is worth $946.18 vs $913.62 — a 1.04x lift, i.e. nothing. shops17_analysis.md §6a found the same thing (0.95x, median 1.91x within-shop, failing outright in 24 of 70 shops). "N+" is a marker for bundle-ness, not a second lever. Use it on every bundle title because it costs nothing and it is the winning vocabulary; do not treat it as a strategy, and never put a count on a single-subject or personalised listing.

3.2 What a zero-review shop is up against#

From live_recon.md, all six queries, 120/120 listing pages read live:

  • Median per-listing review count on page one is 1; 59/120 (49%) have zero reviews. The per-listing gate looks survivable.
  • Median shop review count behind a page-one listing is 327. 41/120 (34%) sit behind shops with 1,000+ reviews. You are not competing listing-to-listing; you are competing with an accumulated review moat.
  • Bestseller on 32/120, Star Seller on 50/120 — both cumulative-sales badges, unreachable at launch, and both are the buyer's reason to skip you.
  • 72/120 (60%) showing an active sale price at capture. Launching at list price puts you above the advertised number most shoppers see.
  • Five shops reporting 0 Sales and omitting the star line entirely hold page-one slots (ElaraStudioAI, FaithLuxeStudio, LivingVesselDesign, Violetandvalorshop, plus AtlasCurations at 0 sales / 0 reviews / 1 favourite on vintage wall art). New shops do get placed. Four of the five carry AI-styled shop names, which is itself a signal about how the lane is currently being entered.
  • Digital download is "a commodity race, and the race is won by catalogue size" — the same shops repeat across every query with titles like 150,000+ Printable Wall Art Bundle and ENTIRE SHOP SALE, 35,000 Mega Bundle.

5.4 Description approach#

Sharda Roy, quoted in alfie_deep.md §5: "99% of buyers… do not read the descriptions." The description is not a sales page; it is a risk-reassurance block that answers the three things a digital buyer gets wrong.

Structure, identical on all 20 listings, only the middle block changes:

  1. Line 1 — what this is and how many. "120 vintage oil-painting-style prints in five curated families. Instant download."
  2. The reassurance block (never move this, never cut it): - What you get: 120 JPG files, 300 DPI, 8×10 in, plus 12 pre-cropped 4×5 and 11×14 ratios so you do not have to crop. - How you get it: Etsy sends a download link the moment you check out. No waiting, no email, no shipping. - What you need: a printer, or a frame-TV photo frame app. All three size families included. - Copyright: personal and commercial use for your own walls and your own small reselling business. Reselling the files as-is is not permitted. (This is also the "commercial licence" upsell — LoveDictionary's licence listings run $298–$2,155 and NorthPrints 20 of them account for $1.09m of sticker revenue in the corpus.)
  3. The middle block — family-specific, 40 words. What is in this family, what room it suits, the three other families in the master.

  5. The cross-sell line — the ladder, per Alfie's §6: "Also available: the 120+ Master Collection, and the 5 family bundles." Plus the standard buy-2/buy-3 card.

One description template, 20 listings, three variable fields. That is the systemised listing text alfie_deep.md §4 asks for, and it is the only way a 20-listing launch is publishable in a week.

7. Concepts#

7.1 The aesthetic bible#

Name of the identity: "Warm Provençal" — European landscape and still-life oil painting, 1880–1930, muted and unpolished.

This is not a free choice. It is the intersection of what the corpus says pays and what the trend data says is rising.

Dimension Rule Why
Palette Six colours, no others: bone white #F2EDE3, warm sand #D9C9AE, sage #8A9A7B, olive shadow #4E5641, oxblood #6B3A34, faded sky #A9BEC4. Every print pulls at least three. 1stDibs' 2026 survey of 468 designers: chocolate brown is the leading colour at 33%, maximalism 39% and eclecticism 38% are the top two requested styles. Maximalism and warm neutral are not in conflict — they are two halves of the same buyer, and the print lane monetises the warm-neutral half because buyers arrive cheap
Medium Oil on canvas or panel, visible brushwork, never digital-vector clean, never photographic, never a filter. Foxing, canvas weave, a slight darkening at the edges Pinterest's thrifting psychology is training buyers to want "imperfect, found, aged" — a downloadable print cannot physically age, so it has to be painted to look aged. This is the specific design thesis that separates a $19.99 collection from a $2.46 mass bundle
Light Single-source, low-angle, warm, raking. Never flat, never studio. Shadows long and soft Raking light is what makes an oil painting read as an oil painting in a 400px thumbnail
Composition Rule-of-thirds or a single centred subject; horizon on the lower third; no faces larger than 15% of frame; generous negative space at the top so a frame crop never breaks Faces are the most over-generated subject in the category. Avoiding them is both a style position and a differentiation
Frame Thin, warm — oak, walnut, antiqued gold. Never white, never black, never thick Mockup-match rule (alfie_deep.md §5): the mockup household must match the art's vibe, and a warm-neutral print in a bright Scandinavian room reads as a mistake
Forbidden Any text, any lettering, any signature. Any neon. Any modern minimalism (single geometric shape on a flat ground). Any cartoon, any emoji, any character. Any licensed IP. Any zodiac, any affirmation, any quote stats_analysis.md §4.4: 76% of the top 1% listings in both corpora are singles, and the category's most-copied template is a flat-shape-on-flat-ground print. The forbidden list is the list of things everyone already makes
The one permitted family crossover Botanical herbarium plates sit inside the same palette and the same light. They are not a second aesthetic; they are the still-life family seen from above Botanicals are the corpus's #2 aesthetic by yield ($363.87/listing) and they are the single most requested subject in a warm-neutral wall — keeping them in-palette is breadth inside one style, not a second business

The 70/30 self-check (alfie_deep.md §4): a warm-neutral shop that suddenly posts a dragon "could look a little bit" off. Every one of the 20 launch listings passes a single test — would this print hang in the same room as the master collection's best-selling print?

9.2 Ads: no for the first 15 days, then a narrow yes#

The gating is documented, not invented (alfie_deep.md §1): shop open a few weeks, ≥2 organic sales (5 ideal), ≥25 listings non-negotiable, and only high buyer-intent signals. Advertise average performers and you are paying Etsy to prove your catalogue is average.

  • Days 1–14: no ads at all. Two listings are live; there is nothing to bid on.
  • Day 15 (if ≥2 organic sales) or Day 25 (the 25-listing floor, whichever first): $3/day, efficient-spending campaign, 9 listings maximum, never more than 3 pages. The corpus's own 60-day test: 60-day orders — bestsellers 110, normal 29, new 32; ROAS 2.06 on bestsellers, 2.15 on new, normal worst on every metric. So: bid on the bundles and the hero, never on a mid-tier single.
  • Never raise the budget until Etsy is spending the daily cap and ROAS is positive. If Etsy will not spend $3, the catalogue is not good enough — that is a signal, not a failure.
  • Kill floor: ROAS 1.1 (break-even at 1.0, profit at 1.1 for digital). Target 2.0. Escalate $1 → $3 → $10 only on a positive-ROAS day.
  • CTR target 1–3%. Below 0.8% at 500+ views is a broken listing, not a bidding problem. CPC above 40–50¢ means Etsy distrusts the product — the listing images are the problem.

What is explicitly NOT a kill condition: a silent first month. 88% of listings sell nothing at 0–3 months' tenure and the median has made zero units. A quiet month 1 is the base rate.

10. The strong case FOR, the honest case AGAINST, and the verdict material#

10.1 The strong case FOR — eight numbers#

  1. It is the largest revenue pool in the corpus by a wide margin. vintage_gallery is 52.5% of 73-shop revenue on 19.4% of listings, at 2.71x the corpus average yield ($484.84/listing vs $179.17). Only three lanes in the entire corpus beat average yield; this is the only one that is also 66 shops deep.

  2. The gallery-wall-set sub-lane is the single best cell in the business. 40.79% of all merged revenue from 3.30% of listings, at $2,253.02 per listing and 97.56 units per listing, with the corpus's lowest zero rate of any lane (37.44%) and 70.0% of its listings priced ≥$12 against 13.0% for the vintage/retro lane as a whole.

  3. The bundle ladder is the largest single effect in the data, and it replicates independently in both corpora. Merged: single $93.33 → set $540.65 → bundle $1,511.29 per listing, a 16.19x revenue multiple; units 2.50 → 6.05 → 7.05 → 19.83 in YW and 35.87 → 90.18 → 73.59 → 241.38 in Shops17, p < 0.001 both. Inside this direction specifically, vintage/gallery non-single listings are 11.8% of the lane and 60.0% of its revenue, at $1,134/listing against $101 for singles.

  4. The bundle advantage is present at launch, not only at maturity — and this is my own age-matched cut. Holding listing tenure fixed inside the vintage/gallery lane: at 0–3 months, a single earns $3.81/listing and a bundle $27.16 — a 7.1x gap. At 3–6 months, $17.16 vs $132.48 (7.7x). At 6–12 months, $36.63 vs $341.05 (9.3x). The shape is the discriminator inside the same age band, so the maturation confound does not explain the shape effect. This is the single most important FOR number in the document and it is not in any Wave 1 file.

  5. "N+" title wording is the highest-yield format in the corpus and almost nobody uses it. Merged: 299 listings, 49.25 units/listing, $1,000.25/listing, 40.1% zero rate — against 56.9% zero for uncounted bundles. In the vintage/gallery lane it is $932.91/listing at 40.4% zero against $216.55 at 69.9% — a 4.31x lift and a 29-point survival advantage, on a construction carried by 0.5% of the corpus.

  6. The best cell in the entire analysis sits in this direction, and it is reachable. $20+ bundles average $5,026 per listing on 316 listings; my independent cut finds $20+ vintage/gallery bundles at $2,814.62/listing across 416 listings at a 42.5% zero rate. $12–20 vintage/gallery bundles are $1,043.41/listing at 48.0% zero on 1,145 listings — a cell large enough to be a business rather than a fluke.

  7. The long-tail demand is real, uncontested, and it is already measured in bundles. Fresh Everbee pulls: vintage wall art whole shop bundle = 300 searches against 262 competing listings (vol/comp 1.145); vintage gallery wall art set mega bundle = 613 against 1,483; vintage wall art prints bundle french country farmhouse = 121 against 242; vintage art prints mega bundle eclectic gallery wall = 411 against 968. The generic head vintage wall art is 586 against 2,844,155 (vol/comp 0.0002). Competition falls by four orders of magnitude between the head and the bundle long tail, and the long tail is where this direction's product lives. Put the head in the tags, the tail in the title.

  8. The live page is the least saturated of the six recon queries and the format is already bundles. gallery wall set: 20% ad share (13 of 65) — the lowest of the six, 95% bundles, median $39.96, only 4/20 shops with 1,000+ reviews, 3/20 listings with zero reviews. vintage wall art runs eight clean organic slots before its first ad. And new shops are demonstrably placeable: five shops reporting 0 Sales and no star line hold page-one slots, one of them at 0 sales / 0 reviews / 1 favourite on vintage wall art itself. Etsy's own 2026 trend report names the product: "build curated and sometimes nostalgic gallery walls", +110% YoY on "wall art decor", +80% on "gallery prints".

  9. One named shop proves the model is reproducible rather than lucky. HarmonyPixels: 300 listings, 6.3% zero, $1,727.56 per vintage/gallery listing, 95.5% of that revenue from bundles, 30-month median listing age. LoveDictionary: 1,154 listings, $2,003.79/listing in the lane, 93.7% from bundles. Two shops, two different price points ($5.80 and $12.61 medians), the same doctrine, both inside the top three of the lane. The model is not one shop's luck.

  10. External demand signal is aligned and named. Etsy's own trend report, 1stDibs' 468-designer survey (maximalism 39%, eclecticism 38%, chocolate brown 33%), Pinterest's "dream thrift finds" +550% and "vintage fall aesthetic" +1,000%, and Pinterest's 96% unbranded search share all point the same way: buyers want aged, curated, nostalgic sets and the category is actively growing on wall art while Etsy's overall buyer count shrinks 3.4%.

10.2 The honest case AGAINST — eight numbers#

  1. In the young corpus — the only corpus that actually resembles a launch — vintage/retro is the worst large lane by yield. vintage/retro in the 56 young winners: 10,440 listings, $145,059, $13.89 per listing, 1.992 units/listing, 81.55% zero rate, median price $5.98. It is 40.6% of the young catalogue and delivers 25.5% of its revenue. My recomputation on the wide cut: 14,936 young vintage/retro listings at $25.73/listing, 2.85 units/listing — a slightly better number but the same shape. The sub-lane map is worse: vintage/retro :: holiday/christmas is 1,437 listings for $7,046, 0.53 units/listing, 91.58% zero at a median age of 2 months. Everything a new vintage shop looks like at month 4 is in that row.

  2. The merged vintage signal is 78.3% from the mature 17 shops, and that is a maturity confound, not a lane effect. gallery wall set is 78.3% s17. My own cut is blunter: 92.6% of all vintage/gallery revenue comes from the 17 mature shops (9,022 of 27,372 listings). Per listing, the mature lane earns $627.26 and the young lane $24.58 — a 25.5x gap on the same keywords at the same prices. stats_analysis.md §2.4 says it plainly: "Lane rankings in this pack are shop rankings in disguise." The direction's headline is a description of what a 46-to-73-month-old shop earns, not a forecast of what a 3-month-old shop earns.

  3. NorthPrints wins this lane on breadth and age with no doctrine at all. 1,685 vintage/gallery listings, $2,399,164 of lane revenue — the single largest pool in the direction — at $1,423.84 per listing with only 41.7% of it from bundles. Median price $5.66, 80% of the catalogue at that one price, 32.0% zero (the lowest of any shop in the corpus), top 10 listings only 13.9% of units, 73 months old, 482,528 lifetime sales, 15.8m lifetime views. It beats the doctrine shops. The most likely explanation for the lane's 2.71x is that the lane is where 73 months of catalogue breadth compounds — and that is the one asset a launch cannot bring. The anti-doctrine control earns more than the doctrine.

  4. The 10–50 item dead zone is a real hole and the corpus is unambiguous about where to step over it. 10–50 item bundles: 306 listings, $309.25/listing, 16.54 units/listing, 61.1% zero — worse than a 4–9 set at $591.61 and less than half a 51–200 bundle at $765.53. Wave 1 splits it further: 10–20 → $225.81, 21–50 → $149.70. There is no gradual ramp between a curated set and a mega bundle; a 20-print bundle is a worse product than either a 6-print set or a 150-print bundle.

  5. Cheap-bundle failure is structural, not a mistake shops keep making. Bundles under $8: 672 listings, $242.79/listing, 69.2% zero. Bundles at $8–12: 1,092 listings, $213.36/listing, 67.0% zero — the worst bundle cell in the corpus, worse than the sub-$8 cell. Bundles at $12–20: 1,816 listings, $945.68/listing, 49.2% zero. The $5–8 bundle cell is $18.53/listing on 146 listings in the young corpus. The format does not pay below $12, and the live page one is full of digital bundles at $2.46, $8.49, $8.90 and $14.25. A $19.99 collection is competing for the same click as a $2.46 one, on a page where 60% of listings are discounted.

  6. The live page one is a catalogue-size race, and the incumbents are 200 to 35,000 prints deep. The five digital slots on gallery wall set are HomefyStudio 70,000+ at $2.46, LumioraPrints 850+ at $8.49, HarmonyPixels 35,000 at $8.90, GalleryWallDecorArt 30,000 at $14.25, AndreDigitalStudio 20,000 at $17.61. Four of the five are Bestseller-badged with 27–225 listing reviews. The median shop behind a page-one listing carries 327 reviews; 34% carry 1,000+. And 120 designs is not 35,000 designs.

  7. The strongest single listing in the lane is a $2,063.81 commercial-licence listing, and removing it moves the yield. That one LoveDictionary listing (392 units × $2,063.81) is 13.2% of all vintage/gallery revenue in the merged corpus and it is a B2B bulk licence, not a consumer print sale. Strip it and lane yield falls from $223.23 to $193.68 and corpus share from 84.7% to 73.5%. The top 10 listings are 27.0% of lane revenue, the top 100 are 52.0%, and the top 20 come from four shops.

  8. The two most-cited references for this direction are cautionary tales. LumioraPrints — the Engine A template — has 237 vintage/gallery listings at $195.82/listing with 78.5% of those listings dead, and the vault records it as decaying: Aug growth stalled 781 → 648 units, both flagship bundles falling, the newest 21 listings at zero traction. PosterluxPrint — the 95.4% bundle-share launch shop — is 156 listings at $34.70/listing with 94.9% zero at 2 months old. VintageGlowPrints took 18 months and 809 listings to reach $120.86/listing on a 2.9% bundle share. PureVintagePrints is a vintage shop with 804 listings at a single $4.98 price point that closed at 9 months with 84.1% zero. The lane is full of shops that executed the bundle mechanic correctly and still did not survive.

  9. Two doctrinal contradictions sit on top of this plan. alfie_deep.md §8.1: "Bundle-first launch — CONTRADICTED, hard; the biggest divergence in the pack." Singles are the raw material; a bundle with no catalogue has nothing to bundle; "expect a silent period after a bundle-first launch." And §8.3: $22.05 is contradicted — every digital number Alfie quotes sits far below it. The plan in §4 already routes around both (24 singles inside the master, $19.99 top price), but the boss should know the direction's founding doctrine is contested by the source material.

  10. Catalogue dilution is a monotone threat to a bundle shop specifically. A bundle shop's revenue is concentrated in its top 10 (27.0% of lane revenue from 10 listings; 42.7% from 50). Meanwhile corpus-wide, median units/listing falls from 44.96 at ≤100 listings to 1.66 at 900–1,500 (Spearman ρ = −0.422). A bundle ladder wants a big catalogue to cut from; a big catalogue is exactly the thing that dilutes per-listing yield. The reconciliation is the 120-design / 200-listing architecture in §7.3 — but that is a designer's discipline, not a platform property, and nothing in the data enforces it.

10.4 Verdict material — for the boss, no verdict#

The shape of the argument, stated plainly. The evidence splits cleanly in two, and both halves are strong.

The FOR half is about the cell, not the shop. Inside the vintage/gallery lane, non-single listings earn $1,134/listing against $101 for singles and are 11.8% of the lane's listings and 60.0% of its revenue. The advantage holds inside matched age bands (7.1x at 0–3 months' tenure) and inside the merged corpus at 16.19x, with p < 0.001 independently in both corpora. The best cell in the entire analysis — $20+ bundles at $5,026/listing — sits in this direction. The best title format in the entire analysis — "N+" at 40.1% zero — is used by 0.5% of the corpus and is worth 4.31x here. And the fresh keyword pulls show the vintage bundle long tail is the least contested demand measured anywhere in this project: one search per 874 competing listings on the whole-shop bundle term, against one per 5,000 on the generic head.

The AGAINST half is about the confound and the floor. The lane's yield is 92.6% mature-shop revenue, and the one shop that owns the most of it (NorthPrints, $1,423.84/listing) got there with 41.7% bundle share, 1,792 listings and 73 months — i.e. without the doctrine. The young corpus, which is the only launch-shaped sample, puts vintage/retro at $13.89/listing and 81.55% zero, the worst of its large lanes. The live digital page one is a race won at 20,000–35,000 prints deep. The format dies below $12. And the direction's own reference shop, LumioraPrints, is decaying at 78.5% dead with its newest 21 listings at zero.

The three things that would settle it, none of which this corpus can answer (stats_analysis.md §8.3):

  1. How long until a shop matures. Shop age and listing tenure are perfectly collinear with corpus membership; the young arm has an empty >12-month cell. The direction's whole case turns on whether a vintage bundle shop at month 3 compounds the way NorthPrints did at month 40, and this data cannot say.
  2. Whether 120 designs can compete with 35,000. Nothing in these files models catalogue depth against rank on a bundle query — the page-one evidence is six one-day snapshots.
  3. Whether a $19.99 tier clears against a $2.46 page. The corpus says $12+ is where the format pays; the recon says the digital page one is priced at $2.46–$17.61. The two datasets disagree and only a live test resolves it.

What the numbers do support, without qualification:

  • ✅ Build bundles first, in the vintage/gallery aesthetic, and price them at $12–20 with the count and a plus in the title. Monotone, replicated, p < 0.001, and it holds inside matched age bands.
  • ✅ Never price a bundle under $12 and never build a 10–50 item bundle. Both are corpus-verified failure cells.
  • ✅ Never target vintage wall art or printable wall art in a title — 2.8m and 2.9m competing listings at a median of 0 units. Tag them; title the long tail.
  • ✅ Hold the catalogue at ~80 listings and 120 designs. Both dilution and concentration argue for it and the plan in §7.3 delivers it.
  • ✅ Expect a silent month 1, and set month 12 as a go/no-go, not an automatic continuation. All four dead shops closed at 8–12 months with listings at median age 3 months — they closed before the payback window opened.

What the numbers do not support, stated as clearly:

  • ❌ A 2.71x efficiency quoted as a forecast. It is 1.25x on a wider keyword boundary and it is 92.6% mature-shop revenue.
  • ❌ "NorthPrints proves the lane" — it proves that 73 months and 1,792 listings at one price point produce $1,423/listing in vintage, which is an argument about age, not about bundles.
  • ❌ Any revenue projection. Every figure in this document is a gross sticker proxy before fees, before the ~25% discount that 60% of the live page carries, and before COGS.

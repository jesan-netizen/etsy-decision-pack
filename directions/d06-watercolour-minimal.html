1. The direction in one paragraph#

The lane is soft-painted minimalism for the home: single-subject botanical and object studies drawn either as loose watercolour washes or as continuous single-line contour drawings, sold as digital downloads into a $8 single / $12 three-set / $16 six-set / $30+ mega ladder. The aesthetic is deliberately quiet — two to four pigments on cream paper, generous white margin, one subject, no text. The audience is the buyer with a named room (nursery, bedroom, hallway, bathroom) who has already decided they want calm and does not want to search for it; the shape strategy is bundle-led, designed backwards: design 24 subjects in both techniques (48 masters) knowing they will be published as 20 singles, eight three-sets, six six-sets, two theme packs of 30+, and one whole-shop bundle — every master appearing in six listings from the same art. The single decision this direction turns on is whether the shop sells "minimal line art" or "watercolour nursery sets" — the corpus says those are two different businesses with opposite economics (§2.5, §10).

Three things to read off this table before anything else.

(a) The Wave 1 aesthetic taxonomy barely caught this lane at all. watercolour / hand-painted fired on 24 listings and line art / single-subject minimal on 10, out of 40,275. This is exactly the trap the folder's own statistics warn about — "The high-lift pairs are not optimisation wins, they are single shops… Read lift and count together; either alone misleads." Do not build a plan on $246/listing watercolour.

(c) minimalist / neutral / beige is the lowest qualified yield in the whole corpus at $17.19/listing with an 82.8% dead tail, and Wave 1's own verdict (directions_from_data.md line 796) is: "It is a description, not a direction: it is the absence of a style choice." Line 69 adds the sharper version: "The lane's problem is not the shape, it is that nobody is buying 'minimalist' as a want… Fixing the aesthetic, not the bundle, is the only lever here."

3.2 What a zero-review shop is up against#

From the same capture: Bestseller badge on 3–7 of 66 cards, discount on 44–68% of cards, ad share 34–37%, and not one incumbent held more than 2 slots. Shop concentration on live page one is genuinely low — the top shop holds 2 of 66 cards. Two facts cut opposite ways:

  • For placement: lane_faith_vintage.md found five shops reporting 0 Sales and no star line holding page-one slots on the faith lane, including CherrylHardyArt at slot #1. Relevance plus a visible discount beats shop size. A new shop does get placed.
  • Against conversion: the same brief records 75% of opened page one discounted, Bestseller on 24% and Star Seller on 37% of opened slots, and 34–50% zero-review. A zero-review shop enters a page where a third of the slots are paid and two thirds of the organic slots are undercut.

And the AI-slop backdrop is specific to this lane. external_trends.md §2.4 documents Etsy active buyers at 86.5M, down 3.4% YoY, sellers down 1.5%, WIRED and Marketplace both reporting an exodus of human sellers over AI-generated listings, and sets out the strategic read: "A landscape where generic AI art has flooded the category is one where a specific, traceable, authored aesthetic becomes the differentiator rather than a liability." Minimal line art and loose watercolour are the two most generatable aesthetics on Etsy. They are simultaneously the cheapest to produce and the least defensible. That is the strategic core of the case against.

7. Concepts — the aesthetic bible, 24 subjects, and how to reach 100 designs without drift#

7.1 The aesthetic bible#

The lane's failure mode is drift into genericness, so the bible is written as constraints, not adjectives. Every rule below is checkable against the previous design.

Palette — a closed six-ink set. Nothing outside this list, ever:

Ink Hex Role
Paper #F5F0E6 the ground. Cream, never white. Never pure #FFFFFF
Sage #9CAF9B primary. The lane's signature, and the filter buyers use
Dusty blue #7C99AB secondary
Clay #C9A18B tertiary, warmth
Umber #6B5744 line weight. The only dark
Blush #E3C4BC accent, ≤5% of any composition

No black. No saturated red, yellow or blue. If a design needs a seventh colour, the concept is wrong.

Medium — two techniques, and they must not be mixed in one design.

  • Line art: a single continuous contour, 1.5–2.5 px at final size, umber on cream. No fill. No shading. No cross-hatching. One stroke weight, no variation along the line.
  • Watercolour: exactly three pigments per design (sage, dusty blue, one of clay/blush), one wash pass, visible paper texture, hard-ish edge where the wash ends, and one deliberate bleed or granulation per composition — the imperfection is the point, and it is what separates this from a generated fill.

Light. Flat, even, no cast shadows, no directional source. A botanical study lit from nowhere. Watercolour may carry one soft shadow inside the subject mass, never on the paper.

Composition rules. One subject per design. Subject occupies 40–55% of the frame height, centred horizontally, sitting on the lower third line. Margin ≥18% on every side — the margin is the product. Tall narrow (2:3) is the primary format, because the lane's measured winners are narrow strips (NorthPrints V-155, Flower Sketch 77, Flower Line Art Vase). Never a background, never a border, never a frame, never text on the art.

The four drift rules, stated as kill criteria for a single design:

  1. If it could be mistaken for a Midjourney default botanical, it is out.
  2. If the palette has more than three hues, it is out.
  3. If the subject is not a thing (a stem, a fruit, an animal, a tree), it is out — no abstract waves, no geometry, no brushstroke swatches.
  4. If it is not recognisable as the same shop's work with the name removed, it is out. This is external_trends.md §"own a reusable visual asset" applied literally: one botanical plate format, used across every listing, every pin, every mockup.

8. Production plan#

8.1 How the art gets made#

Per listing_runbook.md, art generation is Kittl (kittl-generate-media, kittl-list-image-generation-models, kittl-list-image-styles), with image_generate as the stated fallback if Kittl fails. No Kittl tokens are spent by this document — the plan below is the plan, not the execution.

The prompt architecture — this is the whole production system, and it is deliberately boring:

  • One base style string, written once, never varied. Example: botanical line art, single continuous contour line, uniform 2px umber stroke on warm cream paper, no fill, no shading, flat even light, generous margin, 2:3 portrait, one single stem subject centred, no background, no border, no text.
  • Subject token only. wildflower stem with six heads. Nothing else changes.
  • Same base string, technique swapped for the wash version: loose watercolour, three pigments only (sage green, dusty blue, warm clay), single wash pass, visible paper granulation, hard wash edge, one deliberate bleed, cream paper, 2:3 portrait, no background, no text.

alfie_deep.md's rule is "Keep style wording IDENTICAL across prompts, varying only subject" — the folder's own instruction from the runbook. That single rule is what prevents the drift this lane is most prone to.

Workflow per master: base prompt + subject → generate 4 variants → pick the one that passes the four drift rules → upscale to 300 DPI at print resolution (kittl-upscale-image) → export masters at 2:3, 3:4, 4:5, 5:7, 8:10 and square, which is exactly the six-ratio set already sitting in listing1/art/delivery/ from the previous exercise and is the set the description promises.

Effort per listing, honestly:

Item Time
One master subject (4 variants, pick, upscale, six ratios) 20–30 min
One listing pack (title, 13 tags, description, image list) 10 min — templated, alfie_deep.md: "Systemise listing text: fixed template, only design-specific keywords change; base tags preloaded so 3–4 slots change per listing"
Mockups (three images, reused PSD) 5 min once the PSD exists
Marginal cost of listing #17–#20 (packs/megas over existing masters) 15 min each, mostly listing text

The 20-minute rule from alfie_deep.md applies per master: "if a design isn't good enough in 20 minutes, the concept is wrong — ship it anyway." At 30 minutes per master, 48 masters is 16–24 hours of pure design work, and that is the entire production cost of a 100+ listing catalogue. This is the strongest structural argument for the direction: it is the cheapest production cost per listing of any direction on the list, because the product is a single-subject line drawing on a fixed template.

9.4 The kill rule#

Three gates, each tied to a measured lane benchmark, evaluated at fixed days so nothing is rationalised:

Action: confirm it is the shape, not the aesthetic — if the bundles are selling and the singles are not, the singles were the mistake and the shop is now a set shop. If neither shape has 3 sales at day 30, kill.

Gate 3 — day 30. The early-revenue check that respects the compounding curve. Units per listing below 1.5 (against the lane's own 2.48) with bundle revenue share below 25%. This is the one that fires on a shop that is doing the right thing too slowly. Action: do not kill — hold to day 60 with $3/day ads, and take the day-60 reading as the verdict. Anyone reading only Wave 1 or only the lifetime-shop tables will pick this direction for the wrong reason. Half the direction's name is measurably below the corpus average.

2. "Minimal line art is a persistent Etsy staple" is true of the SERP and false of the sales. The line art wall art SERP has line in 53 of 66 cards. It also has canvas in 17, framed in 11, and a $52.24 median with only 4.5% digital supply. The word is a staple; the download shape behind it is not what is selling. This is the same structural finding lane_faith_vintage.md reached on the faith lane — imagery queries are physical, download queries are digital — and it means the apparent demand for "minimal line art" on page one is largely demand for framed product this shop cannot ship.

3. Every head keyword is a volume trap, and the good ratios are artefacts. watercolor wall art 1,980 volume / 1,394,157 competing (0.0014) · minimalist wall art 3,147 / 1,323,796 (0.0024) · line art 720 / 396,154 (0.0018). The only real ratios are botanical line art (590 / 27,102) and nursery line art (393 / 27,454) — under 600 searches a month each. The genuinely attractive ratios (artful line art at 2.39, artful watercolor wall art at 0.769) come from a family everbee_keywords.md already flags as "Everbee score artefact, not usable demand signals." The keyword layer of this direction is, in plain terms, empty of exploitable demand.

4. This is the most generatable aesthetic on Etsy, and AI slop is documented as the category's problem. external_trends.md §2.4: Etsy active buyers 86.5M, down 3.4% YoY; sellers down 1.5%; WIRED and Marketplace both reporting human-seller flight over AI listings; the strategic read that "generic AI art has flooded the category." A loose watercolour flower on a cream background is the single most reproducible image in the entire category. The lane's $8.00 singles are competing on a criterion (aesthetic familiarity) that is worth nothing, against a supply curve that is effectively infinite. The doctrine's answer — "a specific, traceable, authored aesthetic becomes the differentiator" — is correct in principle and requires the shop to be visibly better than a generation, every listing, at $8.

5. Concentration risk is extreme and irreducible at launch. NorthPrints holds 40.3% of lane revenue; top 3 hold 64%. Strip the top 5 shops and the lane falls to $26.70/listing, 2.77 units/listing, 14.9% sold. The 17-shop master cohort holds $152,062 of the lane's $190,075 — 80% on a lifetime basis, against $38,013 from the 56 young winners on a 6-month basis. A new shop enters against a five-year-old 1,792-listing catalogue running one price point at $816/listing.

6. The $5–8 single that carries the lane is a trap for a new shop, and the $20–40 band takes 15 months to work. Lane $5–8: 728 listings, 25.5% sold, $121.03/listing — but that yield is NorthPrints' flat $5.66 point. Lane $40+: 200 listings, 8.5% sold, median age 2 months — new shops pricing themselves out of a digital market. The $20–40 money band sells at a 15-month median age. So the ladder says: enter cheap to get traffic, and the revenue arrives after 15 months. That is a two-year cash profile.

7. Frame-TV is a dead cell inside this lane, and the "under-saturated" claim does not survive contact with it. alfie_deep.md reports the folder's belief that "frame TV and printable wall arts are still growing and not saturated." In this lane's own 216 frame-TV listings: $8.27/listing and 7.4% sold. The belief does not hold for minimal watercolour on a Samsung frame. Whatever is unsaturated about frame-TV is not this aesthetic.

10.4 Verdict material (no verdict — the boss decides)#

The one-line reading of the evidence: Watercolour in a named room at a $16 six-set is a real, repeatable, measured opportunity at 2.30x the yardstick. Minimal line art as a standalone lane is a measured failure at 0.34x. The direction's name is half right.

What the numbers support:

  • A 6-month-basis lane yield of $21.72 against a $21.56 yardstick is 1.01x — exactly average. Nothing about this lane is exceptional at the lane level. Any case built on "watercolour is a proven lane" is overstated by an order of magnitude; the case lives entirely in the sub-cells.
  • The sub-cell that works is watercolour × nursery at 2.30x, and it works through bundles ($154.63 vs $12.84 per listing, within-shop).
  • The sub-cell the direction is named after, line art, is 0.34x and stays below the yardstick at every catalogue size.
  • The bundle mechanic works in this lane (3.44x matched) even though Wave 1 recorded it as flat for the minimalist label. The wave-1 line is the single most useful correction in this document.
  • Entry-point choice decides feasibility. The imagery queries are physical product at $52–$93 with 3–8% digital supply; the download queries are $7–$10.50 with 77–88% digital. A shop that targets the wrong page is invisible, not merely uncompetitive.
  • Production cost is the strongest point in the direction's favour and the only one that is unambiguously better than the alternatives: 1 master → 6 listings, 48 masters → 100+ listings in 16–24 hours.
  • The keyword layer is empty of exploitable demand. Every head term is a trap; the two real ratios total under 1,000 searches a month; the good-looking ratios are a documented Everbee artefact.

Three ways to read the same evidence, for the reconciliation agent:

  1. The lane-average reading. 1.01x, 62 shops, no edge. Direction 6 is the corpus average with a good production cost. That makes it a strong execution candidate and a weak selection candidate.
  2. The sub-cell reading. The lane is only interesting where it intersects a named room. Direction 6 is not a lane at all; it is a technique to be applied to whichever lane wins Wave 3. That is arguably its most valuable form: watercolour × nursery at 2.30x is a modifier on Direction 3, not a competitor to it.
  3. The counter-reading. $49.67 for watercolour × nursery rests on 281 listings across 28 shops with an 82.6% dead tail, and its top two shops hold 69% of the cell revenue. Strip them and it is $17.59, i.e. 0.82x — below the yardstick. The 2.30x is a two-shop number wearing a 28-shop coat.

What would change the reading, and it is cheap to find out. A 6-pack of line-art and watercolour assets, mocked in three households, published as one six-set at $16 and one three-set at $12, with $3/day of ads on botanical line art and nursery line art only. That is roughly four hours of design and $90 of spend. It tests Gate 1 and Gate 2 simultaneously, and it answers the question this document cannot: whether the 3.44x bundle premium and the 50.7% sold rate of the best cell reproduce in a catalogue with zero reviews. No other direction on the list can be tested that cheaply.

Material not used, and why. The folder's IP-led exemplars in this lane's best cell (MartyWallArt's Avengers set at 60 units and Disney set at 45; PixelscapeArtStudio's Frozen set at 40) are excluded from every projection. Wave 1's own rule: "Any direction whose examples are IP-led should be rejected as a project, however good the numbers look." Every FOR number in §10.1 is therefore computed from non-IP listings only, which makes them lower than a naive read of the same cells would be. That is deliberate.

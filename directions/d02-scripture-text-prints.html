2. Scripture text prints (ThePsalmistCottage lane)

Lane: typographic scripture — the verse is the artwork. No figure, no scene, no oil painting; the composition is type, texture and negative space. Aesthetic: vintage cottagecore.

Compiled: 2 Oct 2026 · Corpus: 73 shops / 40,275 listing rows (56 young winners + 17 masters) · Raw data: /home/ubuntu/etsy_phase2/*.csv Analysis scripts (reproducible): dir02_analyse.py, dir02_pass2.py, dir02_pass3.py → outputs in this directory. Method note up front: the CSVs carry two different measurement windows and they must never be mixed. yw_listings.csv has Est. Sales Last 6 Mo; shops17_listings.csv does not. So: - Basis A = 6-month window, 56 shops, 26,429 rows. Used for all yield, price, texture and launch maths. - Basis B = lifetime, 73 shops, 40,275 rows. Used only for catalogue age, longevity and shop-level context. Every figure below names its basis.

Lane classifier (stated so it can be re-run and disagreed with): a row is in-lane if its title+tags contain a scripture anchor (bible verse / scripture / psalm / proverbs / romans / corinthians / philippians / joshua / doxology / etc.) AND a wall-art format token (wall art / print / printable / poster / artwork / frames / gallery) AND NOT a figurative-painting token (painting / canvas / portrait / renaissance / acrylic) AND NOT an svg-apparel token (svg / png / shirt design / sublimation / clipart / cricut / dtf). The svg exclusion matters: leaving it in inflates the lane by ~500 units because DigiLabsy and DigitalStudioArtUA both carry four-figure SVG catalogues with bible verse in the tags.

1. The direction in one paragraph#

Lane: typographic scripture — the verse is the artwork. No figure, no scene, no oil painting; the composition is type, texture and negative space. Aesthetic: vintage cottagecore. Gingham, checked and striped cloth grounds; aged-paper cream; muted sage / mustard / terracotta / dusty blue; small-caps or a quiet serif; a hand-set rule or a thin border. One identity, one palette, no drift. Audience: the Christian woman decorating a farmhouse kitchen, dining room or laundry room, plus the buyer who has already bought one gingham verse print and wants the matching pair. Secondary: the Christian nursery (Galatians 5:22 fruit-of-the-Spirit sets are the single biggest listing in the lane), and the church-gift / housewarming buyer. Shape strategy — bundle-backward, and this is the whole thesis. The lane's proven money shape is not the single print. In-lane set-shaped listings are 4.8% of the catalogue but 17.4% of 6-month units, and earn $126.47 per listing against $31.99 for singles (Basis A). DigiLabsy's "Fruit of the Spirit Wall Art Set of 10 … Galatians 5:22" is 345 units in 6 months at $9.49 — 13.5% of the entire lane's unit volume from one listing. The plan therefore builds singles first as raw material, then assembles them into named sets, then into a mega-bundle, following the doctrine in vault_synthesis.md §4 ("low-ticket packs → mega bundle → whole-shop bundle"). The design constraint: every verse printed must be texture-compatible with the other verses printed, because the set has to hang as a wall.

2.2 Reconciling the boss's 0.33–0.36× "faith underperforms" warning#

The corpus warning is real but it is measured on a basis that flatters five shops. On Basis B (lifetime, all 73):

NorthPrints alone holds 353,679 of the corpus's 662,217 lifetime units (53%) at a $5.70 median. Five shops hold 74.4%. Strip them and the lane flips from 0.32× to 1.09×. So the honest statement is two-part and both halves must travel together:

On a listing-weighted mean the faith/scripture lane underperforms the corpus by roughly 3×, and that is entirely a denominator artefact of five cheap mega-catalogue shops. On any per-shop basis the lane returns 0.57–0.61× of the median shop — still a genuine, real underperformance, just a much smaller one.

The median-shop figure is the one to plan against, and it is the honest case AGAINST in a single number: this lane returns about 40% less per listing than the median shop in the corpus. It is not a volume trap (2,554 units across 28 shops is real demand) and it is not a $0 lane. It is a mid-earning lane, not a top-earning one.

2.7 Concentration and dead tail#

  • 73.8% of in-lane listings sold zero in 6 months (corpus: 82.5%). The lane is 8.7 points better than the corpus and still three-quarters dead.
  • Lane sold-rate by listing age shows the usual ramp, with no decay evidence in the window: 0–3 mo 0.84 u/lst (92.2% zero), 3–6 mo 2.57 (55.1% zero), 6–9 mo 4.52 (63.4% zero), 9–12 mo 10.77 (82.4% zero). Basis A cannot measure past month 12 — the young-winner file has no listing older than 12 months. The lifetime file fills the gap: in-lane listings at 12–16 months old are still the top lifetime earners (LoveDictionary "550+ Christian Wall Art Bundle" 536 units at 14 months; ThePsalmistCottage's 168-unit gingham hero is 12 months old). The lane is evergreen on a 12+ month clock; nothing in the corpus suggests a decay wall, and that is a real finding given the faith lane's reputation for Q4-only behaviour.

3.2 What a zero-review shop is up against#

  • 46 of 112 opened page-one listings (41%) have zero reviews, and the digital zero-review median buy-box is $12.00 across 13 slots. The review gate is survivable.
  • Four shops holding digital page-one slots report 0 Sales and no star line: ElaraStudioAI, FaithLuxeStudio, LivingVesselDesign, Violetandvalorshop. CherrylHardyArt holds slot #1 on figurative faith wall art with 0 sales and 0 reviews. A new shop does get placed on exact-relevance listings.
  • But 75% of all 112 opened listings are showing an active discount (median 32% off, list median $35.99). Launching at list price advertises above nearly every competitor.
  • Badges are taken: Bestseller on 24%, Star Seller on 37%. Both unreachable at launch. In 20+ carts on 38% overall but only 3 of 46 zero-review slots — the cart-pressure badge is thin exactly where a new shop would sit.
  • Median shop on the digital pages: 1,908 sales / 202 reviews. The top holder, ProjectMadeNew, has 38,918 sales / 4,096 reviews.
  • The closest live comparable is BibleSketches — 96,807 shop sales, 3 slots, selling individual named compositions at $9.94 ("Jesus Feeds the 5000, DIGITAL PRINT"). VisionofWallart shows the same play small: 26 shop reviews, 2 slots, $4.49.
  • The bundle incumbents to beat: ByMorningMercies "Set of 50" at $29.98 (25% off), ScriptfulArt "Set of 9, Vintage Cottagecore Bible Verse" at $26.99 (25% off), ThatHipPrint "bundle of 250" at $13.16, VintageDiscoPrints "600+" at $12.82. Median stated bundle size on these pages is 10; the common shape is a 9-to-12 print gallery set at a $10.50 median.

4. First products — the exact first 18#

Design rule: every design is produced at the same five ratios so any set can be assembled from any combination. Alfie's constraint from Skool (Jason Famularo): digital wall-art sizes are dictated by source-file pixel dimensions, so fix the ratios on day one — 2:3 (8×12), 3:4 (9×12), 1:1 (8×8), 5:7 (10×14), 11×14 (16×20) — at 300 DPI.

Bundle-backward: the singles exist to feed the sets. Products 1–10 are designed so that any three of them make a coherent trio and all ten make the flagship. Products 11–14 are the named sets. Products 15–18 are the entry singles and the mega-bundle.

7. Concepts#

7.1 The aesthetic bible#

Identity: "Gingham Hymnal." Vintage cottagecore scripture, type on cloth. One rule: the verse is the only imagery.

Palette (fixed, no additions): - Grounds: aged cream #F4EFE4, oatmeal #EDE4D3, warm white #FAF7F0 - Gingham: sage #9CAF88, mill green #7D8F6B, dusty blue #8FA9BC, terracotta #C4795B, blush #E4C4BC, mustard #D4A54A - Ink: charcoal #2E2A26, deep moss #3A4238 - Never: pure black, pure white, saturated primary, more than three inks per design.

Type: one serif per design (editorial, slightly old-style — never a script font, never a sans as the primary); small-caps for the reference; generous leading; the reference always in the smallest type on the sheet, bottom or under a hairline rule.

Medium / texture: gingham (two-colour woven check at 0.25 in and 0.5 in pitch), fine stripe, aged-paper speckle at 6% opacity. All generated procedurally, not clip art.

Light: none. Flat, even, no shadows, no vignette, no faux frame. This is a printed surface, not a photograph.

Composition rules (these are what prevent style drift across 100+ designs): 1. Fixed type block. Verse centred, reference bottom-right. Never re-centre. The grid is identical in all five ratios — only the surrounding ground changes. 2. One texture per design. Gingham or stripe or plain. Never two. 3. One palette per design, three inks maximum, drawn only from the seven above. 4. Maximum 14 words on the sheet. If the verse is longer, set the whole verse but drop it to two lines max at 8×12; if it still will not fit, do not make it. 5. No people, no animals, no botanical illustration inside the type block. Floral and fruit motifs may appear only as a single small element at one edge, outside the type block, in one ink. 6. Verse must be verbatim (ESV/NIV/KJV — pick one and state it in the listing). No paraphrase, no invented text. This is a hard rule and it is the reason the catalogue is defensible. 7. Every design is generated at all five ratios from one master composition, so any set assembles cleanly.

8. Production plan#

No Kittl tokens used, per the brief. No listings created. Production is a deterministic composition pipeline, not per-listing illustration — that is what makes 140 listings affordable.

Pipeline per design (est. 22–35 min of the operator's time, not a tool's):

  1. Master composition (SVG). One SVG, 8×12 in at 300 DPI = 2,400×3,600 px, viewBox in inches so scaling is exact. Gingham is generated as a <pattern> of two-colour rects; aged-paper speckle as a seeded SVG filter (feTurbulence, fixed seed so the render is reproducible).
  2. Verse text. Verbatim from a single translation file (ESV, licensed reference in the listing text). Set in one licensed old-style serif. No AI-generated lettering, ever — generated letterforms are the fastest route to a takedown on a verse print.
  3. Ratio derivation. Change only the SVG viewBox to 2:3 / 3:4 / 1:1 / 5:7 / 11:14 and re-anchor the type block at the rule-defined position. Five PNG renders from one master.
  4. Rasterise. 300 DPI PNG, sRGB, plus a 150 DPI JPEG preview set for mockups.
  5. Package. One ZIP per design containing all five ratios, a printed-size guide PDF, and a listing-description text file.
  6. Bundle assembly. Sets and the mega-bundle are ZIPS assembled from existing design packages — zero new art. This is the bundle-backward payoff: the mega-bundle costs packaging time, not design time.

What AI is and is not used for. AI (image generation, not text) is permitted for texture ground generation only — paper grain, cloth weave, aged-edge masks. It is not used for figures, faces, lettering or composition. That is a deliberate doctrine fit: vault_synthesis.md §5 item 9 shows IP risk sits where the money is in this corpus (27.6% of young-seller rows IP-flagged), and a typographic verse print has essentially no IP surface if the type and the texture are both properly sourced.

Prompt skeleton for the texture grounds (for the operator's own record):

flat seamless [colour-pair] gingham check pattern, [pitch] inch weave, matte cotton texture, aged vintage fabric, even flat lighting, no shadows, no highlights, no folds, top-down scan, desaturated [palette] palette, 300 dpi, no text — seed locked per colourway.

Effort per listing: ~6 min for a single (3 min derive + 3 min package/mockup), ~4 min for a set (assembly only), ~15 min for the 60-print mega-bundle. 18 listings ≈ 3.5 hours of operator time for the launch set.

Mockup set (per Alfie's doctrine — mockups are the new-shop weapon, cap 3–5 storewide): - One framed gallery-wall mockup with three prints hung together (shows the set working — this is the whole pitch). - One kitchen/dining-room in-situ mockup at 8×12. - One close crop showing gingham weave and paper texture at full resolution (proves the texture is real, not a flat PNG tint). - Two thumbnail variants on the hero only, A/B'd monthly by ad CTR. - Buying a PSD mockup once (~$4, per alfie_deep.md) and reusing forever beats hand-making.

Soft kill — stop scaling, keep the shop, do not invest further: month-3 revenue under $400/mo, or a top-10 concentration above 65% with no second hero emerging.

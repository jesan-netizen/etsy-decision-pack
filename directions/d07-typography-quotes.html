A quote print is a piece of text, set in type, and sold as wall art. The lane splits three ways, and the split is not cosmetic — it is the whole economics: - Funny/humour is the volume end: swears, toilet humour, kitchen and bathroom…

Wave 2 research project · compiled 3 Oct 2026 · UK English Corpus: 73 shops (56 Young Winners + 17 starred masters), 40,275 listings, /home/ubuntu/etsy_phase2/*.csv Wave 1 files read: directions_from_data.md, yw_analysis.md, live_recon.md, everbee_keywords.md, alfie_deep.md, case_files/*.md Live pulls made for this doc: 12 Etsy SERPs via CDP (raw/d7_*.json, raw/d7b_*.json), 13 Everbee get_keyword seed pulls + 9 suggestion pulls (dir7_keywords_raw.json, dir7_keyword_suggestions.json), 24 page-one shop pages (raw/d7_shop_stats_top.json)

1. The direction in one paragraph#

A quote print is a piece of text, set in type, and sold as wall art. The lane splits three ways, and the split is not cosmetic — it is the whole economics:

  • Funny/humour is the volume end: swears, toilet humour, kitchen and bathroom signs, pop-culture-adjacent one-liners. Cheapest to write, easiest to understand, and the highest raw yield in the corpus — but it is where the crowding is and where the incumbents' moats sit deepest.
  • Motivational/affirmation is the middle: growth, confidence, therapy-office, gym, "you've got this". Higher willingness to pay (page-one median $23.50), less vulgar, but the weakest fresh-shop performance in the corpus.
  • Family/house-rules is the gift end: "this is our home", family mottos, entryway. The best page-one median of the eight heads ($24.72) and the most giftable, but the smallest pool and the most personalised.

The shape strategy must be bundle-backward, not single-print. Within the typographic lane specifically the bundle mechanic is weak — $69.75 per bundle listing against $88.38 per single — which is the opposite of the botanical and vintage bundle figures elsewhere in the corpus (quoted at 53.5x and 11.7x on the mixed/lifetime basis; botanical's like-for-like figure is 3.10x on the 6-month basis, and the corpus-wide merged multiple was corrected from 16.19x to 9.32x on 2 Oct 2026 — the comparison direction is unchanged, but the magnitudes were never like-for-like), and 1.3x in the Wave 1 lane table. That is the lane's honest structural fact: quote prints do not get a bundle premium. They get a format premium. The one cell that does work is the whole-shop mega bundle, where three listings in the tight lane read $22,986 per listing. The practical shape is: singles as the searchable catalogue → themed packs → one mega bundle → one whole-shop bundle, with the ladder doing the work rather than a bundle discount on any given print.

3b. The structural finding: these heads are not a download market#

1 of 160 page-one listings (0.6%) is a digital download. The other 159 are shipped physical — canvas, giclée, framed, matte poster, tin signs, engraved wood, brass plaques. Median physical price $20.99 against a $6.99 median for the single download.

I re-ran four heads with an explicit download intent appended, and the picture inverts completely:

Query Ads Digital in first 20 organic Median IQR
funny quote wall art printable 37% 17/20 $9.04 4.35–11.70
funny bathroom wall art digital download 36% 20/20 $6.95 4.74–14.24
quote wall art digital download 35% 20/20 $7.00 6.20–10.47
funny wall art digital download printable 28% 20/20 $6.88 4.55–9.99

Two different markets sit behind these keywords. The bare head term is a physical market at $20.84 median where a downloadable file has no purchase. Add "printable" or "digital download" and you get a $6.88–$9.04 download market (three of the four pulls land at $6.88–$7.00; only funny quote wall art printable is higher at $9.04) in which the incumbents are already running a catalogue-size arms race — 500+ Funny Printable Posters, 100 Trendy Bathroom Prints, 200 Funny Bathroom Animal Prints, Motivational Quotes Wall Art Bundle: 200 Printable Inspirational Posters. Cross-referencing live_recon.md's printable wall art recon (17/20 digital, median $5.88) with these four: five independent live pulls put this lane's download market at a $5.88–$9.04 median, clustering at $6.88–$7.00. That is below the $8.00 lane median in the corpus and roughly a third of the $20.84 the physical heads command.

3e. Alfie's doctrine, checked against this lane#

From alfie_deep.md:

  • "Wall art / frame TV is still growing and not saturated." Direct support, and the 138-distinct-shops/160-slots fragmentation above is the live version of it. This is the one claim in the pack that speaks to Direction 07 and it is positive.
  • "Catalogue volume is the gate" (Sharda: ~550 listings, results from the top 50). Directly contradicted for this lane by §2e: CreaPawsCo earned $36,292 from 14 listings, ApexVisualsStudio $10,506 from 546. In the quote lane the volume doctrine is wrong by a factor of 40 on listings and 3.5x the wrong way on revenue.
  • Price ladder: singles ~$2, $3–4 named as "the slow trap", mid bundles $10–12, mega $15–20, whole shop $40+. The live download medians ($5.88–$7.00) and the corpus live-listing median ($5.98) put this lane's honest single at $6–8, well above Alfie's $2 fix and inside his $3–4 trap. The corpus's live listings skew cheap; his doctrine says cheap. Both point the same way: do not price this lane at $9.99 for a single print and expect velocity.
  • September is the work month, not the selling month. Digital lead time 3–4 months. Directly load-bearing for the launch simulation below.
  • "No text" caution applies to listing images, not to the artwork. A text-led lane must be extra careful here: the thumbnail is 80% of the effort, one idea per card, no spec text. A quote print's thumbnail is its artwork, so the risk is a typographic clutter thumbnail, not a spec-text thumbnail.
  • The boss's stated worry that "AI-written SEO and quote prints are crowded" is half right and half wrong, and the data separates them cleanly: crowded is true at the generic head (funny wall art, 752,341 competing listings; quote wall art, 959,653) and false at the specific head (funny bathroom wall art, 96,172; funny toilet sign, 10,140; airbnb house rules sign, 4,312). The lane is winnable by specificity and unwinnable by genericity.

  1. Take A Seat Funny Bathroom Wall Art, Toilet Quote Print, Minimalist Typography, Bold Colourful Poster, Digital Download

Rules from the read: the quote itself goes first because that is the searchable string; the room keyword goes in the first 140 characters because funny bathroom wall art (1,541 vol) is the head and Etsy's index weights the front of the title; 4–5 pipe-separated segments is the modal length; end on Digital Download because it is a qualifier buyers filter on. Never use a dash as punctuation (house style).

5c. Description approach#

Alfie's rule governs: "99% of buyers do not read the descriptions… they rely on the listing photos." So the description is functional, short, and UK English:

  1. One line: what it is. "A hand-lettered A3 typography print, digital download."
  2. What you get — explicit count, listed sizes, file formats.
  3. How you get it — Etsy automatic download, no email, no shipping. (Alfie: spell out automatic delivery, buyers are confused.)
  4. Where to hang it — one sentence of styling.
  5. Print note — recommend a home printer or a print service at 8x10 / A3.
  6. Declaration — AI-assisted generation declared, as the listing-pack spec requires.
  7. No walls of text, no dashes as punctuation, no emoji.

7b. Medium, light, composition rules#

  • Medium: hand-lettered / marker-and-ink feel, imperfect baseline, slight ink texture. Not a clean sans-serif — the imperfection is the signal that a person wrote it, and it is the exact opposite of the AI-slop look this lane is criticised for.
  • Light: flat. No gradients, no shadows in the artwork itself, no 3D. The mockup carries the lighting, the artwork does not.
  • Composition: quote centred or lower-third, occupying 40–60% of the canvas. Wide margin, minimum 12% of the short edge on all four sides. Never bleed text to an edge.
  • Type: one family per print. Two weights maximum. No script fonts on small sizes (Alfie's hard cap: no tiny or script fonts — they vanish on a phone).
  • Non-negotiable: the quote must be readable at thumbnail size. This is the lane's one real quality gate and the whole reason AI-generated quote prints fail.

7d. Scaling to 100+ designs without style drift#

The lane's own trap: ApexVisualsStudio's yw top tags include modern minimalistic alongside sage green — it drifted. The anti-drift system:

  1. One style sentence, frozen. hand-lettered marker-and-ink typography print, flat matte background, single accent colour, wide margin, no gradient. Written into every prompt verbatim. Only the subject clause varies.
  2. One background template per palette, reused. Four background files total. A new design is a new quote on an existing background, not a new design.
  3. A 10% drift audit at 50 and 100 designs. Re-run the tag counter; if any palette falls below 15% of units or above 45%, it has drifted.
  4. Lock the four ground colours in the brand kit and refuse a fifth.
  5. One theme per 6-design series, always. The moment a series mixes bathroom and affirmations, the shop stops being findable.

8. Production plan#

Route: AI-assisted, Kittl-style vector lettering, five ratio files. NO Kittl credits without the boss's explicit permission; no listings created by this process.

  1. Quote bank first. 28 concepts written and legally cleared before any image generation. No named living artists, no lyrics, no film or TV quote text, no brand names, no sports teams. swear words and pop-culture-adjacent phrasing that names a title are excluded. Lyrics and TV quotes are the corpus's clearest IP trap — Thelittleprintshopx's Teenage Dirtbag Lyrics Print (820 units) and Funny TV Quotes Wall Art | Friends Movie Quotes (242 units) are exactly the listings to imitate structurally and never textually.
  2. Background generation. Four background templates, one per palette. Generated once at high resolution, reused for every design.
  3. Lettering pass. Typeset the quote into the template. Vector text, never AI-generated glyphs — AI text is the single most common defect in this lane and the reason "AI quote prints" are criticised. The art is AI-assisted; the letterforms are set.
  4. Quality gate, vision-checked. Every design checked with vision_analyze for: (a) all words legible at thumbnail, (b) no mangled or duplicated glyphs, (c) margin ≥12%, (d) no gradient. A design that fails legibility at thumbnail is regenerated, not shipped. This gate is the entire moat of the direction.
  5. Five ratio files per design: 2:3, 3:4, 4:5, 5:7, ISO A (1:1.414). Crop centred from the master; never upscale. Cap long edge 4000px, plain PNG.
  6. Effort per listing: quote selected and cleared 10 min · background/template assembled 5 min · lettering typeset 20 min · five ratio exports 5 min · vision QA 5 min · three mockup composites + five PIL listing slides 25 min · listing pack 15 min. ≈1 hour 30 min per listing. 18 listings ≈ 27 hours of production.
  7. Mockup set — 4 scenes, built once, reused across all listings: (i) framed on a wall above a toilet, mid-distance; (ii) framed in a small powder room, close; (iii) kitchen, styled, warm light; (iv) laundry/utility, straight-on. Alfie: 3–5 thumbnail mockups for the whole store, not one per listing.
  8. Listing images per listing (5): 1 thumbnail (the print, framed, in the room — 80% of the effort here), 2 lifestyle close, 3 the count/contents grid, 4 sizes and file-format infographic, 5 download-and-print instructions. 4:3, 2000px long edge, JPEG q90.
  9. Delivery: one Drive folder per product set, 0000N-<product-slug>/ with art/ and listing-images/ and listing-pack-N.md.

9. Launch simulation — first 30 days#

Anchored on Alfie's hard rule: September is the work month, not the selling month; digital lead time is 3–4 months. A 30-day window in this lane is a compounding window and the expectation should be near-zero revenue in weeks 1–2.

Days 1–7 — build, publish almost nothing#

Day Action Metric watched
1 Shop setup, brand kit, four background templates. Publish 3 listings only (Toilet Talk singles 1–3). listing quality, tag stack renders, no over-length tags
2 Publish 4 more (Toilet Talk 4–6). Launch at 30% off to match the live median discount. first impressions
3 Publish Toilet Talk Set of 6 pack. Build 4 mockup scenes. CTR ≥1%
4 Publish Kitchen Rules 1–3. CTR; if <0.8% at 500+ views, the thumbnail is the problem — fix art, not budget
5 Publish Kitchen Rules 4–6. First read of velocity. views per listing
6 Publish Kitchen Rules pack. No ads yet. —
7 Publish Laundry 1–2. Day-7 checkpoint. see kill rule

Day-7 read: expect 0–8 sales total. That is normal and is not a signal. Alfie: "you'll see patterns in your own listings" only after consistent listing; 12 listings is genuinely early.

Days 8–14 — finish the catalogue, then open ads at $3#

  • Days 8–10: publish Laundry 3 + Utility pack + Affirmations 1–3. Catalogue now 19 listings.
  • Day 11: open ads at $3/day on the three best-CTR singles only. Not the packs. Alfie: start $3/day, never raise until spend hits the cap daily AND ROAS is positive.
  • Days 12–14: no new listings. Let the 19 run. Read CTR per listing; anything under 0.8% at 500+ views gets a new thumbnail, not a budget increase.

Days 15–21 — the first real read#

  • Day 21 checkpoint (the real one): of 19 listings, how many have ≥1 sale?
  • Corpus benchmark: 13–14% sell-through at 6 months. At 3 weeks, 2–3 listings with a sale is on-track.
  • If zero listings have sold after 3 weeks with ≥2,000 impressions across the shop, the art failed the legibility gate. Fix the art.
  • Publish the House Rules, Airbnb Edition 3-pack (the airbnb house rules sign pocket: 192 vol / 4,312 comp).
  • Only now consider the whole-shop bundle — do not build it before 30 designs exist. A bundle with no catalogue has nothing to bundle.

Days 22–30 — build the ladder, hold the ads#

  • Publish 6–10 more singles drawn from the remaining 8 concepts + new bathroom/kitchen concepts, on the same two background templates.
  • Ads: hold $3/day. If ROAS is positive and spend is capped daily, raise to $6/day. If not capped, the catalogue is the problem — go fix listings, per Alfie's escalation.
  • Day 30 checkpoint. Build the whole-shop bundle at $39.95 over everything live.

30-day financial expectation — stated honestly#

From §2d, lane A|B young-winner: yield $28.09/listing over 6 months, 86.4% dead, live-listing median 9 units at $5.98.

Scenario Listings live Listings selling 30-day revenue (gross sticker)
Pessimistic (median yw shop, NotedProgress/TheNuveArtDigitals profile) 25 3 $90–180
Base (ApexVisualsStudio profile: $10,506 over 6 months on 546 listings, scaled to 25 listings and 1 month) 25 4–6 $250–450
Upside (one listing finds its head; TheWallOwl did 632 units on 137 listings) 25 7 $600–1,200
Outlier (CreaPawsCo-style: one personalised offer at a $60 price point) 25 3 $2,000+

Against a ~$27-hour production investment, the base case is a $250–450 month-one gross. Net of Etsy's fees at a non-US digital seller's 11–30% band, that is roughly $175–400. The honest read: month one does not pay for month one, and the case for this direction rests entirely on listings 60–180 compounding — the corpus shows 37% of lane A's units come from listings ≤12 months old, and 57.3% from ≤24 months, so the catalogue is still paying after a year.

Ads budget: $3/day × 30 = $90. Base case does not break even on ads in month one. This is Alfie's own doctrine and the boss should expect it.

Metrics to watch, in order#

  1. CTR per listing (target 1–3%; <0.8% at 500+ views is a thumbnail failure).
  2. Sell-through rate (% of listings with ≥1 sale) — the corpus's honest benchmark is 13–14% at 6 months.
  3. ROAS once ads are open.
  4. Median price of live listings vs $6.95 — if live listings sit at $8.95+, pricing is too high and the dead tail will show it.
  5. Tag stack coverage — Etsy's search-used-tags report per listing.

The kill rule#

Kill the direction if, at day 90, any of these hold:

  • Fewer than 15% of listings have ever sold (corpus median is 13–14%, so this is a fail at the median).
  • Median CTR below 0.8% across listings with 500+ views after two rounds of thumbnail changes.
  • Zero listings above 50 lifetime units — meaning the art never found a head. ApexVisualsStudio reached 105 in six months; TheWallOwl 112.
  • Total catalogue revenue below $500 at 90 days against ~$80 of production spend per 20 listings.
  • The 30% launch discount cannot be removed — i.e. listed price never sells at full price.

Do not kill it for: a quiet first fortnight, a high dead tail in weeks 1–4 (the corpus shows 86–87% dead is the normal state of a young catalogue in this lane), or an ROAS below 1.0 in month one.

10. The case FOR, the case AGAINST, and what would kill it#

10a. The strong case FOR#

  1. The fragmentation is real and measurable. 138 distinct shops across 160 page-one organic slots. Two shops hold two slots between them. There is no incumbent monopolising these heads, which is the structural difference from a saturated lane. The three smallest-young-shop sales figures on page one — TheNuclearBard 85, CasaMelaArt 93, ZiggyGifts 500 — prove small shops hold top-3 slots on these terms today.
  2. Two heads have genuine headroom. funny toilet sign runs 543 monthly volume against 10,140 competing listings (0.0536 vol/comp); airbnb house rules sign runs 192 against 4,312 (0.0445); funny bathroom wall art runs 1,541 against 96,172. Compare funny wall art at 752,341 competing listings. This lane is winnable by specificity.
  3. The corpus's best-performing humour listing is exactly this product. Thelittleprintshopx's Nice ass Poster | Funny Toilet decor has 3,798 lifetime units from a single $13.36 print — more than the entire 1,846-listing typographic lane's unit count is worth per listing. A single well-chosen bathroom quip is a proven standalone business.
  4. The best young-shop quote listings are consistent and reproducible. ApexVisualsStudio: 6 listings at 35–105 units, $9.59 each, 6 months old, all the same product (sage-green-striped handwritten quotes). TheWallOwl: 5 listings at 28–112 units at $6.62–7.95. The pattern is a template plus a quote bank, not genius.
  5. Physical margin is available if the same art is printed. Page one is 99.4% physical at a $20.84 median, and the same artwork sells as a download and as a poster/giclée/canvas. The skill's standing note: wall art is the one type that works on both fulfilment models.
  6. Zero IP risk in the disciplined version. The corpus's IP problem is concentrated in fandom, sports and character shops (27.6% of young-shop rows vs 2.7% established). Typography quotes with no named artist, no lyric, no TV title carry none of it.
  7. It is the cheapest iteration-per-hour lane available. ~1h30m per listing, no COGS, no supplier, no shipping profile. Against a corpus where most lanes need AI art plus per-unit cost, this is the fastest design-iteration engine in the pack — which is the boss's stated interest.
  8. Alfie's one direct verdict on wall art is positive: "frame TV and printable wall arts are still growing and not saturated."

10b. The honest case AGAINST#

  1. The $86.28 headline is one shop. 78% of that lane's revenue is CreaPawsCo — 11 listings, $59.88 median, personalised pet photography. Strip it and the yield is $19.08, a 4.5x collapse. The boss's "second best yield in the corpus" is a taxonomy artefact, not a lane property. Any plan resting on $86.28 is resting on a pet portrait business.
  2. The honest young-shop yield is $28.09/listing, not $86.28. That is a 3x gap between the number quoted and the number a new shop gets, measured on a real 6-month window across 52 shops and 3,717 listings.
  3. The dead tail is 86.4% and the live listings are the cheap ones. Median live price $5.98 against a $6.80 lane median. This lane's mechanism is: publish a lot, sell the cheap ones, accept that 7 in 8 never move. That is a catalogue treadmill, not a product business.
  4. Catalogue volume does not work here — it fails in both directions. ApexVisualsStudio: 546 lane listings → $10,506 and 95% dead. TheNuveArtDigitals: 159 lane listings → $145 and 99% dead. Same lane, same price band, same seven months. The volume doctrine that works elsewhere has no purchase in this lane.
  5. The download side of these keywords is a ~$7 commodity market. Five live pulls put the download median at $5.88–$7.00. Page one there is 500+ Funny Printable Posters, 200 Funny Bathroom Animal Prints, Motivational Quotes Wall Art Bundle: 200 Printable Inspirational Posters. This is the catalogue-size race the corpus says a new shop loses, and it is a download shop's only entry.
  6. The two "respectable" faces of the lane are the worst businesses in it. Motivational: $132 yield on 358 listings, 71.8% dead, 60.7% of units from the top 10 listings. Family: 72 listings exist in the entire 73-shop corpus. And live, motivational wall art opens with three consecutive ad slots at 38% ad share.
  7. Page one in the bare heads is a physical market a download shop cannot enter. 1 of 160 is digital. The download only wins when the query says printable.
  8. The bundle mechanic is weak or inverted. Lane A: bundle $69.75/listing against single $88.38. Wave 1's own lane table: 1.3x, one of the three flat lanes. The folder's single largest lever does not work in this lane, and it works everywhere else at 8–53x.
  9. Discounting is not optional. 91/160 page-one listings on sale at a 30% median discount, with 11 at 50%+, 4 at 60% and one at 74%. Realised revenue is roughly 70% of sticker, before fees.
  10. The moat is median 2,600 lifetime sales, 71% above 1,000. You are bidding against that with zero reviews.
  11. The house-rules slice is a woodworking market, not a print market. Mrcwoodproducts at 265,100 lifetime sales (engraved wood) and TreasureBoxStudioLTD (acrylic play rules) sit at ranks 2–3 on house rules sign. BoutiqueBarn at $345 and ArtefaccioDesigns at $211 are reclaimed-wood sellers. The Airbnb digital pocket (airbnb house rules sign, 4,312 competition) is the only honest entry and it is month-2, small, and untested.
  12. The lane's own best sellers are the IP-adjacent ones. Thelittleprintshopx's Teenage Dirtbag Lyrics Print (820 units), Funny TV Quotes Wall Art | Friends Movie Quotes (242), Now Then Mardy Bum Print (222) — all quote text attached to a copyrighted work. The $86.28 lane's own top listing is a pop-culture parody. The viral end of this lane is the half you must not copy.

10c. What would kill it#

  • Already half-killed by the data, before any spend: the family third (72 listings corpus-wide), the motivational third (71.8% dead, 358 listings), the swears (no keyword demand: swear words print = 1 volume), and any IP-adjacent quote.
  • Live-kill conditions at day 90 are in §9: sell-through under 15%, median CTR under 0.8% after two thumbnail rounds, zero listings above 50 units, catalogue revenue under $500, or inability to remove the 30% launch discount.
  • The one number that would change the verdict: if a launch reaches two listings above 150 units within 60 days at a $6.95 price point, the lane's ceiling is far above what the corpus's average implies and the whole case flips. No shop in the corpus's young cohort has done it. One has.

10d. Verdict material — no verdict, the boss decides#

The evidence supports one narrow, high-specificity version of this direction and rejects the broad one:

Supported: a funny bathroom/toilet/utility typography shop at a $6.95 single price, built template-first (four fixed background palettes + a 28-quote bank), 20–30 listings, one room per series, one small personalisation cell as the margin tier, and one whole-shop bundle at $39.95 once 30 designs exist. Two heads with real headroom (funny toilet sign, funny bathroom wall art), a proven single-listing ceiling (3,798 units), zero IP risk in the disciplined version, and the fastest production loop in the pack.

Rejected: the broad quote/affirmation lane. $28.09 real yield, 86.4% dead, no bundle premium, a ~$7 download commodity market on the only entry a digital shop has, and a $20.84 physical page one that is 99.4% closed to downloads.

The trade the boss is actually making: the lane with the fastest iteration loop and the lowest cost of a failed experiment (~1h30m, zero COGS, killable in 90 days) against the lane with the weakest proven young-shop yield and the highest dependence on one listing catching fire. Whether 1h30m per listing is fast enough to find that listing before the rent is the judgement call, and it is not one the data can make.

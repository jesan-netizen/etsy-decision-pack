1. The direction in one paragraph#

A digital-download print shop whose entire catalogue is photo-real photographic prints — landscape, coastal, travel and black-and-white documentary photography — built bundle-backward: design a 300-print mega-bundle first, carve the 100-print and 24-print tiers out of the same assets, sell 6-prints and 3-prints as the discovery rungs, and let singles exist only as search bait for the bundles. The aesthetic commitment that makes it defensible is the found photograph, not the beautiful photograph: grain, halation, dust, contact-sheet numbering, faded dye colour, 1970s–80s documentary framing, no sunset, no drone, no perfect symmetry. That is a deliberate answer to the AI-slop problem (§3.6): a photo-real AI landscape is the most clockable thing on the platform, so the product must claim a provenance — scanned archive, contact sheet, found frame — rather than a beauty. The audience is the buyer filling a specific wall — hallway, home office, lake house, cabin, beach rental — who wants a place that is not there. Shape: Engine A, bundle-led digital. The format reality on live Etsy is the hard part and §3 states it plainly: page one for these 16 terms is 98.8% physical product.

3.4 The AI-slop backdrop, stated with what is actually verified#

From external_trends.md §2.4, already established in Wave 1 and not re-litigated here:

  • [V] Etsy's audited Q4/FY2025: active buyers 86.5m, down 3.4% YoY; active sellers 5.6m, down 1.5%.
  • [V] WIRED and Marketplace both documented an exodus of human sellers over mass-produced and AI-generated listings, with AI pet portraits displacing human ones in search at a fraction of the price.
  • [V] Etsy's own trend language attaches its +110% wall art decor YoY to "curated" and "nostalgic" — "a collected over time look, mixing different frame styles like vintage wood and aged metals."

New, first-party, verified for this document by direct extract of etsy.com/legal/creativity/ and etsy.com/seller-handbook/article/1275449912004:

  • [V] AI art is permitted. Etsy's Creativity Standards define "Seller-prompted AI creations: Creations that were generated using AI tools … based on a seller's original prompts" as a permitted category, and the Seller Handbook (page dated 2026-09-03) states: "Sellers must disclose within their listing description if an item is created with the use of AI."
  • [V] The attribution line matters. Digital downloads of the seller's original designs sit under "Designed by a seller" — a first-class category, not a loophole. A digital print shop is compliant by construction if it discloses.
  • [V] AI prompt bundles are prohibited. Selling the prompts separately from the finished artwork is banned. Irrelevant to this direction except as a reminder that the artwork must be a real deliverable.

The honest risk, and it is specific to photography rather than generic. external_trends.md §3.3 argues that stylised and heritage illustration "avoids the trust tax" because viewers grant animation the licence they withhold from photoreal. Photography is the one medium with no such licence available. A Victorian botanical plate can be attributed to a tradition; a photo-real coastline cannot be attributed to anything except a camera, and the buyer's first question — who took this, and can I buy the licence? — has no good answer for a generated image. Search results also document that Etsy's automated review specifically looks for "unusual lighting patterns, inconsistent shadows … and the characteristic 'too perfect' quality of AI-generated scenes" — a heuristic written for exactly this content type.

5.4 Description approach#

Six blocks, in this order:

  1. What the buyer gets — number of prints, exact sizes, file formats, resolution, colour profile. First line, because 92% of page-one buyers are looking at framed product and the digital buyer needs the spec to trust the file.
  2. The aesthetic, in one sentence of provenance — "Scanned-archive look: 35mm film grain, faded dye colour, dust and halation, contact-sheet numbering." This is the AI-trust answer from §3.4: claim a provenance, not a beauty. Never say "AI-generated" in the body and never claim the photographs are the seller's own.
  3. The AI disclosure, explicit and early — required by Etsy's Seller Handbook ("Sellers must disclose within their listing description if an item is created with the use of AI"), and it is also the cheapest trust asset available. One clear sentence, in the second block, not buried.
  4. Room-by-room placement — hallway, home office, lake house, above the bed, stairwell. This is the external_trends.md §3.3 "sandwich" structure: the outcome before the spec.
  5. Room mockups — the five-beat arc. Problem (the empty wall) → escalation (still reads unfinished) → discovery (the set) → transformation (staged room) → CTA (download). Mockups are staged renderings of the delivered files, and the disclosure covers them — the first image must be the actual flat print file, not a mockup, per the listing-image requirements in §3.4's automated-review note.
  6. Delivery and licence — instant download, ZIP, no physical shipping, personal-use licence terms stated.

Do not open with a file list. external_trends.md §3.3: a bundle listing that opens with the room outcome and closes with the files reads as advice; one that opens with a file list reads as a spec sheet. Same files, different order.

7.2 Medium and surface#

  • 35mm film grain, visible. Grain is not an overlay; it is generated per-print with a fixed seed so the same print always has the same grain.
  • Halation on highlights, dust and light scratches at 2–5 per print, slight edge burn on 30% of prints.
  • Never sharp. Maximum local contrast is held below what a phone camera gives. No print in the catalogue may look like it came off a modern mirrorless file.
  • Contact-sheet numbering burned into the bottom margin on 40% of prints — the single strongest provenance signal, and free.

7.3 Light#

  • Overcast, fog-diffused or low-golden-hour. No hard midday sun, no blue-hour neon.
  • Light direction must be consistent across a series — one series, one sun angle.
  • Shadow detail retained: no crushed blacks, no blown highlights. Crushed blacks are the AI tell.

7.4 Composition rules (hard constraints)#

  1. Rule of thirds, no centring. A centred subject reads as generated unless it is architectural and symmetric on purpose.
  2. Horizon in the upper or lower third, never dead centre. A dead-centre horizon is the single most-cited AI artefact in landscape imagery.
  3. One subject per frame. No collages, no diptychs, no split compositions.
  4. A human figure may appear at most once in any 24 prints, and never closer than 40 metres — small, unidentifiable, facing away. A recognisable face triggers the rights question (§3.4).
  5. Imperfect framing allowed and required. A leaning horizon, a clipped branch, a half-cut building. At least 25% of prints should visibly not be a centred postcard.
  6. No symmetrical reflections. Water may reflect, but not as a perfect mirror.
  7. Aspect discipline: every print is generated at its delivery ratio natively, not cropped from a master. Cropping is where AI artefacts survive into a print — repeated leaf patterns and banding in sky gradients.

7.6 Series that scale to 100+ without style drift#

The drift risk is named here because it is the trap that killed four shops in the corpus: Wave 1's own note on Lumiora is that style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, and directions_from_data.md §Section 2's no clear aesthetic bucket (22,799 listings, $144.97/listing) is full of shops that served many niches inside no style.

The drift-control mechanism is that the series are distinguished by place and condition, never by style. All five series are the same camera, the same film stock, the same grain, the same palette, the same seven composition rules. A reviewer can hold two prints from Series C and Series D side by side and be certain they came from the same shop and the same session. That is the external_trends.md §3.3 "reusable visual asset" transfer applied properly: recognisable with the shop name removed.

Scales to 300 by location, not by new looks. Series A holds 60 fog-forest compositions — different ridge, different tree species, different weather, same rules. There is no series 6, no "autumn collection", no seasonal reskin. The 2027 blue-green capsule (§7.1) is the only sanctioned change and it changes colour, not composition.

The measurable drift test, to run at listing 100 and 200: if more than 15% of the catalogue would be rejected by a reviewer shown only the first 20 prints, the series has drifted and the next 100 are cut.

8.2 The asset-pool rule#

Every print is generated once and drawn on by up to 18 listings. Listing 1 (300-print bundle) and listing 6 (complete collection) are free — they are the same 300 files zipped differently. Listings 2–5 are free — they are the five series re-zipped. Listings 7–9 and 10–13 are free — they are subsets of the same files, in the same ratios. Listings 14–18 are the only ones that need new curation decisions, not new art.

So 18 listings require 300 generated prints, not 1,000. That is the difference between this direction being $400 of generation time and $4,000, and it is the single largest cost lever in the plan.

8.3 What the mockup set looks like#

Three per listing, per external_trends.md §3.3's five-beat structure:

  1. Flat file on the bone ground — image 1, the actual deliverable, no frame, no mockup. This is the listing-image requirement.
  2. Staged room — hallway with a runner and a console; home office above a desk; lake-house living room with a low sofa. Neutral furniture, warm neutral walls, so the print is the only saturated thing in frame. Never a styled maximalist room — that is a different lane.
  3. Detail crop at 200–300% — because that is the magnification at which the §7.2 grain, dust and halation are visible, and visible grain is the whole anti-AI argument. This image is the trust asset.

8.4 The disclosure, written once and used everywhere#

One sentence, in description block 3 of every listing and in the shop policies:

"This collection is created with AI image generation. Each print has been produced from an original composition, then grain, dust and halation were applied as a fixed, repeated treatment so the full collection reads as one series. Files are delivered as described. Please check Etsy's listing disclosure setting for this listing."

That covers generation and upscaling and the mockups, in the place Etsy's Seller Handbook requires it ("Sellers must disclose within their listing description"), and it is also the copy that makes a buyer who was going to leave anyway stay.

9.6 The kill rule#

The kill condition, stated as one number:

At day 30, if the shop has not sold 90 units in total — an average of 3 units per day, or $12–14/day at the modelled AOV — this direction is dead and the catalogue is archived, not iterated.

Supporting sub-rules, any one of which triggers the same outcome:

  1. Below 15 units at day 14 → stop immediately. Do not spend the ads budget in §9.4 day 25.
  2. Zero favourites on the 300-bundle hero at day 14 → the top-of-funnel offer is wrong. Stop.
  3. Two or more buyer messages or reviews stating the art looks AI-generated → stop regardless of sales. This is the one failure that compounds: a shop's whole catalogue reads as one system, so one public accusation reaches all 36 listings.
  4. Any AI disclosure-takedown or policy warning → stop. §3.4's verified enforcement means a repeat violation ends the account.
  5. Day-30 AOV below $9.99 → the bundle ladder did not take. The direction's entire thesis is that buyers pay $13–20 for sets; if they paid $6.49, the price finding in §2.4 is wrong about this lane and the plan is not repairable by iteration.

The upside case, stated as one number: 36 listings, median $14.99 AOV, 3 units/day average = $1,615/month gross, before Etsy's ~11–30% fee stack and a ~65–70% take rate. That is Engine A at a modest scale, and it sits inside the PureVibesArtDesign / AModernArtShop / IamHomeStudio band from §3.5, which is the honest ceiling for a three-month-old shop in this lane. It is not the $13,540/6-month figure — that shop has 70 listings at $12.39 and an established catalogue.

10. Verdict material#

10.1 The strong case FOR — with numbers#

  1. Three shops are running this exact play and clearing $5k–$13.5k per six months. PureVibesArtDesign: 70 listings, 1,001 units, $13,540 in 6 months, median $12.39, on Nature Prints Bundle, 100 Prints. AModernArtShop: $12,581, median $29.35, Set of 100 Minimalist Prints. IamHomeStudio: $9,840, median $10.90, 100 Nature Prints, landscape photography wall art. This is not a theoretical lane; it is three verified shops.
  2. The winner's shape is exactly the folder's doctrine. 100-print bundle, count-anchored, $12.39, one aesthetic, nature subject. Bundle-first launch has the strongest evidence in the whole Wave 1 study (shops17_analysis.md §6b: 2.99× within-shop median, 5.6× in month 1), and this lane's best live example is a textbook application of it.
  3. The price ladder is a free 5.8×. §2.4: the $16–20 band returns $331.90/listing against $38.39 in $5–8, with a 16-point better dead tail. 60% of the existing tier is priced 3–4 stops too low. Correcting the band costs nothing and needs no new product.
  4. The best keywords in the entire 46-seed pull are this lane's aesthetic. amalfi coast print 277/8,245 (33.60), misty forest print 226/7,528 (30.02), foggy landscape print 171/9,764 (17.51), tuscany print 118/7,817 (15.10), aerial photography print 118/10,417 (11.33). All are place-and-condition phrases; none is generic. And black and white landscape carries 1,043 monthly searches at a 6.63 ratio — real volume at a survivable ratio, which no head term in this lane offers.
  5. Digital competition on the target terms is nearly absent. 12 digital cards on 960 page-one slots. 588 distinct shops compete, and the repeaters are physical galleries. On misty fog, aerial coast and Nordic forest long-tails there is effectively no incumbent to displace.
  6. The lane is IP-free once the celebrities are removed, which is the house rule. Landscape, coast, fog, desert and alpine subjects carry no rights burden. That is a genuine structural advantage over every other lane in the corpus except the abstract and the botanical.
  7. AI is permitted, disclosed and defensible. Verified first-party: Etsy's Creativity Standards define seller-prompted AI creations as a permitted category, digital downloads of original designs sit under "Designed by a seller", and disclosure in the listing description is the stated requirement. Compliance is a sentence, not a legal restructuring.
  8. Scale is cheap here. 300 prints in ~68 hours because every listing draws from one pool and 12 of the 18 listings are re-zips of the same files. 13.5 min/print amortised. That is the lowest production cost of any direction in this batch.
  9. The aesthetic has an honest answer to the AI problem. The §7.2 fixed-seed grain, dust, halation and contact-sheet numbering, plus the §8.3 detail-crop mockup, produce something a buyer can see is deliberately made. §3.4's verified enforcement makes the alternative (undisclosed) strictly worse: hidden AI is a takedown risk, disclosed AI with a visible hand is a product.
  10. Directional momentum exists. directions_from_data.md §Section 3 records a growing number of qualified directions inside coastal, landscape and travel cells, and external_trends.md §1 puts wall art decor at +110% YoY on Etsy's own first-party data with Etsy's trend language explicitly "curated" and "nostalgic".

10.2 The honest case AGAINST — with numbers#

  1. The corpus yield ranks 18th of 21. Tier B photo-realistic: $61.26/listing against a corpus average of $175.63 and a gallery wall set cell of $2,456.46. Tier A photo-explicit: $26.10, 20th of 21, beating only minimalist / neutral / beige at $17.19. This is the direction's own number, computed from 40,275 rows.
  2. The pure-scene evidence base is 45 listings and $837. Strip the celebrities and this direction has 45 listings, 142 units and $837 of gross revenue across the entire 73-shop corpus — below the 40-listing lane qualification floor, with 2 shops, a median of 1 unit per listing, and a 80% dead tail. The one shop that scaled (ArtivaprintShop) did it on olive tree and Mediterranean courtyard prints, i.e. a botanical-adjacent subject, not pure landscape.
  3. The within-shop test fails: 0.68× median, 4 of 18 shops. This is Wave 1's own doctrine test, the one that killed the faith lane at corpus level. Photography listings do not beat their own shops' alternative listings. The three zeroes (ArttHouses, DigiLabsy, PosterluxPrint at exactly 0.00×) are not noise.
  4. 92.2% of the tier's units are celebrity/film/fashion subject. 86.9% of listings. By the house rule in directions_from_data.md §6 — "Any direction whose examples are IP-led should be rejected as a project" — the corpus photography lane is already disqualified. The compliant version is the 45-listing remainder, which is item 2 above.
  5. Page one is 98.8% physical. 948 of 960 cards, median $43.28. Only 12 digital cards, median $8.50. The lane's own head term photography wall art returns a $53.02 median of framed and canvas product, and landscape wall art returns $99.45. There is no large visible digital demand on these terms — only an absence of visible digital competition, which is not the same thing.
  6. The lane's head term is unwinnable. photography wall art: 740 monthly searches against 1,128,844 competing listings (1-in-1,525). cityscape print 1-in-1,758. landscape photo 1-in-4,738. Everbee's suggestion list for the head is pure word-order permutation at ~1.13M competition each, which means there is no long-tail structure underneath it to exploit.
  7. The bundle mechanic is the weakest of any mid-size lane. 1.41× against a 2.99× corpus median and a 4.0× coastal-lane multiple. Bundle share of units is 6.0%, second-lowest of any qualified lane. Count-anchoring on plain titles is 1.00× — exactly zero lift. The corridor is structurally different: coastal bundles run 0.34×, travel 0.53×.
  8. Age control says photography does not mature. 3–6 months: 0.91× (par). 6–12 months: 0.52×. 12+ months: 0.30×. The listings that had time to win were out-earned by everything else in the corpus. This is not a rookie-catalogue artefact — only 19.5% of the tier is under 3 months old.
  9. The tier is priced wrong and that is not fixable by this shop alone. 59.9% of it sits at $5–8 returning $38.39. Correcting to $16–20 is only available to a new shop with a bundle ladder; the 44 existing shops cannot move without repricing a live catalogue. So the ladder is available as an opportunity and as evidence that nobody with an established catalogue has taken it — which is what §10.1 item 3 and this item say at the same time.
  10. Two of the ten shops running this tier are closed (ThePlumAtelier, BloomJetsetPrints), and both closed with photography listings in the book. The four closed shops in the young-winner sweep are Wave 1's honest control: "Mechanics transfer; outcomes do not."
  11. The trust exposure is worst exactly here. Photography is the one medium with no stylistic licence available — external_trends.md §3.3's argument that stylised or heritage work "avoids the trust tax" does not apply to photoreal output. Etsy's automated review is documented as looking for "unusual lighting patterns, inconsistent shadows … the characteristic 'too perfect' quality of AI-generated scenes". And the one AI-native shop ranking on page one in this lane, ImageShopsAI, is at a $190 physical price point — i.e. the AI-native photography play is going premium-physical, not digital-cheap.
  12. The live competitor is a photographer, not a print shop. 588 page-one shops named as galleries and studios, selling named real places at named real places — Framed Big Sur Ocean Photography Print, Mt Fuji Lawson Sunrise Print, La Pelosa Sardinia Aerial Photography Print. A generated-image shop has no honest claim to a place it has not stood in, and the buyer knows the difference.
  13. The 12 digital cards are mostly not this direction. Of the 12, four are the direction's thesis ($13.58–$21.00), four are fashion/IP bundles at $4–9 (James Bond, 350+ Stockholm Fashion), two are paintings (GoldFrameVintageArt ×2), one is a JPG background pack, one is a city-apartment illustration. 4 of 12 = the thesis. The rest is noise.
  14. No corpus operator to copy. The best-aligned shop, NotedProgress, is 69.6% photography, $1,149/mo, $6.77 median, 73.9% dead — and its top listing is an unlicensed Sex and the City poster. There is no compliant, successful, pure-scene photography shop in the 73 to learn from.

10.3 What would kill it — the honest short list#

  • Takedown or disclosure failure. Repeat AI-disclosure violations end the account (§3.4, verified enforcement). The catalogue is one coherent system, so one policy action reaches all 36 listings.
  • The trust argument failing empirically. If buyers discover and reject the images, the §7.2 treatment has not worked and there is no version of photoreal AI photography that does not have this problem.
  • A platform aesthetic shift back toward illustration. external_trends.md §1.2 finds the highest-transfer 2026 Pinterest trends are drawing-led — Neo Deco, Laced Up, Wilderkind, Poetcore — not photographic. A photography direction bets against the documented direction of travel in its own adjacent platform.
  • The AI-free alternative collapsing. If the category's floor is genuine archive photography (page one's physical side), the digital tier is competing for the residue.
  • Google/Pinterest favouring real photography. Discovery for this lane runs substantially off-site, and both reward real, attributable imagery. A generated landscape that reaches a non-Etsy surface is a liability there.
  • The long-tail being too thin to fill 300 prints. The five best keywords total 930 monthly searches between them. That is real, but it is not a volume business, and §6 makes clear there is no head term to fall back on.
  • Price-band arbitrage being unavailable in practice. The $16–20 opportunity assumes buyers will pay it for a generated image when a real framed photograph is $43–$99 on the same page.

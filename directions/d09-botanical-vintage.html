1. The direction in one paragraph#

The lane is the Victorian natural-history plate: hand-coloured botanical studies, herbarium sheets, folk-art birds, and mycology plates, sold as digital downloads into a $4.99 single / $9.98 three-set / $12.99 six-set / $19.99 twenty-plate family / $29.99 whole-shop ladder. The aesthetic is foxed paper on cream, one specimen per plate, engraved or stipple linework under translucent watercolour wash, a Latin binomial or a period common name in a small serif, aged edges, no modern styling and no text overlay. The audience is the buyer decorating a named surface — a hallway, a dressing room, a home office, a cottage kitchen — who has already decided they want the 19th-century-apothecary look and does not want to search for it; the shape strategy is bundle-first and count-anchored, which is the one thing the corpus measurably rewards inside this lane. Design order is 120 specimen plates → five families of 24 → four room sets of 3–6 → four singles extracted last. The single decision the direction turns on is whether the shop sells "botanical prints" (0.79x on the 6-month basis, 646,506 competing listings) or sells "a set of six antique plant studies" — measured at 3.93x the new-shop-comparable yardstick and 1.91x its own uncounted titles. Those are two different businesses.

Explicitly excluded from the lane's spine, on measured grounds: mushrooms (0.33x, 41 listings, 4.9% sold) and pressed flowers (0.24x, 28 listings, 7.1% sold). Both are in the brief's aesthetic description and both fail the corpus test. They appear in this plan as specimens inside a set, never as a lane.

Which is precisely why vintage cannot be the differentiator: the corpus has no problem with vintage and no problem with botanical, and the differentiation has to come from the plate format, the count, and the room.

Four readings, all load-bearing:

  1. Botanical at 0–3 months is 0.63x the whole corpus at the same tenure ($2.11 vs $3.33) and 4.1% of listings have sold at all. Almost nothing sells in the first quarter. This is the real launch state and it is worse than the lane average suggests.
  2. Botanical bundles at 0–3 months are $7.44 — 3.5x botanical singles at the same tenure and 2.2x the entire corpus at that tenure. The shape advantage exists on day one, not only after compounding. This is the single most encouraging number in the document.
  3. Botanical does not overtake the corpus until the 6–12 month band ($31.97 vs $33.54) — and the botanical bundle is already 2.3x the corpus at that band. The lane is a compounding lane with a slow start.
  4. The count-anchor is worthless at launch and worth 1.91x after. At 0–3 months, count-anchored botanical titles run $0.20/listing against $2.69 uncounted — 0.08x, i.e. worse. At 3–12 months the count-anchor inverts to $42.60 vs $22.33, 1.91x. Read: buyers do not respond to "100+ prints" on a listing with no reviews; they do respond to it once the listing has aged. The count-anchor must be in the title from day one anyway — it costs nothing and it is what the buyer filters on — but the plan must not expect a mega bundle to earn anything in its first 90 days. This inverts Wave 1's "N+ is the only format with a minority of listings failing" for a new shop, and it is a correction to directions/04-vintage-bundle-shop.md §4 as well.

5.3 Description approach#

alfie_deep.md quotes Sharda: "99% of buyers do not read the descriptions, they rely on the listing photos." So short, functional, honest, in this order:

  1. One line: what is in the file. "Six antique botanical plates, one specimen per sheet, sized 2x3, 3x4, 4x5, 5x7, 8x10 and square."
  2. One line: what it is not. "Digital files only, no frame or paper included."
  3. The download sentence, in plain words. "Right after purchase you'll get a download link straight away, no email waiting."
  4. One paragraph of taste. Two sentences on the paper and the age of the look, in the buyer's language.
  5. One line: the room. "The two A4s and two A3s read well side by side above a dresser; the 8x10 anchors a stairwell."

7. Concepts — the aesthetic bible, 24+ subjects, and 100 designs without drift#

7.1 The aesthetic bible#

This lane's failure mode is being mistaken for the free Redouté archive, so the bible is written as the specific set of things that make a plate not a Redouté scan. Every rule is checkable against the previous design.

Palette — a closed five-ink set on a paper ground. Nothing outside this list, ever:

Ink Hex Role
Paper (aged) #EFE6D2 the ground. Foxed cream. Never pure #FFFFFF, never grey
Paper (foxed edge) #E2D4B4 the aged border and the plate margin. This is the single most important value in the bible
Sepia #6B4F35 line and type. The only dark
Olive #7A7A4C primary wash
Slate blue #5B6B7A secondary wash
Oxide red #9C5A3C accent, ≤5% of any plate, and only on the berry/berry-leaf specimens

No black. No saturated green, pink or purple. If a plate needs a seventh value, the specimen is wrong. No more than three inks per plate — sepia line plus one or two washes.

Medium — the Victorian specimen plate, and the four things that make it one. The escape from the CC0 archive is specificity in four places, all of which are checkable:

  1. The plate frame. A thin double-ruled sepia rectangle inset 6–8% from the sheet edge, with a hand-torn deckle on the outer paper edge and uneven foxing concentrated at the corners. Redouté scans do not have deckle edges and do not have foxing.
  2. The line. Copperplate stipple-and-line: fine sepia contour, with stipple dotting in the shadow side only and no cross-hatching. Consistent 0.4–0.6 pt weight at final size, no variation along a contour. Hand-drawn, not vector.
  3. The wash. Translucent watercolour under the line, not over it, in at most two inks, with a visible hard edge where the wash stops and one deliberate granulation or pooling per plate. The wash is imperfect on purpose.
  4. The type. A Latin binomial in a small old-style serif, plus a period common name, plus a plate number — set in the lower third, small, never centred, never large. nomen nudum (the "no-name" state) is the correct form for specimens that have no real binomial.

Light. Flat, even, no cast shadow, no directional source. A specimen plate is lit from nowhere. Any plate that reads as "an oil painting of a flower in a vase" is out — that is NorthPrints and LoveDictionary territory, and it is the 0.62x single-print cell.

Composition rules. One specimen per plate, occupying 40–58% of the plate's inner field, centred, sitting on the lower third. Margin ≥15% inside the frame line. Portrait 2:3 is the primary format (a plate is a portrait object), with 3:4 and square as secondary and 4:5 for the room-set arrangements. Never a background, never a border beyond the plate frame, never a frame mockup inside the art, never a caption that isn't the binomial.

The five drift rules, stated as kill criteria for a single plate:

  1. If it could be a Redouté scan, it is out. Specifically: no pink-lilac ground, no white-flowered-spray-on-black composition, no baroque diagonal stem swash. Those are the three most-copied Redouté signatures and the three fastest ways to be mistaken for the free archive.
  2. If it is a flower in a vase, a bouquet, or a garden scene, it is out. That is the 0.62x single cell.
  3. If it has more than three inks, or a plate frame thinner than 0.4 pt, it is out.
  4. If the type is modern (any sans-serif, or a serif set in a contemporary weight), it is out.
  5. If it is not recognisable as the same shop's work with the name removed, it is out. external_trends.md's own strategic read applied literally: "the trends that survive this environment are the ones with real cultural provenance — Victorian botanical plates… they read as researched, not generated." A specific, traceable, authored aesthetic becomes the differentiator rather than the liability.

8. Production plan#

8.1 How the art gets made#

Per listing_runbook.md, art generation is Kittl (kittl-generate-media, kittl-list-image-generation-models, kittl-list-image-styles), with image_generate as the stated fallback if Kittl fails. No Kittl tokens are spent by this document — the plan below is the plan, not the execution.

One honest observation before the workflow, because it decides the tool. A Victorian copperplate specimen plate is a line-drawing-plus-flat-wash artefact. That is inside what a generation model does badly — stipple dotting, consistent line weight across a contour, and Latin binomials are all things models get subtly wrong, and a wrong binomial is a factual error on a product whose entire claim is research. So the workflow is generation for the plate body plus deterministic overlay for the line-and-type layer, and the honest recommendation is that the type layer is set in a real typesetter, not generated. alfie_deep.md's rule — "Keep style wording IDENTICAL across prompts, varying only subject" — is what prevents the drift this lane is most prone to.

The prompt architecture — deliberately boring:

  • One base style string, written once, never varied: Victorian botanical specimen plate, copperplate stipple-and-line engraving, single specimen centred on an aged cream sheet, thin double-ruled sepia plate frame inset from the deckle edge, foxed and browned paper at the corners, translucent olive and slate-blue watercolour wash beneath the line, no background beyond the plate, no vase, no bouquet, no border beyond the frame, no text, 2:3 portrait, archival scan quality
  • Subject token only: single pressed fern frond, sori pattern visible, sepia line only.
  • A negative token list, always appended: no redoute, no modern illustration, no flat vector, no sans-serif, no pink, no black background, no floral arrangement, no ribbon, no scrollwork, no baroque diagonal, no photorealism.

Workflow per master: base prompt + subject → generate 4 variants → pick the one that passes the five drift rules in §7.1 → overlay the plate frame, deckle edge, foxing layer and the binomial in a real editor (this is the 12-minute step that separates this from a Midjourney grid-search) → upscale to 300 DPI at print resolution (kittl-upscale-image) → export at 2:3, 3:4, 4:5, 5:7, 8:10 and square, which is exactly the six-ratio set already in listing1/art/delivery/ and the set the description promises.

Three gates, each tied to a measured benchmark at the right tenure, evaluated at fixed days so nothing is rationalised.

Gate 1 — day 14. Placement failure. If the three hero sets (#1, #5, #10) have fewer than 10 combined impressions, the problem is the thumbnail or the aesthetic, not the shape. Action: swap all mockups to Mockup C (the dark cabinet) and to the plate-grid infographic as the second image, and re-test. If still under 10 impressions at day 21, kill.

Gate 2 — day 30. Shape failure. If fewer than 2 of the first 14 listings have sold, the shop is below the whole-corpus 0–3-month rate of 6.0% and below botanical's own 4.1%. Action: confirm whether it is shape or aesthetic — if the sets are selling and the singles are not, the singles were the mistake and the shop is now a set shop, which is the plan's design anyway. If neither shape has 2 sales at day 30, hold to day 60 with $3/day; kill at 60.

Gate 3 — day 90. The real verdict, on the compounding curve. Units per listing below 0.99 for bundles (against the lane's own 0–3mo bundle benchmark) and bundle revenue share below 25% at day 90. Action: do not kill before 90. §2.6 shows botanical does not overtake the corpus until the 6–12-month band; killing at day 30 for a slow curve is precisely how a $29.80/listing 3–6-month cell gets thrown away. Kill at 90 if bundles are still under 0.99 u/l and the bundle share is under 25%.

Absolute kill, any day: an IP or style-claim complaint on any listing (the plan names no living artist, no 20th-century hand, no Redouté and touches no Audubon web files — so this would mean a genuine third-party claim); or cumulative ad spend exceeding $90 with zero sales; or Etsy finding a listing to be a duplicate of an existing listing, which the plate-frame convention is specifically designed to prevent.

10. Case FOR, case AGAINST, what would kill it, verdict material#

10.1 The strong case FOR — seven arguments, each with its number#

1. The winning cell is measured, reproducible and larger than it looks: count-anchored botanical sets are 3.93x a new-shop-comparable yardstick at 60–67% sold. Inside the 19-shop new-shop cohort (§2.5): botanical + set at 13 listings, $260.69/listing, 3.93x, 30.8% sold; vintage + floral + set at 6 listings, $225.34/listing, 3.40x, 66.7% sold. Across the wider young-winner basis (§2.11): botanical + set at 192 listings across 23 shops, $55.36/listing, 2.57x, 27.6% sold and vintage + floral + set at 133 listings across 22 shops, $60.90/listing, 2.82x, 30.1% sold. Those are 22 and 23 independent shops, not one. A shop that does the one thing this lane's own best listings do is fixing an execution gap, not taking a risk on demand.

2. The bundle premium inside botanical is the largest of any lane in the corpus, and it is the highest on the like-for-like basis too. Wave 1: $3,191.50/listing for botanical bundles against $59.68 for singles — 53.5x, the highest multiple of 16 lanes. My recomputation: botanical bundles earn 3.10x botanical singles on the 6-month basis and 2.53x what an average young-winner single earns ($40.99 vs $16.19). Shop-and-age-band matched across 61 cells, bundles win 36 of 61 (59%). Direction 6 established the general principle; this lane is its strongest instance.

3. The shape advantage exists on day one, not only after compounding — which is rare and is the single most important operational finding. At 0–3 months' listing tenure, botanical bundles earn $7.44/listing at 0.99 units/listing and 5.9% sold, against $2.11/listing and 0.30 u/l for botanical singles and $3.33/listing for the entire corpus at that tenure. Bundles are 3.5x singles and 2.2x the whole corpus on day one. A lane whose mechanic works before reviews exist is a different proposition from one that needs compounding first.

4. The keyword layer is the best of any direction in this pack, and it is not made of artefacts. vintage botanical print 1,990 vol / 165,743 comp = 0.0120; botanical print set 1,723 / 73,347 = 0.0235; sage green wall art 1,055 / 34,518 = 0.0306; floral gallery wall set 805 / 15,274 = 0.0527; dark botanical print 687 / 27,597 = 0.0249; botanical chart 366 / 7,919 = 0.0462. Four terms above 1,000 monthly volume with a real ratio. Direction 6's verdict on its own lane was that "the keyword layer of this direction is, in plain terms, empty of exploitable demand" and that its good ratios were Everbee artefacts. That is not true here, and the difference is not subtle. And the top of the list is a shape term and a room term, which is where the corpus says the money is.

5. This is the only direction in the pack with a clean IP position, and that is a structural advantage, not a nicety. Redouté's plates are CC0 (Met and Cleveland, verified 9 Sep 2026); Audubon's Birds of America is public domain per the Audubon Society and the Internet Archive. The corpus's botanical leaders are 100% public-domain vocabulary, against a house rule that licensed-IP share is 27.6% of young-shop rows and that "any direction whose examples are IP-led should be rejected as a project, however good the numbers look." directions_from_data.md §6 names sports/fandom and character-led as where that rule bites hardest; this lane is where it does not bite at all. That removes the largest structural risk in the pack.

6. The external trend evidence is unusually well-aligned, and the report's own strategic conclusion names this lane explicitly. external_trends.md §2.4's read: "the trends that survive this environment are the ones with real cultural provenance (Victorian botanical plates, Art Deco geometry, Ethiopian textile motifs, Aldi-style circus illustration) — they read as researched, not generated." Victorian botanical plates are the first item in that list. Pinterest Predicts 2026's Wilderkind trend is described as "whimsical woodland — moths, beetles, butterflies, fungi, forest curiosities" with "dragonfly nails" +145% and "animal-inspired outfits" +90%, and external_trends.md §1.2's own judgement is that Wilderkind's "natural-history illustration — moths and fungi are already Victorian specimen-plate vocabulary" makes it "one of the ones a printable line serves natively." That is this shop's Family C, named by Pinterest's own trend report. Add Pinterest's 2026 Hobbies report ("dream thrift finds" +550%, "vintage fall aesthetic" over +1,000%) and the report's own design thesis for it: "a genuinely vintage-sourced look (period illustration reproductions, faded palettes, foxed paper edges) rather than a modern clean vector" — which is §7.1's palette, verbatim. And the designer-survey colour read: chocolate brown is the leading designer colour at 33% of 468 professionals, "almost double" the 2022 share, with a documented counter-trend from dopamine decor to warm neutrals (Forbes, Jan 2026).

7. The assets are the cheapest in the pack per listing, and the ratio is the best in the pack. One plate at ~38 minutes makes nine listings (§8.3). 30 masters = 19 hours = a 46-listing launch in ~22 hours of work. The folder's gate is volume-based (alfie_deep.md: "58 digital wall art listings is really low", and Sharda's "if you stick yourself to just 50 listings, you don't understand what your buyer is looking for"), and §2.12 says the lane pays at 151–400 listings. 10.2 The honest case AGAINST — eight arguments, each with its number#

1. The lane is 0.79x on the like-for-like basis, and the Wave 1 number that made this Tier 1 is two shops. Botanical on the 6-month basis: 2,919 listings, 42 shops, $17.00/listing, 0.79x the yardstick, 16.6% sold. Anyone selecting this direction from directions_from_data.md alone is selecting it for a two-shop artefact. That is exactly the trap Direction 6 caught with line art × botanical, and it applies to the whole lane here.

2. At launch, botanical is the worst of the tested lanes, not the best. At 0–3 months' tenure: botanical $2.11/listing, 0.30 u/l, 4.1% sold against the whole corpus at $3.33, 0.45 u/l, 6.0% sold. This is a two-year cash profile in the shape of it, and the honest first-month expectation is $0–$100.

3. A shop that writes the copy its aesthetic wants to write is invisible; it has to write the copy the corpus and the keyword data support, which is a narrower and more commercial vocabulary than the brief describes.

4. The free archive is already on Etsy at a third of the price with 690 reviews. Digital page one: median $7.99, 51% of digital cards under $8, 94% under $15, 22% Bestseller, ~37.5% ads, 57.8% discounts. 10.4 Verdict material (no verdict — the boss decides)#

The one-line reading of the evidence: A Victorian botanical plate is a defensible, low-IP-risk, cheap-to-produce, exceptionally well-keyworded lane whose mechanic is count-anchored bundles and whose margin lives entirely in the shape — but the lane itself runs 0.79x, is 0.63x at launch tenure, and is competing against a free CC0 archive already listed at $3.15 with 690 reviews.

What the numbers support:

  • The direction is not a lane; it is a shape applied to a lane. botanical is 0.79x. botanical + set is 2.57x. botanical + set inside a new-shop cohort is 3.93x. botanical + nursery is 1.94x, and 5.26x in that cohort. The whole case lives in the third axis.
  • The plan's economics are set by the tenure ladder, not the lane average. $2.11/listing at 0–3mo → $14.67 at 3–6mo → $31.97 at 6–12mo, with bundles at $7.44 → $29.80 → $76.10. The verdict window is day 90.
  • The count-anchor is a day-90 instrument, not a day-1 instrument (0.08x at launch, 1.91x after 3 months). Every bundle in the plan is count-anchored anyway, because it costs nothing — but no projection in this document assumes a mega bundle earns in month one.
  • The keyword layer is genuinely strong and genuinely not what the direction is called. The exploitable demand is in botanical print set, floral gallery wall set, sage green wall art, vintage botanical print and flower market print. It is not in heritage, herbarium, herbar or audubon.
  • Two of the three named aesthetics are dead and have been excluded with numbers, and the third is barely average. The direction's value is the plate format, not the subject list.
  • IP risk is the lowest of any direction in the pack — CC0 and public-domain throughout, with a documented trap (Audubon's own digital files) that the plan routes around.
  • The structural vulnerability is a free archive, not a competitor shop. That is a different kind of risk from every other direction in the pack, and it is not solved by better art alone.

Three ways to read the same evidence, for the reconciliation agent:

  1. The lane reading. 0.79x, 42 shops, HHI 0.412, 0.63x at launch tenure, 70% dead tail. Direction 9 is the corpus average with a good keyword layer and a good IP position. That makes it a weak selection candidate and a strong hedge candidate.
  2. The shape reading. botanical + set at 2.57x/27.6% sold on 23 shops, 3.93x in the new-shop cohort, and a bundle premium that exists on day one ($7.44 vs $2.11 at 0–3 months). Direction 9 is not a lane at all; it is the bundle mechanic applied to a subject that is well-keyworded and IP-clean. Read that way it is close to a technique — and it should be read alongside directions/04-vintage-bundle-shop.md, which already runs the same mechanic on oil-painting subjects. The two are compatible and partially redundant, and the reconciliation agent should treat them as one mechanic in two aesthetics rather than as two candidates.
  3. The counter-reading. Strip LoveDictionary and NorthPrints and the lane is $17.88/listing at 2.51 u/l and 16.2% sold, i.e. 0.51x. Add the launch tenure and it is 0.63x. The positive case is then carried entirely by 13 listings in the new-shop cohort and 192 listings flagged as sets — and botanical + set is 2.57x because set-flagged listings are a minority of the catalogue with selection bias in what gets flagged. The 3.93x is a thirteen-listing number. That is the load-bearing fragility, stated at its true size.

What would change the reading, and it is cheap to find out. Two six-sets and one three-set, mocked in three households, published as listings #1, #3 and #5 with $3/day of ads on botanical print set and floral gallery wall set only. That is 18 plates, about 11 hours of design, and $90 of spend. It tests the bundle advantage at launch tenure, the keyword layer, and the Redouté price anchor simultaneously — and it answers the question this document cannot: whether 2.57x survives in a catalogue with zero reviews, against a $3.15 incumbent, on a page where a third of the slots are paid. No other direction in the pack can be tested that cheaply, and this one is also the only one where the test could come back negative for reasons that are not about the product.

Material not used, and why. The two mature shops that carry the lane — LoveDictionary's $2,063.81 hero and NorthPrints' 73-month $5.66 book — are excluded from every projection in §10.1, which is why those numbers are lower than a naive read of the same cells. Mushroom, pressed-flower and the Audubon name are excluded from the lane's spine on measured grounds and retained only as specimens inside sets. And no figure in this document is derived from a licensed-IP listing, consistent with the house rule.

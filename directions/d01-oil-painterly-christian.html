1. The direction in one paragraph#

A single-aesthetic shop that draws named Gospel scenes and modern-symbolic scripture as warm-neutral oil paintings with a human figure in them, sold as instant digital downloads, priced in Alfie's digital ladder and designed bundle-backward so that ten singles become four sets of three, one set of nine and eventually one whole-shop bundle. The lane is faith/christian, the aesthetic is painterly/oil, the shape is single-first-then-set. The buyer is a woman in a new house or a freshly-converted Christian with one wall and a specific scene in mind — not a gallery wall filler and not a scripture-typographer. The differentiator is not the style (the style is table stakes in oil painting wall art print, 2,160 volume / 336,853 competition) — it is the named scene. The corpus says plainly that one named scene sells and the same painting under a different name does not: Jesus Leaves The 99 appears as a hero in three separate shops in this corpus and as the top-gaining 4-gram in this direction. The strategic claim of this direction is that subject specificity, not price, is the lever, and that the price ladder should be built underneath it rather than used to escape it.

The discount convention is the resolution of the price argument. The page-one list median is $35.99 and the buy-box median is $10.50. Sellers in this lane list high and run a permanent ~32% strike-through. That is exactly the "September discount" Alfie prescribes ("accept ~5% less profit so products sit high on 1 October") and it is already the ambient habit of the lane. Listing at $7.99 and discounting 20% to $6.39 is below the page median and reads as cheap on the card; listing at $22.05 and discounting 32% to $14.97 is above it and reads as expensive. The buy-box is what the buyer sees; the list is what the incumbent's card is advertising.

Sharda's number, which governs the whole structure: 99% of buyers do not read the description; they rely on the listing photos. So the description does exactly three jobs and no more.

Block 1 — what they get (above the fold, 3 lines). Exact file count, exact resolution, exact ratio list. This is the digital-specific clarification infographic rule from alfie_deep.md §5.

7.2 The rules#

Palette. Warm neutral ground, one cool accent per composition, brown as the anchor. This is directly forecast-supported and it costs nothing to execute: - Ground: aged cream #EFE7DA, oat #DED2BF, warm parchment #E7DCC8 - Anchor: raw umber #6B5335, burnt sienna #8A5A3B, chocolate brown #4A3728 (1stDibs 2026: chocolate brown is the leading designer colour at 33% of respondents, "almost double" 2022; Benjamin Moore's 2026 colour of the year is a burnt umber) - Cool accent, one per piece: glacier blue #A8BFC9 (Pinterest Cool Blue +50%), celadon #B4C4B0, cornflower #7A93B8 (31% of designers next year per 1stDibs), mossy olive #7A7F63 (Behr's 2027 pick) - Forbidden: dopamine saturation. Forbes, Jan 2026: "Why Designers Are Ditching Dopamine Decor for Warm Neutrals." The sector split is real — but the split favours the cocoon line because buyers arrive at prints cheap and a digital margin cannot support a maximalist production cost.

2027 sequencing, so the re-skin is free: 2026 is brown-over-white (Pantone Cloud Dancer, a "conscious statement of simplification" against cacophony). 2027 is blue-green everywhere — three of five named 2027 colours are blues, two are greens, none are brown. Build the brown/cream/sepia house now; a glacier-blue or mossy-olive capsule in 2027 is the same pipeline with a different swatch file.

Medium. Impasto-adjacent oil on a visible linen or gesso ground. Visible brush direction in the figure's clothing, softened edges in the background, no hard black outlines anywhere, no digital-airbrush gradients. Alfie: Midjourney is the wall-art generator because "people are expecting the paintings feeling." Kitl's "ultra realistic painterly style, warm palette, brush-stroke texture" is the same instruction. Deliberately heritage-styled rather than photoreal — external_trends.md §2.4 documents WIRED and Marketplace covering the seller exodus over mass-produced listings, and §3.3 concludes that a stylised historical illustration language is a defensive position in that environment, not just an aesthetic preference.

Light. One directional source per piece, low and warm (dawn, dusk, or a single window). No flat even lighting. No rim light. The corpus's best performers are all dawn or dusk pieces — Three Crosses in the Light, He Is Risen, New Mercies Each Morning (Lamentations 3:22 sunrise), Empty Tomb, The Empty Tomb, garden light.

Composition — the three rules that produce the collection effect.

  1. The figure is never the whole canvas. The WhereFaithIs top ten are all scene with figure at one-third or less: a woman at a hem, a woman at a well, a shepherd on a ridge, a figure on water. Amazon-style face-forward portrait is not this lane's product. Use over-the-shoulder, three-quarter-rear, or distant-figure compositions. This is also what makes the set look like a set.
  2. Ground the figure in a landscape with a horizon in the upper third. First-century Galilee: olive terraces, stone walls, wild grass, water. This is what TheMossyPrints and CedarPrintHouse are both doing and it is what ties a Jesus scene to a "Tree Planted by Water" still life in the same collection.
  3. Textured ground, never clean white. Cream/aged-paper ground with visible linen tooth, a soft dark edge (the suggestion of an old panel), and 2–4% canvas texture. This is the cheapest available answer to external_trends.md §1.3's warning: a downloadable print cannot physically age, so it must read as a found object.

Scale rules for 100+ designs without drift. One palette file. One light rule. One composition rule. One horizon treatment. Four subject families (Gospel scenes · parables and allegories · modern symbolic still life · Old Testament), each with its own subject grammar. When a design breaks a rule it does not get published; when a subject family has produced 20 pieces it stops and the next one starts. Aesthetic drift is the failure mode of this lane specifically — 6,541 of 40,275 corpus listings sit in unclassified because their titles describe a subject and never a style, and the uncommitted shops earn $144.97/listing against $523.61 for named vintage-oil and $668.55 for named maximalist.

8. Production plan#

8.1 The pipeline, step by step#

  1. Prompt in Midjourney (v7 or later) with a fixed style block appended to every prompt, so the style cannot drift between designers. The style block: warm neutral oil painting, visible linen canvas ground, single low warm light source, muted cream/umber/ochre palette with one cool accent, softened background edges, restrained brushwork, no outlines, no text, no signature, period-appropriate first-century Galilee setting, 2:3 vertical composition. --ar 2:3 --style raw --stylize 250 --no text, signature, watermark, frame, border.
  2. Vary the seed, not the style block. Non-negotiable. The 70/30 rule and the aesthetic-lock rule are the same rule.
  3. Select 1 in 6–8. Alfie's 20-minute rule: if a concept is not right in 20 minutes, the concept is wrong — ship it anyway and move on.
  4. Upscale ×2 and clean. 300 DPI at 2:3 is a 2,400×3,600 minimum; 3,600×5,400 is the working master. Remove artefacts at this stage, not in the mockup.
  5. Ratio exports from the master. Jason Famularo's constraint, from the vault: digital wall-art sizes are dictated by the source file's pixel dimensions and aspect ratio. Generate at the target aspect. Do not crop a 2:3 master into 16:9 — that is the single most common production error in this vertical and it shows.
  6. Colour-ground pass. Cream/umber glaze + 2–4% canvas texture overlay. This is the "reads as a found object" step and it is 5 minutes per file.
  7. The art is finished with no text on it. Alfie's caution is exact: "No text" is right for the art and must not become licence for typographic clutter on the listing images. Four lines of spec text on a thumbnail was called out as self-sabotage.
  8. Mockup in the house style (below). 80% of the effort budget goes into the thumbnail.
  9. Deliverable: master TIFF/PNG + 9 ratio exports + a buyer PDF showing each size in a real frame. Nothing else ships.

8.2 The mockup set#

Three groups, then cut to three. Alfie's narrowing rule: group mockups (light/dark), measure CTR and sales per group, and if no pattern emerges cut to exactly three — A, B, C — and test every future product on them. From day one: A (warm cream wall, oak frame, low side table), B (mid-tone clay wall, black frame, mantelpiece — the wall art on a mantelpiece default from the Infographics masterclass), C (dim evening room, candle-lit, brass frame).

  • Do not repeat the same mockup across listings (Sharda, the wall-art specialist). Vary it: living room, bedroom, far, close, a hand holding it, on a table, small frame to large frame.
  • Match the household to the art. Dark moody art in a bright Scandinavian room is the failure Alfie names. The cream/oat palette of §7.2 makes this easy — the art and the room agree by default.
  • Q4 overlay, live from day 1. From 1 September, Q4-specific mockups — a blurred Christmas tree in the background, even on non-Christmas products — is how a new shop beats a ranked shop, because 4–5-year-old shops are losing on presentation that has not moved. Since the launch date is after 1 September, this applies to the whole first catalogue.
  • Hands in mockups convert better (community poll). Use on the hero of each set.
  • The image sequence, fixed across all 16 listings: thumbnail (80% of the effort) → lifestyle in context → clarification (what files, what sizes) → evaluation (does it fit me — a person in a real room, not a product shot) → risk/reassurance last → how to order. One idea per card, scannable in seconds, designed for phone.
  • The funnel card. Every low-ticket listing carries the buy-2/buy-3 X%-off infographic plus a card funnelling to the whole-shop bundle. Etsy has no cross-sell tool; the infographic card is the cross-sell tool.

2. The direction's two terms have a combined monthly search volume of 2. painterly jesus art = 1. biblical art print digital download = 1. The direction is named after a null query and enters on a null query. The addressable surface is christian wall art digital download at 752 volume against 119,548 competing listings — a 0.0063 ratio, and the recon measured 36% ad share on that page.

3. The addressable page is a $10.50 median with 75% of listings discounted ~32%, and it is a catalogue-size race. VintageDiscoPrints holds slot #1 with 600+ prints at $12.82; ThatHipPrint runs 250 prints at $13.16; the median stated bundle on the top 20 is 10 prints; the median shop on those slots is 1,908 sales and 202 reviews. The bundle fix is necessary — and it means the first 30 days are a build with no money shape at all. Wave 1 is explicit that bundle-first launch is contradicted, hard: singles are the raw material, the bundle is what you build in September to be ready for the peak, and a bundle with no catalogue has nothing to bundle.

The lane is a hero shop, and the corpus's clear instruction is that a new shop should not expect to guess the hero.

Add the corpus's seasonal evidence: 123 nativity listings at 86.2% zero, a corpus-wide Christmas pool at 78.0% zero, and yw_analysis.md's finding that 1,437 Christmas listings produced $7,046 at 91.58% zero at median age 2 months. Hard kill — stop and re-cut, do not extend:

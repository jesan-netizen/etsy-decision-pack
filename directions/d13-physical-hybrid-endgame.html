13. The physical + digital hybrid endgame (Engine C/D)

A digital wall-art shop that, once it has a proven design library and a review base, adds a physical print-on-demand tier at $25–$107 per unit and a personalised hero that carries the AOV.

Compiled 2 Oct 2026 from the 73-shop combined corpus (/home/ubuntu/etsy_phase2/yw_listings.csv 26,429 rows + shops17_listings.csv 13,846 rows = 40,275 listing rows, 73 shops), the Wave 1 evidence files in /home/ubuntu/etsy_research/, a fresh live Etsy SERP capture taken 2 Oct 2026 for this document, and a fresh Everbee keyword pull 2 Oct 2026.

House rules observed: no listings created, no Kittl tokens spent, UK English, no verdict. The boss decides.

Classifier used here, stated once. There is no Everbee field that says "this listing is a physical print", so the physical cell is reconstructed:

  • DIG = title matches digital download | instant download | printable | svg | png | jpeg | high-res | resolution (case-insensitive).
  • B2B = title matches commercial licen | reseller | extended licen | unlimited | wholesale.
  • physical = Price (hidden) >= $25 AND not DIG AND not B2B.

That gives 1,457 listings = 3.62% of the corpus. The $25 line is not arbitrary: it is the price at which no digital-download product in this corpus can plausibly sit (the digital median is $5.99, p75 $9.59, and the highest non-B2B digital price in the corpus is $46.99 — a $44.30 digital mega-bundle, not a print). B2B is carved out because 13 of the 14 commercial licence for N physical wall art prints listings are one shop's wholesale tier and would otherwise inflate the physical yield tenfold. Both readings are reported in §2 so the reader can see the sensitivity.

Revenue everywhere is the gross sticker proxy = units × Price (hidden), before Etsy fees, before sale discounts, before COGS, before shipping. For the 17 master shops Est. Total Sales is lifetime; for the 56 young winners it is Est. Sales Last 6 Mo. §10.1 states where a merged number is misleading.

1. The direction in one paragraph#

A digital wall-art shop that, once it has a proven design library and a review base, adds a physical print-on-demand tier at $25–$107 per unit and a personalised hero that carries the AOV. The lane is not chosen — it is inherited from whatever the digital shop already sells, because the corpus says the physical tier is a format overlay, not a lane: only 12 of 73 shops run both formats meaningfully and 48 of 73 run no physical listing at all. The aesthetic is whatever the digital catalogue already commits to (RadialPosters is music/album with no named aesthetic at all and still earns $106.80/listing on its physical tier). The shape strategy is three-tier: a $5–8 digital singles tier for traffic and reviews, a $25–32 physical single tier for margin, and one personalised listing that does 23–68% of a shop's revenue on its own. The strategic claim of this direction is deliberately not "physical prints are a good business". It is: physical is where a digital shop's money goes once the digital shop has stopped growing in units, and the trigger to switch is measurable in advance (§2.7).

The graduation rule the corpus supports, stated as a rule:

A lane graduates when its physical yield is well above its digital yield and its physical share of listings is small. Nursery is the clean example: physical listings are 1.0% of its catalogue but carry 12.4% of its revenue at $591/listing against $47 for the lane. Vintage/retro is the anti-example: physical listings are 7.1% of a 10,805-listing catalogue and yield $18/listing — worse than a $5.98 median price can even explain. Vintage/retro does not graduate. It just gets a shelf.

The mechanic is arithmetic. Vintage/retro's physical price point is $44.89 against a $5.98 digital median — a 7.5× jump, which no buyer crosses for an interchangeable download of a thing they could get free anywhere. Nursery's physical point is $29.99 against a $10.45 digital median — a 2.9× jump, inside the range a buyer will pay to get a thing onto their wall.

Corollary, and this is the plan's central price rule: graduate at 2.5–3.5× the digital median, not at 7×. At $20–32 the corpus physical yield is $663/listing. At $44.89 it is $258. At $106.80 it is $99. The yield curve runs backwards above $32.

The trigger is month 12, and it is sharp. Under 12 months, 1 of 50 shops (2%) runs a physical tier that matters. At 12–18 months, 3 of 12 (25%) do — a 12× jump in rate. Beyond 36 months, 0 of 8 do, because by then the only physical books that exist are LoveDictionary's wholesale tier and AprilLaneArt's six listings.

The three shops that cross at 12–18 months are the three the plan models:

  • UnspirationalLiving, 15mo — physical = 60.0% of revenue, 51.2% of units, 130 physical listings at a $28.99 median, 249 listings total. This is the target shape.
  • RadialPosters, 16mo — physical = 56.9% of revenue but only 15.0% of units, 327 listings at $106.80. High ticket, low volume, one personalised hero.
  • PixelsToFramesCo, 18mo — physical = 50.9% of revenue on 11.0% of units, 131 listings at $56.09. The §2.2 finding applies: the $50–90 band has the best survival rate in the whole cell.

Three preconditions, all measurable before a supplier account is opened:

  1. A design library of ≥100 finished designs in ONE aesthetic, each production-ready at 300 DPI with the bleed already set. Not 100 listings — 100 designs. ArtPrintsbyCoco had 1,482 listings and failed at physical; CreaPawsCo had 20 and succeeded because all 20 were one product family.
  2. At least 50 shop reviews and a ≥4.0 median listing rating. Every page-one physical listing in §3 carries reviews, and this format has a physical entry barrier: a buyer committing $25–$83 to an unseen photo of their wall needs more reassurance than a $6 download. RadialPosters converted at 9.96; UnspirationalLiving at 1.83 with the group's highest review rate at 25.53. A shop at zero reviews cannot sell this format — that is the hard gate.
  3. A demonstrated personalised or hero listing already earning ≥$150/listing. The corpus says the plain physical tier is fragile (82.6% zero) while the personalised-physical cell is the reliable one ($980/listing, 60.3% zero). Graduate with a hero, not with a catalogue.

The negative precondition — the one that says wait. If the digital shop's velocity per month of trading is falling, do not graduate. vault_synthesis.md records NorthPrints −52%, LoveDictionary −31%, VintageGlow −35% off their March pace. Adding a physical tier to a decelerating digital shop converts a zero-COGS catalogue into a fulfilment business, and the failure mode is a warehouse of dead SKUs with shipping liabilities attached. Graduation is for shops whose digital tier has plateaued at a healthy level, not for shops whose digital tier is dying.

[Count + Noun phrase] · [Head term] · [Second head synonym] · [Aesthetic] · [Subject] · [Format]

Physical listings (§4.2 and §4.3) — format words move into the first five, because that is where the buyer's filter lives:

5.4 Description approach — physical listings need four things digital ones do not#

Per alfie_deep.md §5 and Sharda: "99% of buyers do not read the descriptions. They rely on the listing photos." The description is conversion support, not persuasion. Five blocks, in order:

  1. One line above the fold: what arrives and how. "A 16×20 in stretched canvas, 1.25 in depth, gallery-wrapped and ready to hang. Printed to order and shipped in 3–5 business days." This is the block that does the actual work, because the #1 physical-print support question is when will it arrive and do I need to frame it.
  2. Dimensions and materials. Exact inches and cm, canvas depth, mat and frame stock if framed, paper weight and finish if unframed. State plainly whether a frame is included — it is not, and saying so prevents the review that costs more than a month of sales.
  3. Colour and print note. "Printed to order; screen colours vary slightly from the mockup." This is the single highest-value disclaimer in the format and almost nobody writes it.
  4. Care and returns. "Spot clean only with a dry cloth. Unframed prints ship in a rigid mailer; canvases ship in a protective carton."
  5. Gift framing + review request. "Gift wrapping available — message us after purchase." Plus the review ask.

No keyword stuffing in the body. The description is not ranked. The $1,797 physical yield in §2.1 is not a keyword effect; it is a fulfilment-trust effect, and the description's job is to remove the three objections that stop a $79.99 order.

7. Concepts#

7.1 The aesthetic bible — oil painterly figurative Christian#

One aesthetic, many niches. The rule is absolute (alfie_deep.md §5, quoting the branding FAQ: "using random styles across listings" is mistake #1; "Boho + cyberpunk = confusing brand message"). The whole catalogue is Renaissance oil painting applied to scripture and landscape.

The aesthetic earns its place on numbers, not taste: directions_from_data.md §2 puts vintage / antique oil painting at 42 shops, 789 listings, $523.61 yield/listing, 66.5% zero — versus no clear aesthetic at 22,799 listings, $144.97/listing, 71.8% zero. Naming a real style is a 3.6× yield multiplier across the corpus.

Element Rule Anti-rule (what the dead shops did)
Palette 5–7 warm-shadow pigments: burnt umber #6B4A2F, raw sienna #A4682F, ochre #D19A3C, olive #6E7A4C, deep madder #8C2F39, linen #E8DCC4, sky slate #7C8B93. One saturated accent max per print, no more than 20% of the canvas. No pastels, no neon, no flat vector, no photographic saturation, no modern UI colour.
Medium Visible oil brushwork with a soft underpainting showing through. Canvas texture reads at 1:1. Craquelure on the vintage pieces, none on the modern ones. Not watercolour, not line art, not gouache-flat, not 3D render, not AI-photo.
Light Single directional key from upper-left at 35–45°, warm; a cool bounce fill at 15%. Deep shadow at 40–55% opacity, never black. Golden-hour warmth in landscapes, candle-warm interior in figures. No rim light, no studio three-point, no flat even fill, no neon glow.
Composition Rule-of-thirds with the subject off-centre. Horizon on the lower third for landscapes; figure at 0.62 height for portraits. Generous headroom so a canvas crops cleanly to 16:9. No symmetrical passport framing, no full-bleed collage, no vignette.
Type Never on the physical prints. The physical buyer is buying the painting, and type on a canvas reads as a poster. Type appears only on the digital typography sets (listing 6) and the custom verses (H1–H3). Never type on a physical print.
Border None. Print edge-to-edge, with the supplier's own bleed. Frames are the buyer's choice. Never a decorative border, never a mat printed into the file.

7.3 Series that scale to 100+ designs without style drift#

The drift risk at 100 designs is that "one aesthetic" quietly becomes "oil painting plus whatever sold last month". Four series, each with a fixed rule:

  • Series A — PARABLE (30 designs). One narrative per print, figure at 0.62 height, landscape behind. Rule: no print in this series is a portrait. It is a story, not a person.
  • Series B — PLACE (40 designs). No figure at all. Chapel, field, grove, lake, path. Rule: no print in this series contains a person or a building interior with a window.
  • Series C — DEVOTIONAL (20 designs). Objects only — candle, rosary, book, bread, wheat, flame. Chiaroscuro, heavy shadow. Rule: no landscape horizon above the lower third.
  • Series D — QUIET (20 designs). Abstract and botanical. Rule: maximum two pigment zones plus the accent. This is the Frame-TV and non-devotional-buyer series and it is where the digital ladder gets its reach.

Discipline: the four series share one palette file, one light direction, one horizon rule, one mockup set. A 100th design is a prompt variation, not a new style decision. Recolours are not new designs — the same painting at a different key is a variation, not a series entry.

7.4 The physical-specific constraint that changes the design work#

A physical print is not a digital file. Three design rules exist only because of the print, and they are non-negotiable:

  1. Bleed. 0.125 in on every edge. A print without bleed shows a white line when framed. Every mockup must be generated from the bled master, not the trim-size master.
  2. Crop safety for the supplier's aspect ratios. A 16:9 crop must still work — §7.1's "generous headroom" rule exists for this. When the supplier generates 2:3 and 16:20 from one master, the 16:9 crop is the one that will clip a figure's head.
  3. No fine detail below 0.5 pt at A3. A brushstroke that reads beautifully at 8×10 on screen becomes mud at 24×36 in print, and mud at 24×36 is what generates the one-star review. Design the physical hero at 24×36 first, then downscale — the reverse of the digital workflow, and the reverse is the mistake.

9.1 The gating rules (from alfie_deep.md §1)#

9.5 The kill rules, stated in advance with dates#

PHASE 1 — the digital shop (kill at day 60): KILL if (a) fewer than 10 organic sales shop-wide; or (b) fewer than 2 listings have sold more than 5 units; or (c) a mature ad set (10 listings, 14 days, ≥100 clicks) returns ROAS < 1.1 on every listing; or (d) the +250 mega bundle has zero sales and zero favourites after 45 days live.

PAUSE, do not kill, if revenue is positive but flat and the ROAS gate is unmet — that is a listing-count problem, not a thesis problem.

PHASE 2 — the graduation gate at month 12. DO NOT OPEN if any of: - fewer than 50 shop reviews; or - median listing rating below 4.0; or - fewer than 100 finished designs in one aesthetic; or - no existing listing earning ≥$150/listing; or - the shop's velocity per month of trading has declined more than 25% over the prior 3 months.

The fifth clause is the one that saves money. It is the negative precondition in §2.7 and it exists because graduation converts a zero-COGS catalogue into a fulfilment business. A decelerating shop must not graduate.

PHASE 3 — the physical tier (kill the physical tier at graduation + 90 days, revert to digital): KILL the physical tier if (a) fewer than 15 physical orders in 90 days; or (b) the physical ROAS is below 1.6 on every physical listing after 30 days of ad data; or (c) more than 2 refund/damage cases, or any review below 3.0 that mentions colour, printing or arrival; or (d) physical net after COGS is below 30% at the achieved price points.

Reverting means: delist P1–P20, stop the supplier spend, keep H1–H3 if they are profitable as digital customs. The digital catalogue is untouched throughout, which is the point of the staging.

Clause (c) is the clause that matters. §3.1 shows page one is won on images, and §8.2's mockup discipline exists because the physical buyer's objection is trust, and a single 1-star review on a $79.99 print costs more than a month of physical sales. RadialPosters converted at 9.96; UnspirationalLiving at 1.83 with the corpus's highest review rate at 25.53. The physical tier is a reputation business.

10. The case, weighed#

10.1 The strong case FOR, with numbers#

  1. The physical cell over-indexes revenue by 10.1×. At >= $50, 1.32% of listings carry 13.17% of corpus revenue at a $1,748 yield per listing — against $173 corpus-wide for non-personalised listings. That is the single largest yield multiple of any shape in the corpus, and the $1,748 is real: 533 listings, $931,911 of gross sticker.
  2. A physical sale is worth 3.49 digital sales, corpus-wide. $36.43 vs $10.45 per sale. In the individual hybrids the ratio runs 8.38× (PixelsToFramesCo), 7.51× (RadialPosters), 4.92× (FaithEverywhere), and the two exceptions are a wholesale shop and a photo-portrait shop, neither of which is a POD print business.
  3. The trigger is month 12 and it is sharp. Under 12 months, 1 of 50 shops (2%) runs a physical tier carrying ≥20% of revenue. At 12–18 months, 3 of 12 (25%) do. The three that cross are FaithEverywhere ($4,526/mo at 12 months, the highest revenue of any live shop in the master file), UnspirationalLiving (60.0% of revenue from physical at 15 months) and RadialPosters (56.9% at 16 months).
  4. The three shops that crossed are all live. None of the four dead shops appears among the seven shops with real physical revenue (§2.3, §2.8). ThePlumAtelier's ten physical listings earned $10,319 at $1,032/listing — a yield better than most live shops' physical books — and it died for entirely separate digital reasons (827 of 983 listings at one price point, 90.4% zero). In this corpus, graduation to physical did not kill anybody.
  5. The working band is identified, priced, and empty. The $20–32 band returns $663/listing on a 67% zero rate from 244 listings across the entire 73-shop corpus. The $50–90 band returns $295/listing on the best survival rate in the cell, 56% zero, from 165 listings. Meanwhile the $32–50 band — where ArtPrintsbyCoco put 708 listings — returns $258 at an 88% zero rate. The winner and the loser are $12 apart in price. That is a free, unambiguous decision.
  6. Personalisation is the strongest single mechanic in this document. 809 personalised listings — 2.01% of the corpus carrying 3.39% of revenue at $296/listing, with 62.2% zero against 72.8% for everything else. Filtered to personalised-and-physical (116 listings): $980/listing at 60.3% zero. RadialPosters' single "Choose Your Album" listing is 22.6% of a $52,750 shop and 44.6% of its units on 1.0% of its listings. CreaPawsCo does $1,822 per listing from 20 listings in 12 months, the best in the master file.
  7. Personalised heads are the only winnable keywords in the pull, and they are winnable on the live page. custom pet portrait 7,559/mo against 232,185 competing listings (1:31); pet portrait 7,687 against 559,086 (1:73); album poster 1,150 against 37,952 (1:33). And §3.2 found JennyPawsStudio at 255 sales / 68 reviews and CameoandCrest at 344 / 62 holding page one on those exact heads. Sub-400-review shops do hold physical page-one positions — on the personalised heads.
  8. The softest head in the entire capture is a format head. wall art canvas: 2 ads of 66 cards (3%), 98% free shipping, $81.00 median, 3,220/mo against 2,310,350. ready to hang art: 6%. Every other term in this project ran 18–37%. There is a paid-ad gap in the format heads, and it is at the price point with the best survival rate.
  9. The cost ladder is arithmetic, not opinion, and it is already solved. Printful landed costs are published: A3 matte $13.48, 24×36 matte $26.49, canvas 1.25in 16×20 $38.44. At the plan's prices: A3 at $32.99 nets 49.3%, canvas at $79.99 nets 42.1%, custom canvas at $99.99 nets ~50%. Framed SKUs are excluded on arithmetic (18×24 framed nets 20.0%). Under 12 hours of human time and a $4,000 cash float run the whole tier for a year.
  10. The tag discipline in the physical lane is effectively unclaimed. printed and shipped appears on 251 listings corpus-wide, 247 of them one shop's. Almost nobody marks their physical listings as physical in a way a filtering buyer can find. That edge costs one tag per listing.
  11. Physical buyers return less. Not a corpus number — this is the structural reason §2.4's 3.49× basket is profitable rather than merely large. A $5.50 digital sale costs nothing to serve and earns nothing to resell; a $27.99 print has a cost of goods but no support ticket, no re-download, and a materially lower return rate. The plan's 42% physical net against 89% digital net is the price of that, and it is still worth paying at 3.49× the basket.
  12. It composes with every other direction in this pack. Every direction 01–08 produces a design library. This direction is the monetisation layer on top of any of them, and it does not compete with them for the same design work — it needs the same 250 designs produced at 300 DPI with bleed. The marginal cost of graduating is ~15 minutes per SKU and one $50 sample order.

10.2 The honest case AGAINST, with numbers#

  1. The entire cell is 14 shops, 7 of which have ever earned anything at >= $50, and 48 of 73 run no physical listing at all. There is no market depth here. The >= $50 book is 546 listings across 14 shops and 7 shops carry 94% of its revenue, of which one shop (LoveDictionary) is a B2B wholesale tier, not a consumer print business. Strip B2B and 6 shops carry $931,911 — but strip the wholesale tier too and those 6 carry $110,376, which is 0.9% of the $7,073,661 corpus. That is not an industry, it is six shops and a texture.
  2. The cell's zero rate is 82.6% — the second-worst of any shape in the corpus. Compare: bundle-led 47.9%, gallery-wall sets 47.4%, college/dorm 47.3%, whole-shop bundles 33.8%. A physical print listing is more likely to have never sold than to have sold. At >= $50 it is 78.8%, and at the $90+ tier it is 92%.
  3. The yield curve runs backwards, so the best price point is the one the incumbents avoid. $20–32 → $663/listing. $44.89 → $258. $106.80 → $99. Any advice that pushes a shop toward "premium physical" is pushing it down a 6.7× yield cliff, and the corpus's only two premium-physical shops (RadialPosters 92% zero, BloomJetsetPrints $0 from eleven prints at an $80 median) are the two worst-performing physical books in the corpus.
  4. ArtPrintsbyCoco is the natural experiment and it failed. 1,482 listings, 44% of them physical at $44.94, 75.3% of its revenue nominally from physical, $11 yield per physical listing, 89% zero, $7,115 total physical revenue on 652 SKUs.   7. The margin haircut is real and it is 47 points. A $27.99 digital print nets 89.0% of price. The same $27.99 as an A3 physical print nets 42.0% — $11.76 — because $13.48 of it is Printful. Every physical sale in this plan earns roughly half what every digital sale earns. The direction only makes sense at 3.49× the basket and with a materially lower return rate, and the corpus does not measure return rate at all — so one of the two justifications is unevidenced. That is the single biggest hole in the FOR case and it is stated rather than buried.
  8. The working capital arrives before the cash. First 100 physical orders carry ~$2,500 of COGS and Etsy pays 2–4 weeks after the order. A $4,000 float is the entire requirement, and it must exist before the tier opens. If the boss's float is committed elsewhere, the correct decision is not to graduate, and no amount of yield data overrides that.
  9. The graduation gate has never been tested by a shop that met it. All three §2.7 crossing shops (UnspirationalLiving, RadialPosters, FaithEverywhere) crossed at 12–18 months without a stated gate — they simply added physical listings. There is zero corpus evidence that waiting for 100 designs and 50 reviews produces a better outcome than adding physical at month 6. The gate in this document is reasoned from the data, not read out of it, and it should be treated as a hypothesis with a stated confidence rather than as a finding.
  10. Above 36 months the rate of physical-tier shops goes to zero. 0 of 8 shops older than 36 months run a physical tier carrying ≥20% of revenue. Either the endgame is short-lived, or physical is not an endgame at all but a transitional format that gets replaced — and the corpus cannot distinguish those two readings because it has no shops older than 78 months.
  11. Nothing in the corpus measures a physical shop's operational failure rate. The four dead shops had no meaningful physical books, so §2.8's "physical did not kill anybody" is an absence of evidence, not evidence of absence. The genuine physical risks — colour mismatch, transit damage, supplier delays in peak season, a 3-star arrival review on a 4.8-rated shop — are invisible in this dataset and invisible in the master file too. The plan's §9.5 clause (c) is a guess at where the risk lives, derived from the format's structure, not from a shop that lost money to it.

The house rule is zero-IP, and the physical tier is the format most likely to violate it. The chosen lane (figurative scripture) is IP-safe; the music/album physical lane is not.

Do not buy the premium tier. $106.80 prints return $99/listing at 92% zero. $44.89 returns $258 at 88% zero. $27.99 returns $663 at 67% zero. Framed SKUs net 20.0%.

The honest summary of the trade: this is the highest-ceiling, latest-entry, thinnest-market direction in the pack. The mechanic is real and measurable — a physical sale is worth 3.49 digital sales, a personalised-physical listing is worth $980, the working price band returns $663 — and the timing is unusually well-attested at month 12. Against that: the market is six shops, the format cannot be launched into, the zero rate is 82.6%, the head terms are 1:4,163, the margin haircut is 47 points, the working capital must be committed before the cash arrives, and the corpus contains one natural experiment in this format (ArtPrintsbyCoco) which failed and whose master-file comment says the opposite.

The single most decision-relevant number in this document, and it is not the $1,797: at >= $50, 14 shops list, 7 have ever earned anything, and stripping the one B2B wholesale tier leaves 6 shops carrying $110,376 — 0.9% of the corpus's revenue. Whether that is a moat or a market is the question the boss has to answer, and this document does not answer it.

The second most decision-relevant number, because it is the one that could flip the decision: JennyPawsStudio, 255 sales and 68 reviews, held page one on custom pet portrait on 2 Oct 2026, and that term is 7,559/mo at 1:31 competition. If sub-100-review shops can hold a physical page-one position on a personalised head, then the personalised-physical cell is reachable without waiting 12 months or owning a design library of 100. If that position was one lucky listing rather than a shop, then the month-12 gate in §2.7 stands. That is a single re-check, and it is the cheapest decision-relevant test available in this entire project.

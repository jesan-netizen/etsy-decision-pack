8. Nursery prints (the LoveDictionary lane)

A digital-download print shop whose entire catalogue is nursery and kids' room wall art, built bundle-backward: design the 1,000-print mega bundle first, then extract themed mid-size sets from the same assets, then 3-and-6-print sets, and…

Compiled 2 Oct 2026 from the 73-shop combined corpus (/home/ubuntu/etsy_phase2/*.csv, 40,275 listing rows), the Wave 1 evidence files in /home/ubuntu/etsy_research/, and a fresh live Etsy SERP capture taken 2 Oct 2026 for this document (directions/_nursery_serp_raw2.json, _raw3.json, _shops.json).

House rules observed: no listings created, no Kittl tokens spent, UK English, no verdict. The boss decides.

Classifier used here, stated once: a listing is nursery if (a) in yw_listings.csv its Niche Targeting column equals nursery/kids, or (b) in shops17_listings.csv its title or any of its 13 tags matches nursery|baby|infant|toddler|newborn|playroom|crib (case-insensitive). boy/girl are not in the trigger — they appear in a quarter of the corpus's non-nursery listings and would over-capture. This gives 5,237 nursery listings across 64 of 73 shops. The Wave 1 stats_analysis.md §2.4 keyword classifier gives 5,265 — a 0.5% difference, so the lane boundary is stable.

Revenue everywhere is the gross sticker proxy = units × Price (hidden). It is before Etsy fees, before any standing sale discount, before COGS. For the 17 master shops Est. Total Sales is lifetime (no 6-month window in that file); for the 56 young winners it is the real Est. Sales Last 6 Mo. Merging the two is what the brief asks for, and §10 states plainly where a merged number is misleading.

1. The direction in one paragraph#

A digital-download print shop whose entire catalogue is nursery and kids' room wall art, built bundle-backward: design the 1,000-print mega bundle first, then extract themed mid-size sets from the same assets, then 3-and-6-print sets, and let singles exist only as discovery bait for the bundles. The lane is a named, describable need — a parent with a bare wall and a specific room to fill — which is why it is one of only three lanes in the corpus (with faith and gallery-wall sets) where the buyer can describe what they want rather than browsing a vibe. The aesthetic is the decision this direction must win, because the data is unusually clear: in the nursery lane the format (bundle vs single) and the price tier dominate, and the subject matter is second. The audience is expectant and new parents, plus the shower-gift buyer, and the two behave completely differently in the data — the room-decor buyer is the money, the gift buyer is not (§2.5). Evergreen, no season, no IP. Launch shape per the folder verdict: Engine A, bundle-led digital.

Against the two lanes a nursery shop would actually be compared to:

So the honest reading is: nursery is a mid-table lane on blended yield, well clear of the two volume lanes, an order of magnitude clear of the format that is actually winning money (multi-print gallery sets). It is not a bad lane. It is not the best lane. And §2.2 shows the gap is a format gap, not a lane gap.

2.7 The gift occasion — and a correction to the brief#

The brief's FOR case rests partly on "the gift occasion (baby shower) that never goes out of season." Everbee confirms the demand exists. The corpus says the money is elsewhere.

Everbee, 2 Oct 2026 pull: baby shower gift — volume 11,375/mo, competition 1,852,317. nursery wall art — volume 3,939, competition 1,530,182. kids room decor — 3,858 / 2,620,618. nursery wall print — 2,026 / 1,188,392. nursery prints — 1,982 / 1,136,327. playroom decor — 1,921 / 179,700. nursery gallery wall set — 870 / 22,565. gender neutral nursery decor — 865 / 48,056. cottagecore nursery — 650 / 38,146. gender neutral nursery prints — 638 / 31,749.

Everbee volume is not the constraint; competition is. The gift head has nearly 3× the volume of nursery wall art and 1.2× the competition. It is a wall of physical personalised banners, signs, needle-felt hoops, keepsake books, and framed prints — none of it the digital-download bundle this direction is about.

Both nursery over-indexed dead shops are the two with the worst zero rates in the entire nursery lane — 90.4% and 93.3%, against a 77.3% lane average. Neither failed because nursery is a bad lane. Both failed on a specific, identifiable mechanic:

  • ThePlumAtelier: 827 of 983 listings at exactly $10.45 — a single price point, 98.8% of the catalogue, median listing age 3 months at the sample point. It shipped 26 mega-bundles and 50 bundles and still closed, because 729 of its nursery listings were singles at $22/listing.

The live trap is the opposite of the one the brief names. The brief says "dead shops over-indexed on nursery single-price bets." The data refines that: the two dead nursery shops did not merely pick one price, they priced the whole room as singles at a commodity price. LinoaVintage had 39% bundle share — near MartyWallArt's 94%? No, near LoveDictionary's 21% — and its bundles still died, because a bundle of commodity singles at $10 is a worse buy than a set of six at $12.

3.4 Verdict on competition#

The nursery lane is hard to enter and hard to hold, and it is entered through a side door, not the front:

  • Front door (nursery wall art, 61/66 physical, $45 median): closed to digital, and closed to anyone without thousands of reviews.
  • Side door (digital-filtered queries, $12.00 median, 5% ad density, one 10-month shop at rank 4): open, and it is where a new shop should aim.
  • The mega-bundle is the profit object, not the ranking object. It is won from inside the shop by the mid-tier set, via the infographic-card ladder (alfie_deep.md §6: singles → themed packs $10–12 → mega $15–20 → whole shop $40+).

4. First products#

Designed bundle-backward per LoveDictionary's order of operations and Alfie's ladder. The mega bundle is designed first; every other listing is a subset of the same asset pool, so production is one art pipeline with fifteen cuts, not fifteen art projects.

Aesthetic commitment (one aesthetic, many niches — non-negotiable per alfie_deep.md §5): bold-colour maximalist nursery — the bold colour / maximalist / eclectic cluster that carries LoveDictionary's 21.1% style markers and 23.0% of the lane's revenue on 8.3% of its listings. Why these prices and this shape#

  • $12.99 sets, not $9.99.   • $17.99–$19.99 mid-mega, $29.99 mega, $39.99 whole shop maps Alfie's ladder exactly ($10–12 packs → $15–20 mega → $40+ whole shop).
  • Six sets of six, not one hero. 5. Copy#

5.1 Title formula#

[Count + Noun phrase] · [Subject + lane head term] · [Second lane synonym] · [Aesthetic] · [Occasion/room] · [Format]

Rules the corpus backs: - Obvious keyword in the first three words (alfie_deep.md/DIG §2), then layer. "Bundle of +1250 Nursery Prints" wins; "Beautiful Nursery Bundle for Baby Room" loses the head term. - Numbers first. Count-anchored titles are 5.8× per listing, 62.4% of units, 60.2% zero vs 81.4% — the single strongest title mechanic in this lane. - Pack in two head-term variants — nursery wall art (3,939/mo) and nursery prints/nursery wall print (~2,000/mo each) and kids room decor (3,858/mo). LoveDictionary's tags confirm the pattern: nursery wall art 397 uses, nursery prints 358, nursery wall print 358, playroom decor 356, kids room decor 343. - Keep it under ~140 characters so the count and the head term never truncate.

5.4 Description approach#

Per alfie_deep.md §5 and Sharda: "99% of buyers do not read the descriptions. They rely on the listing photos." So the description is a conversion-support asset, not a persuasion asset. Five blocks, in order:

  1. One line, above the fold: what the buyer gets and in what format. "1,200 nursery prints. 8 sizes each. Instant download after purchase."
  2. How to download — spelled out, because it is the single most common digital-download support ticket.
  3. What is in it — the five collections, with counts, so the 1,200 claim is auditable.
  4. How to use it — print sizes, paper recommendation, whether it works in a Samsung Frame, whether the frames are included (they are not, say so plainly).
  5. Gift framing + a service line — "prints are not physically shipped," and the review request.

No keyword stuffing in the body. The description is not ranked, and the corpus's own finding is that digital download + printable wall art travel as a pair — that is a tag signal, not a body signal.

7. Concepts#

7.1 The aesthetic bible — bold-colour maximalist nursery#

One aesthetic, many niches. The rule is absolute (alfie_deep.md §5 quoting the branding FAQ: "using random styles across listings" is mistake #1; "Boho + cyberpunk = confusing brand message"). The whole catalogue is circus-poster maximalism applied to nursery subjects.

Element Rule Anti-rule (the dead shops' mistake)
Palette 5–7 saturated flat colours per print. Primary: circus red #E23B2E, marigold #F5A623, teal #1B9AAA, leaf #4A7C3F, cream #F7F1E3, hot pink #E4559B, ink navy #22304A. Max 2 neutrals. No pastels. No beige/boho neutrals as the base. No all-monochrome sets.
Medium Folk-naive / flat vector with visible ink texture. Consistent line weight, 2–3px at print size. Not watercolour, not photographic, not painterly, not fine-line minimal. Those are different shops.
Light Flat, no cast shadow, no directional light source. Optional single offset drop shadow in ink navy at 8% opacity for the circus-poster lift. No modelling, no glow, no soft gradient lighting, no window light.
Composition One subject, centred, occupying 55–65% of the canvas. Consistent margin of 12% on every side so a mixed bundle still hangs as a grid. No vignettes, no busy collage backgrounds inside a single print, no off-centre subjects.
Striping Bold 4–6 stripe bands in the palette, on roughly 1 in 6 prints. This is the LoveDictionary signature. Never stripes plus florals plus a border plus a quote in one print.
Border Optional single 1.5px ink-navy rule, or a 4-stripe band. Nothing else. No ornate frames, no scalloped edges, no double borders.
Type None in the set-of-6 prints. Type appears only in listing 9 (name/stats) and 15 (affirmations), in one rounded slab face, never small. No quotes in animal prints. The corpus shows quote/text nursery at $83/listing vs $172 baseline — it drags the lane down.
Subject Animals, always, bold and stylised. Realistic-species or folk-naive, not cartoon-IP. No licensed characters. MartyWallArt's own master-file note: "Avoid IP samples like Spidey and His Friends."

The grid rule that makes the bundle work: every print in every set uses the same 12% margin and the same 5-colour minimum, so a 6-print set and a 150-print bundle both hang as coherent walls. This is the "match the mockup household to the art" doctrine from alfie_deep.md §5 applied to the asset, not just the mockup — and it is what separates a bundle from a folder of unrelated files.

7.3 Series that scale to 100+ designs without style drift#

The drift risk is that "one aesthetic" quietly becomes "several" at 100 designs. Three series, each with a fixed rule:

  • Series A — CIRCUS (40 designs). Every print carries a stripe band. Animals, fairground props, ticket-poster type borders. Rule: no print in this series omits the stripe.
  • Series B — WILD (40 designs). Every print is one animal, flat, no props, no ground line. Rule: one animal, one pose, no scene. This is the volume engine and the 1,200-print bundle's backbone.
  • Series C — GARDEN (20 designs). Bold flat botanicals, no animals. Rule: florals only, no foliage-only, no berries.

Discipline: the three series share one palette file, one margin rule, one line weight, one mockup set. A 100th design is a prompt variation, not a new style decision. Recolours (pink/navy/neutral) are not new designs — they are the same design at a different hue map, and they live as separate listings over the same files.

The 1,200-print count, honestly. LoveDictionary's +1250 is a 46-month-old, 1,205-listing shop. A launch cannot honestly claim 1,200 prints from 100 designs. The first bundle claims +120 across 5 ratios (= 600 files) and the title says so. The count climbs as the asset pool grows: 120 → 300 by month 3 → 600 by month 6. Claiming a number the file count does not support is how this direction gets a bad review, and reviews are the whole entry barrier (§3.2: rank 1 has 3.1k reviews; one bad review costs more than a month of bundle sales).

8. Production plan#

8.1 Pipeline#

Per alfie_deep.md §5 workflow: AI-generate → convert to layered PSD once (~$4 on Fiverr) → reuse forever with batch scripts. No Kittl tokens spent (hard rule); generation is via the image pipeline already provisioned.

Step Tool Batch Effort/listing Notes
1. Concept spec Written brief per design: subject, pose, palette hexes, one stripe or none — 5 min The palette file is fixed; this is a lookup, not a decision
2. Base generation Image model, flat folk-naive vector style, white ground, no shadow 20 prompts/run 2 min each Regenerate on failure, not edit
3. Vectorise mcp__kittl__kittl_vectorize_image is Kittl — not used, tokens not spent. Use a local vectoriser or hand-trace the simple shapes — 3 min These are deliberately simple shapes; vectorising is cheap by design
4. Palette enforce Script: snap every colour to the 7-hex palette file; reject any render with an off-palette colour 100/run 0.1 min each This is the anti-drift gate. Non-negotiable.
5. Margin + canvas Script: place on 8×10 / 8×12 / 4×6 / 5×7 / 11×14 at 300 DPI, 12% uniform margin 500/run 0.05 min each One script, five ratios, zero human time
6. PSD master Once, ~$4 Fiverr, then a QuickFlow batch script does all exports — once $4 × 1, not × 500
7. Mockups 3 mockup templates (§8.2) + a crib mockup 3/listing 4 min Never repeat the same mockup across listings
8. Listing build Title formula §5.1, 13-tag stack §5.3, description §5.4, 5-ratio variation set — 6 min Templated; the count and subject are the only variables

Total human time: ~20 min per listing, ~10 min of that batch-eligible. 20 listings = 6.7 hours of human time for a 1,200-file asset pool. 100 designs = ~33 hours total. This is the most production-efficient of any direction in the pack, and that is a real argument for it: the failure mode of the dead shops was not production cost, it was that nobody ever made a coherent wall.

8.2 The mockup set#

Per alfie_deep.md §5 (Sharda: vary it, don't repeat; Alfie: cut to three if no pattern emerges) and the Q4 overlay (blurred seasonal background on non-seasonal products, from 1 September):

  1. Mockup A — Crib wall, daylight, natural. A real white-crib against a cream wall, three prints hung asymmetrically. The hero mockup for every nursery listing. The document household must be the nursery.
  2. Mockup B — Small frame to large frame. A hand holding a 4×6 next to a hung 8×10. Answers "what size is this really" and converts.
  3. Mockup C — Close crop + room-wide pair. A tight detail of the texture, then the full wall. Answers "what will it look like hung."

Plus, on the bundle listings only, a gallery-grid mockup showing 9 or 25 of the actual prints in a grid — this is the single most important image for a count-anchored bundle, because it is the only proof the count is real. A blurred seasonal element (Christmas tree, spring blossom) sits in the background from 1 September on all listings, Christmas ones or not — that is the documented way a new shop beats a 4-year-old shop.

80% of the effort goes into image 1. No spec text on the thumbnail: no "watercolor, clip art, 300 DPI, HD quality" — alfie_deep.md §5 records that as self-sabotage, and nursery buyers are phone-scrolling at 11pm.

9. Launch simulation — the first 30 days#

9.1 The gating rules (from alfie_deep.md §1)#

Etsy Ads only open when: shop open a few weeks · ≥2 organic sales (5 ideal) · ≥25 listings ("non-negotiable"). Advertising before social proof tells Etsy the conversion rate is terrible. What gets advertised: only high buyer-intent signals — "if we pay for 10 clicks, at least one will buy." 10–20 listings max, never more than 3 pages, screened on favourites/sales/visits. Never advertise average performers. Avg CPC 55¢ gives the best ROAS; 71¢ buys visibility at a worse ROAS.

Implication for the 30-day plan: no ads in month 1 at all. With 20 listings, the ≥25-listing gate is not met until week 2 of month 2. The plan respects the gate.

9.5 The kill rule#

Stated in advance, with a date, so it cannot be rationalised later:

KILL at day 60 if: (a) fewer than 10 organic sales across the whole shop; or (b) fewer than 2 listings have sold more than 5 units; or (c) a fully-mature ad set (10 listings, 14 days, ≥100 clicks total) returns ROAS < 1.1 on every listing; or (d) the mega bundle (listing 1) has zero sales and zero favourites after 45 days of being live.

PAUSE (do not kill) if: revenue is positive but flat and the ROAS gate has not been met — that is a listing-count problem, not a thesis problem. Alfie's gate is ≥25 listings and ≥5 organic sales; below that, the data is not yet informative.

Clause (d) is the one that matters. If the hero bundle is dead at day 45, the format thesis is wrong for this shop, and everything else in the catalogue is feeding a product nobody wants.

10. The case, weighed#

10.1 The strong case FOR#

  1. The lane is genuinely large and the revenue is real. 5,237 listings, 64 shops, $900,535 gross, 67,363 unit-sales (12.9 per listing). It is the third-largest revenue lane in the merged corpus at 12.7% — behind only gallery-wall sets and vintage/retro.
  2. The median price of $9.50 is 58% above the corpus's $6.00. A nursery buyer pays more than a vintage-print buyer for the same file. This is not a commoditised market at the price level.
  3. It is the only lane with a proven young template. MartyWallArt: 109 listings, 95.6% nursery, 94% bundles, 66% zero rate, $182.97/listing on a real 6-month window at 5 months median listing age, 1,698 units. Every other "pure nursery" shop in the corpus is either 38–46 months old or dead. There is exactly one copyable young success, and it is small, tidy, and legible.
  4. The format effect inside the lane is enormous and unambiguous. Bundles $487.50/listing vs singles $99.90 (4.9×). Count-anchored titles $520 vs $89 (5.8×), 62.4% of units on 19.2% of catalogue, zero rate 60.2% vs 81.4%. Bundle-led shops earn a median $75/listing against $18 for shops with <20% bundle share.
  5. The price ladder is monotonic from $8 to $40. Rev/listing: $212 → $156 → $357 → $624 → $12,557. You do not have to guess which tier works.
  6. The aesthetic premium is documented and unclaimed. Bold-colour/maximalist/circus carries 23% of the lane's revenue on 8.3% of its listings at $478/listing — 3.7× the lane average. LoveDictionary's own style markers confirm it (21.1% bold colour/maximalist). The four dead shops had no aesthetic; this is the variable that distinguishes them from the winners.
  7. Everbee confirms real, unsuppressible demand, with findable low-competition pockets. nursery wall art 3,939/mo, kids room decor 3,858/mo, baby shower gift 11,375/mo, nursery wall print 2,026/mo. The digital-filtered SERP has only 5% ad density (3 of 66) and one 10-month-old shop with 63 reviews at rank 4 — the softest page one in the entire live recon across all ten queries.
  8. Production is the cheapest in the pack. ~20 min human time per listing, ~$4 total cash, a 100-design asset pool feeding 1,200+ files across 5 ratios, and zero COGS. The dead shops did not fail on cost. This direction can be built properly on a small machine.
  9. It is evergreen and IP-free. No season to prepare, no licence to expire, no takedown risk. The only evergreen risk is a decline in the lane, and §10.2 shows the lane's recent 6-month family ranking (nursery = 2,572 units, 5th of 16) is healthy, not collapsing.
  10. It survives the fee stack better than any other lane, because the ladder's top tiers are 88–89% take rate and the tail is deliberately small.

10.2 The honest case AGAINST#

  1. The lane's yield is below average, and the brief's 0.90× is confirmed. 0.98× the corpus blended average ($171.96 vs $176.18), 0.78× on units per listing (12.9 vs 16.6), and 5.4 points worse survival (77.3% zero vs 71.9%). It is significantly below the YW cohort average in the rank test (Cliff's delta −0.048, p < 0.001, stats_analysis.md §2.4).
  2. Nursery is not where the money is. Merged, gallery wall set is 40.79% of all corpus revenue from 3.30% of listings at $2,253/listing — 13× nursery. humour/funny is 5.51% of revenue on 2.0% of listings at $299. Nursery is third on revenue and thirteenth on efficiency (zero rate 78.23%, second-worst of the eleven lanes in yw_analysis.md §8.3).
  3. The whole lane rests on three shops. Top 3 = 84.3% of nursery revenue. In the $26+ tier, LoveDictionary alone is 84.5% and only 16 shops are involved at all. A median nursery shop earns $11/listing (young cohort). 14 of 64 nursery shops clear $6,000, against 51 of 73 for the corpus overall. The lane's headline numbers are three shops' numbers.
  4. The two pure-nursery shops that scaled are 38 and 46 months old. LoveDictionary: 46 months, 1,205 listings, nursery is only 14.2% of its revenue — it is a vintage/maximalist shop with a nursery side. StylingHerLife: 38 months, 376 listings, conversion rate 1.58, the second lowest in the master set. There is no evidence a pure nursery shop reaches scale quickly, because there is no example of one.
  5. The gift occasion is the weakest segment, not the strongest. The brief's FOR case leans on the baby shower. The corpus says otherwise: baby-shower/gift-tagged nursery listings earn $81/listing against a $172 baseline — 47% below — with 57% fewer units per listing. In the young cohort the 568 baby shower listings earn $28/listing at an 87% zero rate. Everbee: the head term's page one is median $8.07 digital — the cheapest digital median in the whole recon. gender neutral, the other growth story, is also below baseline at $144.
  6. The head terms are closed. nursery wall art page one is 61/66 physical at a $45 median. nursery decor runs 6,834/mo against 2,786,324 competing listings (1 : 408). The only digital pocket is the digital-filtered query at a $12.00 median, 62 of 66 cards digital — a dense page one owned by shops with 3.1k and 4.3k reviews and 33k–57k sales. A zero-review shop is not competing on the same page as these, it is not on the page at all.
  7. The two dead shops were pure nursery, and one was a bundle shop. ThePlumAtelier (835 nursery listings, 90.4% zero) and LinoaVintage (450 nursery listings, 93.3% zero) are the worst and second-worst zero rates in the entire nursery lane, and the lane average is already 77.3%. LinoaVintage had 39% bundle share and its bundles earned $26/listing against its singles' $55 — it did the bundle thing and still died, because the bundles were commodity singles. The lane's failure mode is not "bought the wrong format," it is "bought a format without a product."
  8. Correlation is not the mechanic. Bundle share correlates with revenue per listing at r = 0.149 across nursery shops with ≥20 listings. The 12 shops with ≥20% bundle share earn a median $75/listing; the 29 with less earn $18. But MartyWallArt is at $12.00 with 94% bundles and the two dead shops were at $10.45 and $10.04 with bundles. Bundle share is necessary and nowhere near sufficient — the plan needs the aesthetic to be real or this becomes LinoaVintage with better timing.
  9. MartyWallArt is the whole evidence base and it is one shop, 109 listings, with 33% of its units in two listings. Its top listing (Monster Truck, 263 units) and second (Rescue Dog, 173) are 26% of its units. Its master_shops_final.csv note also flags the IP exposure: "Avoid IP samples like Spidey and His Friends." It is a live, verified 30 Sep shop at 8 months — it may yet close. Building a direction on an 8-month-old, 109-listing shop with two listings carrying a quarter of its revenue is thin.
  10. No big digital shop in the corpus is still ramping. vault_synthesis.md: NorthPrints −52% off its March, LoveDictionary −31%, VintageGlow −35%. "Only the two concentrated young shops were." Nursery as a direction for a new shop is a bet that the lane holds while a new shop compounds — and the lane's own established operators are decelerating.
  11. The lane gets gamed down and the low bands are where the yield leaks. Under $12 holds 69.3% of nursery listings for 26.4% of the revenue; under $10 holds 49.9% of listings for 21.6%. The $5–8 band alone is 32.7% of the catalogue at $93/listing and the $8–12 band is 29.0% at $44/listing — against $674 at $16–20 and $5,114 at $30+. Faith's merged median has already collapsed to $3.45; the same mechanism is one aggressive shop away from nursery. ThePlumAtelier put 827 of 983 listings at $10.45 and LinoaVintage 450 at $10.04.
  12. A 1,200-print claim at launch is not honest. LoveDictionary's +1250 sits on 46 months of accumulation. Claiming it in month 1 is a bad-review generator, and reviews are the entire entry barrier.

10.4 Verdict material (no verdict — the boss decides)#

If the decision criterion is "highest expected revenue per hour of the boss's time": this direction wins on production cost (~$4 cash, ~33 hours to 100 designs) and on having the only proven young template in the corpus. Nothing else in the pack is this cheap to build properly.

If the decision criterion is "highest probability of hitting meaningful revenue": it loses to the gallery-wall-set lane, which is 40.79% of corpus revenue at $2,253/listing and has a cleaner 37.44% zero rate. It also loses to humour/funny (56.73% zero, $299/listing) on survival.

If the decision criterion is "what the data most clearly says to do": the data says bundles, and it says that within bundles, bold-colour maximalist nursery at $13–20 for mid-size sets and $27–34 for megas. The nursery lane is the third-best place to apply the bundle mechanic and the bundle mechanic is the corpus's one uncontested finding. What the data does not say is that a new shop can own it — 84.3% of the revenue sits with three shops, two of them old.

The honest summary of the trade: this is a direction where the format is proven and the lane is mid-table and the access is hard. The plan's bet is that a $13 set-of-six maximalist bundle, aimed at the 5%-ad-density digital pocket, aimed at long-tail terms at 1:11 to 1:59 competition rather than the 1:385 heads, can take a slice — and that bet is directly supported by the one shop (thelilysatelier, 63 reviews, 10 months, rank 4) that proves a small shop can hold a head-adjacent position in this lane. It is not supported by any example of a shop doing it twice, and 2 of the 4 shops that tried something similar are dead.

The single most decision-relevant number in this document: in the $26+ tier, LoveDictionary is 84.5% of $254,512 and only 16 shops are involved. The money tier of this lane is one shop's eight listings. Whether that is a moat or a market is the question the boss has to answer, and this document does not answer it.

0. The verdict on the contest (read this first)#

The lane is not open. It is occupied, and it is a price-war shell.

Wave 1's two headline numbers are real but are an artefact of a single shop's price point, not evidence of an open lane:

Wave 1 claim What the corpus actually shows

lg tv art at 267.66 units/use 64 listings, 4 shops, 17,130 units. 60 of 64 listings and 17,126 of 17,130 units (100.0%) are NorthPrints, at $2.12. The three other shops hold 4 units between them.
"no young shop uses these" False as written. BitterSweetDigitalCo and PureVintagePrints (both young) tag art for tv frame and lg tv art — and both sold zero on every one of those listings. The correct reading is the inverse: young shops do use them, and they die.

NorthPrints is not a signal; it is the noise floor. It books $139,167 of sticker revenue on 142 listings ($980/listing) — but it does it by having priced below the cost of running one Etsy ad impression against its own page-one competition. It is a strategy, not a market, and it is not reproducible by a new shop without also being willing to sell at $2.12 forever.

The answer to the brief's question — open, dead, or aesthetic-dependent? — is: aesthetic-dependent, and the aesthetic is the only lever that is not already owned.

Because frame-TV is not a subject lane, it is a format tag. It is applied on top of vintage, oil-painterly, nursery, faith and holiday art by the same shop that sells those things on wall art. So: the contested finding resolves as "aesthetic-dependent, and the two aesthetics with the best frame-TV economics are exactly the two the other direction docs argue for on independent grounds." Frame-TV is not a direction. It is a 16:9 export format that pays a premium on faith-typography and oil-painterly work and a penalty on watercolour line work.

1. The direction in one paragraph#

Lane: 16:9 digital art for the Samsung Frame / LG OLED "frame" TVs and generic TV screensavers. Aesthetic: the winner is oil-painterly figurative or scripture/typography — the two cells at 33.2% and 36.1% sold, versus 16.2% for the watercolour cell. Audience: a Frame-TV owner who already owns the hardware and wants a rotating wall of art, not a print. That buyer is seasonal (Q4), trend-sensitive (searches "halloween frame tv art" in September), and buys a bundle far more readily than a single — bundle listings are 5.9% of ex-NorthPrints frame-TV supply and take 12.2% of its units, at 33.4% sold vs 22.4% for singles, median $9.65 vs $5.01. Shape strategy: bundle-backward. Build 120–200 individual 16:9 designs, publish them in seasonal bundles of 30–120 at $12.99–$15.99, and let the singles be the depth that makes the bundle credible. A single frame-TV listing priced at the $5.01 lane median is a 22.4%-alive, 2.58-units-per-listing proposition; a $13 bundle at 33.4% sold and 5.59 units per listing is a different business.

2. The market case from the 73-shop corpus#

2.1 Who owns it#

33 of 73 shops carry at least one frame-TV tag. 23 of 56 young shops and 10 of 17 masters. It is not an open lane — it is a widely-tried, widely-failed lane. Of the 23 young shops that tried it:

  • Zero frame-TV revenue: 2 of 23 (thelilysatelier — 43 listings, 0 units; ColorsAtelierStudio — 32 listings, 0 units). DigitalStudioArtUA adds a third at 11 listings / 0 units.
  • Catastrophically dead despite huge catalogues: LuxFrameStudio 1,306 frame-TV listings, 485 units, 83.7% dead, $2,845 total; PrintandGatherStudio 362 / 270 / 98.6% dead; NorthViviPrints 332 / 439 / 96.7% dead; WeHeartCozy 395 / 774 / 97.5% dead; ChristArtCrafts 262 / 869 / 96.9% dead; LumioraPrints 246 / 1,301 / 87.8% dead; PureVintagePrints 132 / 310 / 91.7% dead; BitterSweetDigitalCo 99 / 419 / 92.9% dead.
  • Genuinely working: VelvetMysticArt 95 / 1,061 / 42.1% dead / 11.17 u/listing; ArgenteStudio 103 / 704 / 6.83 u/listing; PixelsToFramesCo 458 / 3,128 / 46.3% dead / 6.83 u/listing; LumioraPrints on the bundle rung only; ExaFrameStudio 206 / 856 / 37.4% dead — the best dead-rate in the lane; TheMossyPrints 71 / 323 / 40.8% dead / 4.55 u/listing.

Read that list honestly. Nine of the eleven largest young frame-TV catalogues have a dead rate above 82%. The lane punishes volume. LuxFrameStudio ran 1,306 listings into this tag and produced $2,845 of sticker revenue over its whole life — about the revenue of one good bundle from LumioraPrints.

Three things fall out, and two of them are warnings.

One. The digital page-one median is $3.18–$3.67. Not $5.01, not $9.65. A new shop listing a $12.99 bundle is competing against a page whose floor is $1.63. There is a genuine price-quality arbitrage on the page — nothing above $7.99 in the frame tv art digital set is 1-of-a-million-rated, and the bundles at $7.99 ("200+ Vintage Frame TV Art Bundle") and $18.00 ("samsung frame tv art set of 800") both hold page-one slots. But the median is $3.42 and the median is what the algorithm is optimising for.

Two. art for tv frame is a trap query, and it is the exact query Wave 1's 542.90 number came from. Its page one is 72% physical, with a physical median of $164.91 — gilded gold TV bezels, walnut frames, ornate decorative composite surrounds at $152.25–$267.75. Only 10 of 36 organic slots are digital art. The phrase reads as "art for a TV frame" (a picture frame) and Etsy resolves it to TV frames (bezels). The 10 digital slots that do appear sit at a $3.94 median. If you build a listing around art for tv frame as the hero, you are bidding for a query whose page is hardware. This is the mechanism by which the Wave 1 signal was a mirage: the tag is not a demand signal for art, it is a demand signal for gold bezels, and NorthPrints was selling art into it at $2.12.

Three. 39% of organic page-one cards carry a Bestseller badge on the head term, 43% on tv art digital download. A zero-review shop is not competing on merit.

Publishing 4 bundles means entering the 42.4%-sold band with 160 units of production, and the singles exist only to make the bundle's count claim true.

5.3 Description approach#

Four blocks, in this order, all of it short:

  4. One line on what is not included. "Digital files only. No frame, no print, no physical item shipped."

7. Concepts#

7.1 Aesthetic bible#

The look is "old master, wall-lit." Not photoreal, not illustration, not minimal-line. The corpus's own tag-frequency read on the live frame-TV listings: samsung frame tv art 38%, samsung frame tv 28%, frame tv art 25%, digital download 12%, samsung frame art 12%, vintage frame tv art 9%, frame tv screensaver 8%, spring frame tv art 7%, christmas frame tv 7%, landscape painting 5%, cottagecore decor 5%, american flag art 5%, wildflower tv art 3%, vintage oil painting 3%, christian tv art 3%. Vintage + landscape + oil + cottagecore, with a scripture sub-track. That is the vocabulary.

  • Palette. Ground: raw linen / bone / unbleached canvas (#EDE4D3 → #D8CBB4). Mid: umber, ochre, olive, dusty sienna (#6B5744, #B08540, #7A7A4F, #A85C32). One cool accent per piece, never two — slate blue, sea green, or ash rose. Highlights: warm white, never pure white. Max 5 hues per design.
  • Medium. Oil on canvas or panel. Visible weave in the ground. Loaded brush in the subject, thin scumble in the sky, a single crisp accent (a window, a flame, a rim-light on a leaf) to hold the eye at TV viewing distance. Softened background edges, no outlines, no text in the painting, no signature, no border, no frame mockup baked into the file.
  • Light. One source, low and warm (a window at 4pm, a candle, a lamp). Chiaroscuro permitted but never Caravaggio-dark — the TV is often the brightest thing in the room and a black rectangle kills the illusion.
  • Composition rules for 16:9 (non-negotiable, and this is the part every frame-TV seller gets wrong).
  • Horizon on the lower third at most; 60% of the frame is sky or negative space. The Frame TV sits at eye level in a bright room — a busy centre composition reads as noise.
  • The safe area is the centre 80% × 80%. Samsung's bezel crops roughly 4% per edge in Art mode, and the matte anti-glare panel kills fine detail at the extreme edges. Nothing that matters may live outside it.
  • Never place a face or a text block in the outer 10%. The 16:9 crop from a 2:3 or 1:1 source is where most shops lose their subject.
  • Focal point at or slightly above the vertical centre. Rule-of-thirds horizontal placement.
  • One subject per design. Two maximum, and never two equal-weight subjects.
  • The scripture sub-track is a different medium with different rules: hand-lettered or old-style-serif type, max 7 words, on the linen ground at 40% of frame height, centred, generous margin, no decorative flourish. Typography × frame-TV is the highest rev/listing cell in the corpus at $75.64 on 122 listings — but it is only 122 listings, so it is a proven-but-thin cell, not a proven-large one.

7.3 Series that scale past 100 designs without style drift#

The scaling risk is the medium, not the subject. Four series, each with a fixed style block that never changes:

  1. The Seasons Cycle (40 designs) — the same 8 compositions rendered in 4 seasons, 4 times-of-day. Produces the Q4 bundle and the evergreen bundle from one generator run.
  2. The Scripture Set (24 designs) — 24 verses, one fixed lettering style, one fixed ground.
  3. The Old Masters Landscapes (48 designs) — 12 compositional templates × 4 palette variants. This is the NorthPrints play at a defensible price, and it is the bulk of the 60-design B-01.
  4. The Botanical Studies (30 designs) — one species per design, consistent 3/4 lighting, consistent bone ground.

142 designs total, four style blocks, zero drift risk, because each block is a fixed prompt suffix and the composition templates are reused rather than re-invented. That is enough to make "60+" and "40+" in the bundle titles true, which is the whole credibility mechanism at this price point.

8. Production plan#

  • Generation. One text-to-image model, one locked style block per series, appended verbatim to every prompt. Style block for the Old Masters Landscapes: oil painting on linen canvas, visible woven ground, single low warm window light from the left, muted cream umber ochre olive palette with one cool slate accent, softened background edges, loaded brush in the subject, thin scumble in the sky, no outlines, no text, no signature, no border, no frame, 16:9 landscape composition with the horizon on the lower third and the centre 80% safe area clear of detail, --ar 16:9 --style raw --stylize 250. --no text, signature, watermark, frame, border, border.
  • Ratio files. Master at 3840×2160 (4K 16:9, the TV's native panel size — this is the buyer's actual concern and it is the whole justification for pricing over $3.42). From each master export: 2560×1440, 1920×1080, 4:3 (1440×1080), 1:1 (1080×1080), 2:3 (1440×2160) and 3:4. Six files per design, one master.
  • Upscaling only where the native render comes back below 3840px wide. Batch through a single upscale call; the whole 142-design set should not need more than ~30 upscales if the 16:9 renders are native.
  • Colour. One master LUT applied to every export so the set reads as one collection in a slideshow. A buyer flipping through 40 images with four different whitespaces is a bad review waiting to happen.
  • Bundle assembly. Folders named B01_OldMasters_60/, B02_Core_30/, B03_Christmas_40/, B04_Summer_24/, with a _README.txt per folder naming the TV upload path. Compression: zip each bundle once, keep the singles loose.
  • Mockup set. The critical difference from a wall-art shop: mock the art on a screen, not in a frame. A 16:9 image shown inside an oak picture frame tells the buyer the product is wrong. Three mockups, and they are all screens:
  • M-A — the art filling a Frame TV screen, shot in a mid-tone living room in daylight, room visible at the edges.
  • M-B — the art on a wall-mounted TV in a dim evening room, warm lamp, so the buyer can see it glowing.
  • C-S — the TV's own Art-mode UI framing the image: filename, the slideshow thumbnail strip, the matte anti-glare texture. This is the one no competitor in the recon set appears to be running, and it is the single most persuasive image available because it shows the buyer the exact screen they will see.
  • Vary across listings per the masterclass rule: living room, bedroom, kitchen nook, close crop, hand holding a phone showing the upload.
  • Effort per listing. Generation 4–8 min per design (1 pass + 2 rerolls); exports and LUT batch, ~2 min per design amortised; 6 exports ~1 min; mockup compositing ~15 min per listing (three mockups). A single: ~25 min. The 4 bundles: ~1 h each once the 142 masters exist. Total to a full launch set: roughly 50–60 hours, of which ~35 is generation.
  • The bundle count must be true. Listing "60+" with 41 files is the fastest way to a one-star review in a lane where the median organic listing already carries a Bestseller badge.

10. The cases#

10.1 The strong case FOR (with numbers)#

  1. The lane's best economics sit on its bundle rung, not its single rung. The ≥$9 bundle tier: 246 listings, 39.8% sold, 9.70 u/listing — against an ex-NorthPrints lane average of 23.1% sold and 2.76 u/listing, a 3.5× lift on units per listing. The $12–20 band alone runs 42.4% sold at 13.79 u/listing (85 listings, 15 shops) versus 8.4% sold in the $3.50–5.00 band that most of the lane's supply sits in — a 5× swing on alive rate across price rungs in the same keyword space. LumioraPrints books 36.2 units per listing on 20 bundle listings.
  2. 34.0% of ex-NorthPrints frame-TV revenue ($30,233) comes from 4.9% of its listings. The shape is not marginal, it is the centre of the money.
  3. Everbee hands you a 20× better competition ratio on the bundle+season terms than on the head term. christmas frame tv art bundle 196/1,872 = 0.105; frame tv art bundle holidays 158/1,463 = 0.108; fall frame tv art bundle 90/890 = 0.101 — against frame tv art 842/183,611 = 0.0046. And art for tv frames (plural) is 573/2,710 = 0.211, the best ratio of any term in the direction. These are real, rankable, and thinly occupied: frame tv art bundle appears on 69 listings across 15 of the 73 shops in 40,275 — 0.17% of the corpus — and christmas frame tv art bundle appears on zero.
  4. The dedicated specialists are small and beatable. FrameTVAtelier 30 reviews / 604 sales, DuskGlaze 21 / 862, CuratedScreen 12 / 221, matineesociety 1 / 17 — all four hold page-one slots on the head terms. The 4,000–6,300-review shops in the SERP are broad catalogue shops with a frame-TV wing, not specialists. The frame-TV SERP is not defended by frame-TV incumbents.
  5. The 16 singles and their 2:3/1:1 crops are free inventory for the wall-art lane, which is where the corpus yardstick lives — $179.17/listing versus $17.66. One generation run feeds both lanes. That is a real hedge and it is why the downside of this direction is bounded: the art is not wasted if the TV frame is a dead end.
  6. Two aesthetic cells inside frame-TV beat the lane average decisively, and both are independently supported elsewhere in the corpus: oil-painterly at 33.2% sold / $24.21/listing and typography/scripture at 36.1% sold / $75.64/listing — versus the lane's 23.1% / $17.66 and the watercolour cell's catastrophic 16.2% / $13.03.

  1. The lane is not open. It is one of the most widely-attempted and most widely-failed lanes in the corpus. 33 of 73 shops use frame-TV tags, including 23 of 56 young shops. This direction is not a discovery; it is something most of the young corpus has already tried.
  2. Nine of the eleven largest young frame-TV catalogues have a dead rate above 82%. LuxFrameStudio: 1,306 frame-TV listings, 485 lifetime units, $2,845 total. WeHeartCozy: 395 listings, 97.5% dead. PrintandGatherStudio: 362, 98.6% dead. NorthViviPrints: 332, 96.7% dead. ChristArtCrafts: 262, 96.9% dead. Three young shops have zero frame-TV revenue across 86 listings between them.
  3. The headline Wave 1 numbers were a $2.12 artefact and are now disproven. art for tv frame at 542.90 units/use is 99.8% one shop's; lg tv art at 267.66 is 100.0% one shop's. Everbee puts lg tv art at volume 162 — a 162-a-month market that one shop has already taken, at a price no new shop can survive. Anyone acting on the Wave 1 reading would have built a business on a rounding error.
  4. The head terms are unrankable and the SERP is a hardware page. frame tv art vol 842 / competition 183,611. art for tv frame page one is 72% physical gold bezels at a $164.91 median. 39% of organic page-one cards on the head term carry a Bestseller badge. The three shops with 4,000–6,300 reviews sit on it.
  5. The digital page-one median is $3.18–$3.67 and the floor is $1.63. You are proposing to sell at $12.99–$15.99 on a page whose median is under $4. That is either a real arbitrage (see FOR #1) or an unbridgeable price gap, and the corpus cannot tell you which, because the only shops at $12–20 in this lane are the 21 that already run bundles, and they run them with 200–1,100 designs of depth.
  6. The densest price band is a swamp and a new shop lands in it by default. 1,192 listings at $3.50–5.00 sell at 8.4% — the worst band in the lane, worse than the $0–2.50 rung, in a corpus whose overall dead rate is 70.2%. Copying the $5.01 lane median is the single most likely mistake available here.
  7. A bundle is not a product, it is an inventory commitment. "60+" means 60 finished designs, in one coherent style, exported at 6 ratios, with a colour-matched LUT. That is 50–60 hours before a single listing goes live, and the count claim is checkable by the buyer. The competing LumioraPrints bundle is 1,100 designs. A 60-design bundle is not a smaller version of that product; it is a weaker one.
  8. Frame-TV demand is seasonal and the supply is not. 45.6% of ex-NorthPrints frame-TV listings are Q4-tagged and they sell at 17.8% — the worst band. Seasonal-only listings are the worst shape in the lane at 15.4% sold and $11.89/listing.   9. Direction 6's finding is confirmed here, not refuted. The watercolour/minimal/line-art × frame-TV cell measures 16.2% sold, $13.03/listing on 530 listings — the largest and worst aesthetic cell in the lane. If the art in this direction is line art, this direction is dead on arrival. It only works with oil-painterly or scripture work, which means it is not an independent direction: it is a format export bolted onto Direction 1 or Direction 2, and it should be scored as part of them, not against them.
  10. The measurable ceiling is low. Even a strong young shop's frame-TV revenue per listing has a corpus median of $9.39 across 23 young shops (max VelvetMysticArt $63.02, min $0.00). The 30-day simulation's realistic good outcome is $160–$300. That is a rounding error against a business that has to reach meaningful monthly revenue, and it is 30 days of work for a number that will not compound on its own.

10.4 Verdict material (no verdict — the boss decides)#

What the evidence settles: - The Wave 1 "open lane" reading is wrong, and specifically wrong in a way that is now fully explained: 542.90 and 267.66 units/use are one $2.12 shop's tag-stuffing, art for tv frame is a bezel query, and lg tv art is a 162-volume market. - The lane is occupied, and occupied badly — 23 of 56 young shops have tried it, most at 82%+ dead rates. - The lane is not uniformly dead — the ≥$9 bundle rung runs at 39.8% sold and 9.70 u/listing, and carries 34.0% of the lane's ex-whale revenue from 4.9% of its listings. - Direction 6 is confirmed, not contradicted: watercolour × frame-TV is 16.2% sold, $13.03/listing, 530 listings. - Direction 1 is confirmed and then some: samsung frame tv art at 0.39× lift inside the faith lane is correct as a single-listing tag and, on this evidence, incorrect as a sub-lane — the same tags on a scripture/typography listing put out $75.64/listing.

The honest characterisation: frame-TV art is not a direction. It is a 16:9 export format with a bundle-shaped product strategy, worth 0.4–1.2× what the same art earns as a wall print, and worth more in exactly two aesthetics (oil-painterly, scripture) that other direction docs already argue for on independent, larger evidence. Scored as a standalone direction it ranks below every lane in directions_from_data.md on yield ($17.66/listing vs a $179.17 yardstick, against gallery wall / maximalist set at $2,456.46 and botanical / floral at $366.88 — both of those comparator cells are lifetime-basis and both are flagged as such elsewhere in the pack: $366.88 is the botanical lane yield that its own doc identifies as a two-shop artefact (6-month basis: $17.00/listing, 0.79x the yardstick, falling to $10.93 and 0.51x once the top eight shops are stripped), and the gallery-wall-set family does not reproduce under a single matching definition (see directions/04-vintage-bundle-shop.md §10.1 item 2, corrected 2 Oct 2026). This doc's own $17.66/listing is a young-winner 6-month figure, so it is not being compared like-for-like with either comparator). Scored as an attachment to Direction 1 or Direction 2, it is a cheap, well-evidenced 20-listing add-on whose 16:9 masters generate 2:3 and 1:1 crops that feed the parent lane at no marginal cost — and whose Q2 shoulder (41.3% sold, 13.3% of supply) is a genuinely under-exploited pocket.

The two questions only the boss can answer: 1. Is $17–75/listing worth 50–60 hours and a standing inventory commitment, when the same generation run produces wall-art crops earning the corpus yardstick?

1. The direction in one paragraph#

Lane: nursery / kids printable wall art, digital download. Aesthetic: vintage storybook naturalism — hand-painted-looking woodland and safari animals, muted retro palette, no faces, no licensed characters. This is chosen against ThePlumAtelier's actual position: its own case file reads bold colour / maximalist / eclectic (32.0%) inside the nursery lane, and the one thing its dead peers all shared was a catalogue with no named style. Audience: expectant and new parents decorating a specific room (nursery, playroom, toddler bedroom) plus gift buyers at a baby shower — a buyer with a named, describable need, which is what yw_analysis.md §7c identifies as the property separating the top 10 from the other 46. Shape strategy: bundle-backward, and specifically the 51–200 bracket rather than the 200+ bracket. Design one 120-print master catalogue first, extract curated 40-print and 24-print theme sets from it, then a 6-print gallery set, then let single animals feed the whole thing. The evidence for stopping at 120 rather than 500 is in §2c and it is the single most important number in this document: in the merged nursery lane the 51–200 bucket returns $2,658.79 per listing and the 200+ bucket returns $132.95 — a 20x gap — while ThePlumAtelier put 889 of its 983 listings (90.4%) into the dead bucket.

Two live constraints the shape strategy has to absorb. First, the market's realised digital price is $12.00 median with an IQR of $8.01–$13.99, and only 6.6% of ranking digital listings sit at $20+ (§3a) — so the ladder is built at $20+ list prices with a 35% standing discount that lands the advertised price inside the measured IQR. Second, the best terms are not the obvious ones: nursery gallery wall set carries 58.5% bundle density and no dominant incumbent, and baby shower gift wall art carries 4.5% bundle density and is structurally empty. Both facts change which listings go where, and both are in §3c.

5c. Description approach#

Five blocks, ~350 words, because the corpus shows no measurable description effect and the honest reason to write one is conversion, not ranking.

7. Concepts — the aesthetic bible#

7a. The bible#

Name: Vintage Natural History Nursery. One sentence: hand-painted-looking animals and botanicals from a 1950s children's field guide, in a five-colour palette, on cream paper.

Medium. Gouache-and-ink on slightly textured cream stock. Visible brush texture in large flats, no gradients, no digital smoothness, no outlines thicker than 2px. The reference is a natural-history plate, not a cartoon.

Palette (locked, five inks + paper):

Role Hex Use
Paper #F4EDE0 ground — never pure white
Ink #2E2A26 line and type, warm near-black not #000
Ochre #C89B4A the warm anchor
Sage #8A9A7B the cool anchor
Terracotta #B5654A the accent, ≤15% of any composition
Dusty blue #7C9BB0 used in ocean/vehicle themes only

Nothing else. No neon, no black-and-white line art, no 3D, no gradient meshes, no photographic elements, no faces on any animal.

Light. Flat, even, no cast shadows, no directional light source. Two values maximum per shape. This is what makes 120 prints read as one collection when 6 of them hang together.

Composition rules, in order:

  1. Single subject, centred, occupying 45–60% of the frame height. Never a scene, never more than one animal per print. This is what makes a set hangable.
  2. Generous margin — 18% minimum on every edge. The failure mode of competitor sets is edge-to-edge art that cannot be hung with a frame.
  3. Aspect ratios: A3 (11.7×16.5) / A4 (8.3×11.7) / 8×10 in / 5×7 in. Four ratios shipped per design. 5×7 is not optional — it is the US market's default frame size.
  4. Type, when used, is a single line at the bottom 18%, letterspaced caps, ink colour, never a quote. No names on animals, no personalised text baked into the art.
  5. No gradients, no drop shadows, no 3D, no sparkle, no confetti, no pattern fills.
  6. Every design must survive being shrunk to 5×7. If the texture dies at 5×7, the design is rejected.

What this rules out, explicitly: licensed characters (Pokémon, Toy Story, Pooh, Frozen, Bluey, Paw Patrol, Spiderman — 27.6% of young-shop rows ride licensed IP per the folder's own correction note, and the fastest-revenue lane in the corpus is the fastest way to lose a shop); photorealism; AI-visible artefacts (six fingers, melted muzzles, inconsistent ear counts — every design is checked against this list before shipping); gender-coded colour beyond the terracotta/sage/ochre palette; and the seasonal lanes (the corpus's clearest failure: Christmas listings in yw are 1,437 deep for $7,046 at a 91.58% zero rate).

7c. Series that scale to 100+ without style drift#

The drift risk is real — thelilysatelier's master comment reads "Avoid four audiences in one shop" — so the series rules are explicit:

  1. The palette is the contract. No new ink is added. A design that needs a sixth colour is out of the lane.
  2. One subject rule. Never a scene. This is what holds a 120-print set together visually.
  3. Series expand by subject, never by audience. 120 animals is one audience (parents of babies and toddlers). "Also doing weddings" is the drift.
  4. Every series ships in the same four aspect ratios. A fifth ratio is a new shop.
  5. No seasonal series before year two. Christmas at a shop with 6 months of history is the corpus's documented failure mode.
  6. The two template listings — routine charts and personalised names — are the only non-animal series, and they exist because they serve the same audience in a different room (bathroom, and gift-giving), not a new one.
  7. Refresh, don't replace. Every 6 months, 20 new subjects into the hero; the hero listing's count grows (120 → 140 → 160) and the price is held. Holding the price while the count grows is the cheapest possible price increase, and the corpus shows count-anchored titles reward it.

  • Units per listing over the first 60 days, scaled from the lane's age curve: the merged nursery lane returns $9.64/listing at 1–3 months, so a 30-day-old catalogue cannot be modelled on lifetime figures. The simulation uses shape-specific first-60-day yields of 6.0 (51–200 bundle), 3.0 (10–50 bundle), 1.6 (2–9 set), 0.5 (single), 0.8 (personalised single). These are deliberately well under the lifetime cells ($2,658.79 / $271.85 / $170.21) — a month-1 listing has had one month of a 40-month window.
  • Fee stack: $0.20 listing + 6.5% transaction + 3% payment + 2.9% + $0.30 processing.
  • No ads in the base case. Ads are a decision gate on day 15, not a day-1 default.
  • No COGS (digital) and no tax.

Days 15–21 — the ads gate#

Gate criteria, all three must be true or ads stay off:

  1. At least one listing has ≥10 impressions/day averaged over 7 days.
  2. At least one listing has a favourite rate ≥ 2% (favourites ÷ impressions).
  3. A listing has sold at least once.

If all three hold: open Etsy Ads at $15/day on exactly one listing — the one with the best favourite rate — for 7 days. Measure ROAS against the $24.95 take-home of $24.61 (99.3% keep rate at that price per the fee stack in §4). Break-even ROAS at $15/day = 0.61. Anything above 1.0 sustains; below 0.7 kills it at day 21.

If any criterion fails: no ads, and the reason is logged. The most likely failure is criterion 3 — the lane's 1–3 month zero rate is 92.4%, and a month-old listing selling nothing is the corpus's normal state, not a signal.

  • Day 18: publish 8 singles from the hero (lion, fox, bunny, bear, deer, elephant, giraffe, owl) at $4.95. These exist to catch single-subject search and to feed the tag stack; expect them to sell slowly.

Days 22–30#

  • Day 24: publish listing 17–20 — the remaining 40 designs as two more theme bundles (weather/sky 8 + baby milestones 7, and botanical 6 + seasons 8).
  • Day 26: if ads are on and ROAS > 1.0, add a second listing to the ad set at $5/day. Total ad ceiling $20/day. Above that the day-30 revenue projection stops covering it.
  • Day 28: the 30-day review. §9a.
  • Day 30: decide whether to continue. Do not expand the catalogue before this gate.

10. The case FOR, the case AGAINST, and what would kill it#

10a. The strong case FOR#

  1. The lane beats the entire rest of the corpus at every age a launch shop reaches, by 1.6x–2.2x, age-matched. 1–3mo: $9.64 vs $5.87 (1.64x). 4–6mo: $56.44 vs $28.94 (1.95x). 7–12mo: $107.11 vs $61.33 (1.75x). 13–24mo: $546.88 vs $250.81 (2.18x). It only loses at 25mo+, which a new shop does not reach for two years. This is the load-bearing argument and it is not hand-waved — it is the age-matched table, computed by holding age constant so the "the masters are just older" objection is removed.

  2. The 0.90x yield figure in the brief is a basis artefact, not a property of the lane. The 0.90x pools mature s17 listings with 4-month-old yw listings in a lifetime average. On the yw-native 6-month basis the lane is 2.2x the corpus on revenue per listing ($47.43 vs $21.56). On the age-matched basis it is 1.6x–2.2x throughout. A shop should be planned on the age-matched number, not the pooled one.

  3. The 51–200 bracket is the single best-performing cell in this lane, in both cohorts, at a young age. 97 listings at $2,658.79/listing and a 42.3% zero rate; yw-only, 55 listings at $667.47 and 61.8% zero; s17, 42 listings at $5,104.57 and 21.4% zero. Meanwhile 89.3% of the lane's listings sit in the 200+ bracket returning $132.95. The lane's dominant format is its worst format. That is an opening, not a ceiling.

  4. Count-anchored N+ titles are the highest-yield construction in the corpus and are used by only 0.7% of the lane. 41 listings, $2,114.85/listing at a 29.3% zero rate — against a lane average of $182.19 and a lane zero rate of 73.5%. Corpus-wide, N+ runs 24.63 units/listing and a 49.2% zero rate, the only format in yw_analysis.md §3b where a minority of listings fail.

  5. $20+ is 5.6% of the lane's listings and 40.6% of its revenue at a 46.2% zero rate. $1,200.89/listing. And 200+ bundles specifically return $1,288.19/listing at 39.5% zero.

  6. Concentration is higher in this lane than the corpus, which means a small catalogue can win. Top 10 nursery listings = 0.18% of the lane's listings and 30.7% of its revenue, versus 0.25%/45.4% corpus-wide. A 16-listing catalogue is not a compromise here; it is the shape of the business.

  7. A template exists and it is live, small, and young. MartyWallArt: 109 listings, 95% nursery, 93.6% bundle-titled, 98.2% of units from bundles, median $12, three price points, $182.97/listing at 67% zero — the #1 copyable young-winner template in the corpus at ≥50 listings, and it got there in 8 months with 109 listings, not 983. Its subjects are licensed and are not copied; its mechanics are lifted whole.

  8. Two mature shops prove the ceiling and both are in this lane. LoveDictionary: $461.29/listing on the nursery family, Bundle of +1250 Nursery Prints at $34.40 doing 1,662 units. StylingHerLife: $1,026.22/listing on the nursery family, 110 Kids Room Prints at $19.87 doing 1,896 units — one product, four subject lines, 4,533 units. Both are reproducible products, not one-of-a-kind art.

  9. The fee stack does not punish this lane. At $12.61 the keep rate is 98.7%; at $24.95, 99.3%; at $32.10, 99.6%. The $0.20 listing fee is 1.6% of a $12.61 sale and 0.8% of a $24.95 sale. A digital nursery bundle has almost no per-unit cost, so price is free — which is exactly why the lane's $8–11.99 problem is a volume-placement problem, not an economics one.

  10. The page one is not review-locked and it is not held. 10 of 18 measured page-one listings had zero reviews; 17 distinct shops on one page; 164 distinct shops across seven nursery queries. The lane rotates. And the incumbency moat is term-specific, not lane-wide: NatalieMarieWhite (6 years, 33,581 sales, 3.1k reviews) owns 13 of the top 28 slots on nursery wall art digital download, but none of the three large incumbents dominates nursery gallery wall set — the term with 58.5% bundle density. Thelilysatelier (421 listings, 10 months, 1,499 sales, 63 reviews) ranks on two of the seven. A new shop can place, on the term the incumbents have not concentrated on.

  11. The gift term is structurally empty. baby shower gift wall art has 4.5% bundle share against the lane's 32.4% overall, and a $8.07 digital median — while the lane's personalized print returns 59.73 units/use and newborn name sign 73.58 units/use. A $10.95 personalised name bundle aimed at a term with no bundles on it is the single least-contested cell identified anywhere in this document.

  12. The lane's discounting convention is now measured, not assumed. 46.1% of ranking listings are on sale with a median discount of 30%, and the incumbents run 45–70%. A plan that discounts 35% from day 12 is not taking a risk on being uncompetitive — it is joining a convention the incumbents already set. §9a's kill conditions are measured against that convention rather than invented.

10b. The honest case AGAINST#

  1. The worst survival in the corpus is here, and it is not a close call. Nursery/kids has an 84.1% zero rate on the yw-native basis and 73.5% on the merged basis, against a 70.23% merged corpus average. A new shop is planning for a business where roughly three-quarters of its listings earn nothing. That is not a flaw — every lane in the corpus is 70%+ zero — but it is the lane's defining condition and it means the catalogue is a discovery mechanism, not inventory.

  2. The lane's yield advantage exists in units-per-listing terms only for the mature cohort. At merged basis nursery returns 0.82x corpus on units per listing (13.56 vs 16.44). The 2.2x revenue advantage is a price effect, and a price effect can be competed away — §10b.3 is the proof.

  3. The lane's own winners run 45–70% discounts, and the corpus's price bands do not describe what buyers pay. Capture B measures the ranking digital median at $12.00 with an IQR of $8.01–$13.99, and only 6.6% of ranking digital listings sit at $20+ — while the corpus says the $20+ band returns $1,200.89/listing. LoveDictionary advertises at $9.87 against a $24.68 list; LumioraPrints at $6.49/$12.99; MartyWallArt at $6.60/$12.00. §3c resolves this by holding list prices high and discounting 35%, but that resolution is a hypothesis built from two sources that disagree, and the corpus's $20+ evidence may simply be a list-price artefact rather than a yield finding. If it is an artefact, this direction's price discipline is built on a measurement error.

  4. Two of the four shops holding the top of this lane are 38 and 46 months old, and a third died at 10 months. The lane's revenue is real but it is old. A new shop starts at month 0 of a curve whose inflection sits at month 13 and whose top is at month 38–46. This direction has no short-term payoff and the corpus gives no evidence that it ever will.

  5. Both dead shops over-indexed on nursery/kids, and the pattern is specific. LinoaVintage: 98.3% nursery, median $9.46, 29.2% of units from bundles, 93.6% zero, closed. ThePlumAtelier: 86.3% nursery, 82.4% of its catalogue at the $10.45 floor returning 13.0% of revenue at a 94.8% zero rate, 90.4% of its listings in the 200+ bracket at $23.93/listing and 93.5% zero, closed at 11 months. Two of the three shops in the corpus that run a majority-nursery catalogue at a young age are dead. (PixelscapeArtStudio is the third and it is 100% IP-riding — not a model.) The lane's young-age survival rate is 0 for 2.

  6. The $8–11.99 band is the lane's worst corpus cell at $48.86/listing and an 84.2% zero rate — and it is where a new shop's instinct puts it, and where most of the incumbents actually list. Every case file in the folder says "$9-13 is the proven launch band." In this lane that advice describes both the corpus's worst cell and the market's default shelf. Following it is exactly what ThePlumAtelier did.

  7. Only five shops in 73 carry ≥50% nursery listings. The lane is thin at the top, and two of the five are dead and one is IP-dependent. Thin markets have thin moats and thin second chances — there is no second-best shop in this lane to copy when the first attempt fails.

  8. The bundle-count ceiling is a real trap and it is empirically demonstrated, not theoretical. 5,083 of 5,692 lane listings (89.3%) are 200+ prints and return $132.95/listing. ThePlumAtelier put 90.4% of its catalogue there. A shop that decides "I'll do 500 prints because LoveDictionary did 1,250" will reproduce ThePlumAtelier's arithmetic exactly. The successful lane template is 120, and the natural instinct is 500.

  9. Live-recon coverage is eight queries across two agents, not one clean pass, and the two captures disagree materially. This agent's CDP pass (kids wall art, 37.1% ads, 11.1% digital) and the sibling agent's card-level pass (seven nursery terms, 17.8% ads, 26.5% digital) were taken on the same day and differ by 19 points on ad share and 15 points on digital share. The reconciliation in §3c is an argument, not a measurement. playroom wall art — the lane's best keyword ratio at volume 1,413 against competition 137,490 — was never captured by either pass, and there is no page-one evidence for it at all. No per-listing review data exists for the seven nursery terms.

  10. Everbee's keyword data for this lane is unreliable at exactly the point it matters. animal wall art reports volume 192 against 1,771,975 competition — a ratio that would kill the entire subject — while the tag data shows animal nursery print returning 140.77 units/use and $3,578 rev/use, the highest revenue-per-use tag in the lane. Everbee's suggestion lists for this lane are dominated by misspelling artefacts with competition counts of 1–15. The keyword volume table in §6 should be read as direction, not as sizing.

  11. The 35% discount that makes this plan rankable also costs 38% of month-one revenue ($685.76 at list → $425.72 advertised). If the discount proves insufficient for page one, the only remaining lever is a deeper one, and the floor is the $8.01 bottom of the digital IQR — at which point the shop is competing with LumioraPrints at $6.49 and MartyWallArt at $6.60, both of which are corpus-proven but neither of which is making $1,200/listing.

Verdict material — no verdict, the boss decides#

The one-line summary of the evidence: this lane has the best age-matched yield of any lane in the corpus (1.6x–2.2x through month 24), the best-performing format cell in the lane is under-used by 89% of its own listings (51–200 at $2,658.79/listing), and a live, small, young template exists (MartyWallArt, $182.97/listing at 109 listings) — and both young shops that ran a majority-nursery catalogue with the wrong price floor and the wrong count ceiling are dead, one at 11 months on the highest revenue in the pack.

What this direction is: a high-conviction bet on a slow curve, executed with ThePlumAtelier's mechanics and none of its arithmetic. It requires holding three positions simultaneously that the corpus rewards but that are each individually easy to abandon under pressure: list prices at $20+ when the market advertises at $12, catalogue under 60 listings when the folder's own advice is to publish in the hundreds, and count at 120 when the instinct is 500. The lane's whole return is in the discipline, and discipline is what failed at ThePlumAtelier and LinoaVintage.

What this direction is not: a 30-day revenue play. §9a models a correct 30 days at $686 gross at list, $426 advertised — and the PlumAtelier's $11,351/month is a month-11-to-13 outcome on a 983-listing catalogue. Reproducing the catalogue without the discipline reproduces the closure.

17 Master Shops & the Merged 73-Shop Corpus

The 17 starred shops on lifetime units, merged with the 56 young winners into one corpus — with the maturity confound stated rather than hidden.

Prepared: 2 Oct 2026. Sources: shops17_listings.csv (13,846 listings, 17 shops) and yw_listings.csv (26,429 listings, 56 young winners). Charts: charts/shops17_charts.json (32 objects).

Headline: of the four doctrines on the table, bundle-first launch is confirmed and count-anchored titles are confirmed but for a narrower reason than stated. The faith lane is contradicted at corpus level and rescued only within-shop. One-aesthetic is confirmed — it is what the market already does, not a discipline to impose.

0. Method, and the two decisions that shaped everything#

Merge integrity. The 17 and the 56 share zero shop names and zero product links. Union = 73 shops, 40,275 listings. No dedupe was needed beyond confirming that; the "dedupe shops that appear in both" instruction found nothing to dedupe. The two sources also share 1,540 identical tags, which is the real evidence the merge is legitimate — the young winners were tagged in the same vocabulary as the master shops, so lane and tag analysis can run across the union without a translation layer.

Lane assignment is exclusive. The first pass let every listing match every lane it hit, which produced shares over 100% and made every shop look like a 17-way generalist. Every listing now takes exactly one lane — the highest-priority lane it matches, with room-placement lanes (home_decor, vintage_gallery) demoted unless nothing more specific hit. Lane shares now sum to 1.0 within a shop and the corpus. This is the single biggest change from the earlier per-shop notes, and it is why the lane numbers below are lower and more honest than "top theme 31%".

Estimated revenue = price × Est. Total Sales. Currencies are native per shop and no FX conversion is applied (per the Phase 2 caveat). Cross-shop revenue ranks are approximate; within-shop ratios — which is what all four doctrine tests use — are unaffected.

Two numbers that must not be read across the source boundary. The 17 master shops are 34% of the merged listings but 84% of merged revenue, because they are older: median listing age 12 months vs 4 for the young winners (shop-median 10 vs 4). Shop age is the single strongest correlate of revenue per listing in this corpus (r = 0.465) — stronger than bundles, stronger than price, stronger than tag discipline. That is why the top-3/bottom-3 split is run a second time on an age-matched window.

Reading the spread. The set is not uniformly mature: median listing age is 12 months, but it runs from 1 month (PosterluxPrint, PrintandGatherStudio) to 35 (AprilLaneArt). Read yields alongside the age column. The top three (LoveDictionary $1,503, HarmonyPixels $1,376, NorthPrints $1,347) beat the bottom three (PosterluxPrint $34.70, ChristArtCrafts $13.92, PrintandGatherStudio $4.73) by 318x. NorthPrints has the largest revenue pool ($2.41M) but ranks third on yield because it bought that with 1,792 listings. HarmonyPixels reaches near-top yield on 300 listings. StylingHerLife is the standout anomaly: $915/listing on 376 listings at a $16.56 median — high price, low volume, and it is the only shop in the 17 combining a $13-20 median with nursery depth.

The three genuinely different businesses in this set:

  • LoveDictionary — the reference bundle shop. 14% of listings are bundles, 34% of revenue, 67% of revenue from count-anchored titles, 36% of sales from its top 10. Median $12.61.
  • HarmonyPixels — 300 listings, 88% of revenue on 17% of listings, 95% of revenue count-anchored, 83% of sales in its top 10. This is the most bundle-concentrated shop in the corpus and the cleanest proof of doctrine.
  • NorthPrints — the anti-doctrine control. 1,792 listings at one price ($5.66 for the middle 50%), 7% bundles, 3% count-anchored, 32% zero-sale, top-10 only 14% of sales. It still earns $1,347/listing purely on breadth and 73 months of age. The engine-B shop the Pattern Map called "the slowest model" is in fact the second-largest revenue pool in the set.

RadialPosters is not comparable to the rest: median price $106.80, 97% music_band, 45% of sales from two personalised listings. It is a physical + personalised hybrid and its revenue per listing reflects a different product category entirely.

The yield curve peaks at $13-20 and it is a real peak, not a rookie artifact. Holding to listings aged 6+ months, $13-20 still returns $1,306/listing at a 67% sold rate — the best combination of volume and yield anywhere in the corpus. The ladder in the Phase 2 pack ($2-5 floor, $17-36 money tier) is directionally right but the peak sits lower and narrower: $13-20, not $17-36.

$3-5 is the graveyard, and it is 23% of the corpus. 9,341 listings at $26.97/listing and a 25% sold rate. Together with $5-8 (15,621 listings) that is 52% of all listings earning 22% of revenue. A new shop following "cheap singles as traffic floor" is putting its largest catalogue block in the least productive band in the data.

$30-50 is a trap on the full corpus but a prize when mature. Full corpus: 23% sold rate, $616/listing. Mature-only: 61% sold rate, $2,664/listing. The difference is entirely composition — young shops list at $30-50 and the listing does not sell; older shops list at $30-50 and it does. This band is a maturity signal, not a pricing strategy.

$80+ physical is the highest yield per listing in the corpus: $2,384/listing, $1,797 on 1.4% of listing volume across 10 shops. Sold rate 10% — it almost never sells, but when it does it is worth 50-100x a digital unit. That is a 10-shop behaviour, not a launch tactic, and it corroborates the Pattern Map's Engine D endgame.

6. The four doctrines: what the data supports#

6a. Count-anchored titles — CONFIRMED, but narrower than claimed#

Corpus-wide: count-anchored listings are 11.9% of the catalogue, 28.8% of revenue, at $596/listing vs $123 — a 4.85x lift. Sold rate 36.3% vs 28.9%.

Within-shop (70 shops with ≥8 count-anchored listings), which removes shop-quality confounding: median lift 1.91x, and 46 of 70 shops see a lift.

The narrowing. Inside bundles only, the count-anchor lift collapses to 0.95x — i.e. no effect at all. Count-anchoring does not independently add revenue; it is a marker for bundle-ness. The number in the title is doing the same job the word "bundle" does, and once you already know it is a bundle, adding the number is free but not additive.

And it fails outright in 24 of 70 shops (chart c13). The failures are informative, not noise: - ChristArtCrafts 0.08x, PrintandGatherStudio 0.00x — single-subject designs ("Follow Christ – Jesus Walking Path") where a count is meaningless. - RadialPosters 0.34x — the product is personalised, so the count hook competes with "Choose Your Album". - JCSDesignWorks 0.69x, WhereFaithIs 0.68x, LinoaVintage 0.68x — established catalogues whose revenue sits in older listings.

Verdict: count-anchor every bundle title — it costs nothing and the bundle effect is real. Do not treat it as a separate lever, and never put a count on a single-subject or personalised listing.

6b. Bundle-first launch — CONFIRMED, the strongest of the four#

Within-shop (51 shops with ≥8 bundle listings): median lift 2.99x, 38 of 51 shops see a lift — the most consistent result in this study, and the only one where the losers (13 shops) are a minority.

The launch-timing claim holds directly. Among the 7,422 listings aged 0-1 months across 65 shops: bundles are 8.5% of the catalogue but carry 34.5% of month-1 revenue and 24.3% of month-1 sales. Bundle revenue share is flat at 28-35% in every age bucket from month 1 onward — it does not need the shop to age first. Bundle revenue per listing at 0-1 months is $15.20 vs $2.70 for singles, a 5.6x ratio in the very first month.

57 shops have ≥20 listings aged ≤2 months. 17 launched with ≥10% bundles, 40 with less. Median launch bundle share 6.5%.

The honest limit. Launch bundle share has essentially zero correlation with eventual shop revenue per listing (r = 0.009, n=34 shops aged 4+ months). Shop-wide bundle revenue share correlates at r = 0.273. So bundle-first launch does not predict a successful shop on its own — it is a necessary-looking high-yield configuration, not a sufficient condition. What it does guarantee is that the month-1 revenue that does arrive is concentrated in the highest-yield listing type instead of being spread across $4 singles.

Verdict: launch with bundles. The evidence that bundles outperform is strong and consistent. The evidence that launching with bundles makes the shop succeed is not there — do not treat it as the differentiator between winners and losers.

6c. The faith lane — CONTRADICTED at corpus level, rescued within-shop#

This is the finding that most changes the picture, and it only appears once the 56-shop merge is done.

Corpus-wide, faith is the worst-performing major lane. 3,974 listings across 52 shops — 9.9% of the corpus — earning 3.5% of revenue at $64.03/listing vs $191.77 for everything else. A 0.33x lift. Sold rate 24.0%. Median price $6.88. Only 4.4% of faith listings are bundles. Mature listings confirm it: $94.68 vs $407.08 — 0.23x.

The Phase 2 pack called faith "the hottest repeated niche in the folder." On the 17-shop corpus that read was supportable (2 of 17 shops dominated by faith). On the full 73-shop corpus it weakens: 8 of 73 shops are faith-dominant — 6 of the 56 young winners also lead with faith, and they are small. Ranked by total estimated revenue they sit at 19, 28, 29, 30, 57 and 67 of 73: FaithEverywhere ($28.2k, the best faith shop in the corpus), WhereFaithIs ($14.6k), JCSDesignWorks ($13.8k), TheMossyPrints ($13.7k), DigiLabsy ($6.2k), ThePsalmistCottage ($4.0k). Six shops independently discovered the faith lane; the median one lands around the corpus's revenue midpoint and none is a breakout.

But the within-shop test flips it. Across the 19 shops with ≥25 faith listings, faith listings beat that shop's own non-faith listings in 14 of 19 cases, median lift 1.74x — and on mature listings only, median 2.18x. ChristArtCrafts 59x, TheMossyPrints 4.9x, PixelsToFramesCo 4.7x, ThePsalmistCottage 2.4x. Where faith wins, it wins big; where it loses (LumioraPrints 0.26x, LoveDictionary 0.18x) it loses because those shops built an unrelated machine.

The reconciliation. Faith is not a profitable tactic; it is a viable identity. As an identity it concentrates demand and produces a runaway hero ("Jesus Leaves the 99" appears in two unrelated shops as a top seller — DigitalStudioArtUA at 979 sales and PixelsToFramesCo at 535). As a tactic — "add some scripture prints to a shop" — it is dead on arrival, because the 0.33x is the market price for an unfocused faith listing.

Also note the price trap. The faith lane's median price is $6.88 against the $13-20 yield peak. Faith shops sell cheap because DigitalStudioArtUA (1,586 faith listings at $3.37 median, mostly SVG/PNG sublimation) sets the lane's price reference and it is the largest single block of faith supply in the corpus. The pack's "$12-17 personalised Jesus portraits" observation holds only for personalised listings — UnspirationalLiving (57% personalised, 91% of revenue from it, median $28.99) is the profitable faith-adjacent model, not the $3 SVG one.

Verdict: if faith is chosen, it must be the shop's identity, priced at $13-20 not $3, and it must be personalised. Do not add it as a side lane. The Research Pack's "crowded but proven" is right on crowded, wrong on proven-at-corpus-level.

Every shop in the corpus has a dominant lane above 20%, and 66 of 73 are above 30%. The "diversified shop with top-2 = 47%" case from the Pattern Map is the exception, not the rule.

The top3/bottom3 comparison makes it operational: top 3 average 0.90 dominant-lane share, bottom 3 average 0.47. That is the cleanest separator in the study after sold rate.

The important nuance. Correlation between lane concentration and revenue per listing is positive and moderate, not decisive (r = 0.32 on the 73 shops; r = 0.18 on the 17 master shops alone). Concentration alone does not make money — VintageGlowPrints is 24% concentrated and earns $121/listing; NorthPrints is 70% concentrated and earns $1,347, and both clear the median. What concentration does is enable the bundle ladder: you cannot build "100 nursery prints" inside four unrelated lanes. So the one-aesthetic rule is best read as a precondition for the bundle doctrine, not as an independent revenue driver.

The weak-concentration failures are all cases where the shop's apparent variety is really one aesthetic viewed through different keyword sets — VintageGlowPrints' "variety" is all vintage floral at one price. Apparent diversification over a single aesthetic is not diversification.

Verdict: confirmed, and worth acting on precisely because the correlation is real (r = 0.32) rather than an artefact of shop age. Ship one aesthetic — its main job is to make bundles buildable, and it will not make money on its own.

6e. Supporting findings not on the list#

Personalised listings are the highest-AOV digital lever, and the merge strengthens the case. 967 listings (2.4% of catalogue) carry 4.0% of revenue at $302/listing vs $176 — a 1.72x lift at a $13.31 median price. Within-shop it is decisive for the shops that use it: CreaPawsCo (custom pet portrait = 92.9% of revenue), MatchboxPosterLuna (custom couple gift 100%), UnspirationalLiving (90.5% of revenue from 57% personalised listings). On the 17-shop corpus alone personalised looked like it FAILED (0.78x) — RadialPosters and ThatsMyTypePrints drag it. On the merged corpus it is positive. Worth knowing that this was the one conclusion that reversed when the corpus was merged.

Power law is universal and it is the operating condition, not a warning. 70.2% of all 40,275 listings in the corpus have never sold. Median shop zero-sale share 77.9%; the best shops are HarmonyPixels (6.3% zero, 281 of 300 listings selling), AprilLaneArt (10.2%) and Thelittleprintshopx (11.7%); worst is PrintandGatherStudio at 98.3%. Only 4 of 73 shops are below 20% zero-sale. Median top-10 sales share 50.7%, median top-1 share 11.0%. Correlation between top-1 concentration and revenue per listing is only +0.258 — hero-concentration does not predict a good shop. The shops that die are not the concentrated ones; they are the ones where nothing sold at all (bottom-3 sold rate 8.8%).

The age curve kills launch-time curation. Revenue per listing runs $3.76 (0-1mo) → $15.23 → $40.56 → $88.64 → $337.74 → $1,467.42 (24mo+). A listing's winner status is not identifiable in month 1. This is the strongest argument in the corpus for launching wide and cutting later rather than curating a small perfect catalogue — and it also means the two young 1-month shops in this data (PosterluxPrint, PrintandGatherStudio) are not yet evidence of anything.

7. Merged-corpus notes: where the two sources agree and disagree#

Zero overlap. 0 shared shop names, 0 shared product links, 1,540 shared tags. The union is a genuine 73-shop corpus, not a double-count.

They agree on the leader. vintage_gallery is the dominant lane in 7 of 17 master shops and 17 of 56 young winners — the same lane leading both samples, selected independently by different operators at different times.

They disagree on faith. 2 of 17 master shops are faith-dominant; 6 of 56 young winners are. The young winners found the lane later and more often but built smaller versions of it — their six faith shops rank 19th-67th of 73 on revenue, and the corpus's single best faith shop (FaithEverywhere, $28.2k) still earns a fifteenth of what DigitalStudioArtUA earns on faith alone at $3.37 a listing. The 17-shop reading of "hottest repeated niche" was a small-sample artefact that the merge qualified.

They disagree on the size of the prize. The master shops average $179/listing; the young winners average $32. Every lane and every doctrine looks ~5-65x better in the master sample. Any recommendation that compares a young winner's revenue to a master shop's revenue is comparing shop age, not shop quality. The within-shop and age-matched tests in this report are the only comparisons that survive.

They agree on the shape of the ladder. The $13-20 peak holds in both sources and in the mature-only control.

One sample artefact to carry forward: 3 of the 17 shops (NorthPrints, LoveDictionary, Thelittleprintshopx) hold 70.7% of the master revenue; the top 4 (adding AprilLaneArt) hold 77.2%. Any statistic quoted as a "master 17" average is effectively a NorthPrints statistic. This is why the merged corpus, with 73 shops, is the more honest base for future work.

8. What this changes in the doctrine#

Doctrine Verdict The one-line revision
Count-anchored titles Confirmed, narrower Use on bundle titles only — the number is a bundle marker, not a second lever (within-bundle lift 0.95x). Never on single-subject or personalised listings.
Bundle-first launch Confirmed, strongest Bundles return 5.6x in month 1 and 2.99x within-shop median. But launch bundle share has r = 0.009 with eventual success — necessary, not sufficient.
Faith lane Contradicted at corpus level 0.33x corpus-wide; 6 of 6 young faith shops underperform. Only works as a full identity at $13-20, personalised. 1.74x within-shop median is why it still tempts.
One-aesthetic rule Confirmed, descriptive 66 of 73 shops already put >30% of revenue in one lane (r = 0.32 vs yield). Its job is enabling the bundle ladder, not generating revenue on its own.
Price ladder Confirmed, peak revised Yield peaks at $13-20 ($1,306/listing mature), not $17-36. Avoid $3-5: 23% of the catalogue, $27/listing.
Personalisation Confirmed on merge (reversed on 17 alone) 1.72x, $302/listing, $13.31 median. The one conclusion that flipped when the corpus merged.
Physical endgame Confirmed, strongest yield $1,797/listing on 1.4% of listings. Keep as endgame; it is a 10-shop behaviour.
SVG assets New negative finding $43.73/listing, 17% sold rate, 8 shops. Side experiment at $3-4, not a product line.

The single change with the largest expected effect is not any of the four doctrines — it is the price band. $3-5 listings are 23% of the corpus and return $27 each. A shop that moved half that block up to $13-20 would roughly quintuple the yield of its largest catalogue segment without changing a single title. The bundle doctrine tells you what to sell; the price band tells you what it is worth, and the second lever is currently the more misused one.

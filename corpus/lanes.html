What the 73-Shop Corpus Contains

22 qualified lanes, 14 aesthetics, 120 qualified directions — every one with the count that sizes it and the yield that decides it.

Compiled 2 Oct 2026 from /home/ubuntu/etsy_phase2/*.csv only. This is the input list for a full research project per direction.

A direction here is three independent axes, which is how the data actually separates:

  1. Product lane - what the buyer is buying for (nursery, faith, vintage, clipart, humour, ...).
  2. Aesthetic - what it looks like (vintage oil painting, cottagecore, dark academia, Y2K, ...).
  3. Shape strategy - how the shop converts (single-print volume, bundle-led, personalised, frame-tv, high-ticket physical, whole-shop mega bundle).

A shop runs one of each. A direction is a combination that the corpus shows at scale, so the same word appearing in a shop name tells you nothing and these three numbers do.

Read this first: four things that constrain every number below#

1. The units column is mixed-basis and cannot be compared naively. The corpus is two pulls. The 56 Young Winners shops contribute Est. Sales Last 6 Mo - a real 6-month window. The 17 starred shops contribute Est. Total Sales - lifetime. So the total below is 6 months of one third of the corpus plus the entire lifetime of the rest. Units and revenue totals are valid for ranking directions against each other; they are not a market size.

2. Revenue here is a proxy, not an Everbee field. Everbee's own revenue is native-currency (CAD/EUR/GBP/AUD/TRY/INR/USD) and 30-day only, so it cannot be summed across shops. The only per-listing revenue derivable from these CSVs is units x listed price, and that is what this document uses. It is a gross sticker-price proxy in USD, before Etsy fees, before the standing ~95%-on-sale / 35%-off reality, and before physical COGS. The folder's own fee model puts a non-US digital seller at roughly 11-30% of price depending on band. Treat every revenue figure as a relative number with a consistent haircut, never as a profit number.

3. 6,541 of 40,275 listings do not match any lane marker. They land in unclassified and are kept in the corpus rather than dropped, because dropping them would quietly bias every share. A direction that lives inside unclassified is a direction the keyword taxonomy has not learned yet, not a direction that does not exist.

4. "Direction" is counted at listing level, not shop level. A direction with 30 listings across 6 shops is a real cross-shop pattern. The qualification floors below (lane: 40 listings AND 3 shops; aesthetic: 60 listings; direction: 25 listings AND 2 shops) exist so a single shop's peculiarity does not get promoted to a market.

12 of 16 lanes clear a 1.5x bundle premium. That is the rule, and it is the single largest lever in the corpus: the same product sold as a 200-print bundle out-earns the same product sold as one print, at the same price point, in the same lane.

3 lanes sit flat (0.9-1.5x) and 1 go the other way: music / album / band at 0.4x. Together with the flat lanes these are the informative cases:

  • The flat and inverted lanes are 4 of 16 - the named cases follow.
  • Minimalist / neutral / beige (1.0x) - bundles do not help. The lane's problem is not the shape, it is that nobody is buying "minimalist" as a want; it is a description of the absence of a choice. Fixing the aesthetic, not the bundle, is the only lever here.
  • Landscape / nature vista (0.9x) - the lane has the lowest bundle share of any qualified lane (1.3% of units) and the second-highest dead tail (82.8%). Bundles were never built here, so there is no premium to collect. This is an unbuilt mechanic, not a failed one - which is exactly the Lumiora situation.
  • Music / album / band (0.4x) - inverted for a structural reason: its "single" listings are the physical prints at a $106.8 median, so the single-print cell is already the high-ticket one. It is not a counterexample to the bundle mechanic, it is a lane where physical pricing, not bundling, sets the yield.

So: the bundle mechanic is the default and it is large, but the lanes that lack it are the ones with room to build it, and one lane (minimalist) is broken in a way no amount of bundling fixes.

⚠ Basis and matching-surface disclosure (added 2 Oct 2026)#

Two cells in Section 1 are quoted elsewhere in the pack as if they were measurements of a lane a new shop could enter. They are not.

  • botanical / floral at $366.88/listing is a lifetime, two-shop figure. Strip the two dominant shops and the lane falls to $51.67; strip the top eight and it is $17.88 at 16.2% sold. On the 6-month young-winner basis — the population a launch shop is actually in — the lane is 2,919 listings across 42 shops at $17.00/listing = 0.79x the yardstick, degrading to $10.93 = 0.51x at strip-8 (directions/09-botanical-vintage.md §2.7, §2.11). 55.4% of the lane's entire revenue is one $2,063.81 hero mega-bundle in a 46-month shop.
  • gallery wall / maximalist set at $2,456.46/listing (344 listings) does not reproduce under a single matching definition, and it is not the same cell as the gallery wall set figure used in yw_analysis.md §8.3. That one has been restated on its reproducible definition: 589 listings at $2,230.95/listing = 18.22% of revenue, with the widely-quoted 40.79% revenue share belonging to the wider gallery wall surface (1,793 listings, $1,623.88, 40.36%). See directions/04-vintage-bundle-shop.md §10.1 item 2.
  • The corpus-wide merged bundle multiple is 9.32x, not 16.19x — see yw_analysis.md §8.4. Bundle economics remain the largest single shape effect in the corpus under every classifier tested (4.79x–15.48x depending on where the class boundary is drawn).
  • Every score in debate_ranking_v2.md is conditional on reaching scale. This catalogue is a winner's sample: 56 young winners + 17 masters, plus 4 dead shops that left no listings behind. The base rate for a shop started from zero is not in this data.

The unclassified bucket: 68 shops, 6,541 listings, 110,934 units, $987,307 proxy, median $6.6. These are listings whose title and tags name a subject no lane keyword caught. They are kept in the corpus so no share is biased by dropping them. Its yield of $150.94 per listing sits between the strongest and weakest lanes, so it is a mixed bag rather than a single undiscovered direction - but it is the first place to look when extending the taxonomy.

Section 8 - The three questions the corpus answers#

Is one aesthetic necessary? The data says yes and the mechanism is visible: shops with no named aesthetic earn $144.97 per listing, while the named aesthetics that clear a real floor - bold colour / maximalist ($668.55), vintage oil painting ($523.61) and humour / pun (n/a) - all clear it. Naming the style is the cheapest available lever and the corpus is full of shops that skipped it.

Do bundles work outside the bundle shops? Yes, and the margin table at the top is the proof. The same lane at the same price earns multiples more per listing when sold as a set. What does not generalise is the specific bundle revenue share the folder recorded for Lumiora (94%) - that is a property of that shop, not a rule.

How much of a catalogue can be dead? 47.3% to 87% across the qualified lanes, 57.7% to 87.7% across aesthetics. Treat the dead tail as the cost of running a discoverable catalogue, not as a failure - but note that the lowest dead tails (60.1% humour, 47.3% college) are both catalogue-refresh stories, not luck.

Section 9 - Method, and what this data cannot tell you#

Method.

  • 40,275 listing rows loaded from yw_listings.csv and shops17_listings.csv; 73 shops.
  • Every listing classified on three axes independently. Lane and aesthetic markers are counted with an n-gram Counter (unigrams to trigrams) so a marker phrase is a dict lookup, and a cluster only wins if two different marker phrases fire or one fires 25+ times. That corroboration rule was added after finding ocean (15 hits) had claimed RadialPosters as a coastal shop and sports had claimed ThePlumAtelier via "sports nursery".
  • Shape strategy is derived from the title: whole-shop/mega-bundle marker, then any set/bundle marker, then format, then price band.
  • Qualification floors: lane 40 listings AND 3 shops; aesthetic 60 listings; direction 25 listings AND 2 shops.
  • Reproduce: python3 build_directions.py then python3 render_directions.py.

What this data cannot tell you.

  • No search volume, no competition. Nothing here is a demand figure. A lane can have 40,000 units across 59 shops and still be a worse bet than a small one with no competition. The folder's Phase 1 work put demand and competition on separate gates for exactly this reason.
  • No time series per direction. Units are one window per shop (6-month for 56 shops, lifetime for 17). "Trajectory" per direction is not computable from these CSVs; only per-shop velocity vs age is, and that is in the case files.
  • No profit. Every revenue number is gross sticker price before fees, sale discounts and COGS.
  • No differentiation between shops in the same direction. Two shops in the same lane cell here are counted identically. The case files are where that difference lives.
  • The keyword taxonomy is finite. 33,734 listings sit in unclassified. A direction built only from marker phrases will keep missing whatever the taxonomy has not learned.

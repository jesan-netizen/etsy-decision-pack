1. The four engines, with numbers#

Bundle revenue share across the six fully torn-down shops: Lumiora 94%, LoveDictionary 57–83%, RadialPosters 16%, NorthPrints 6%, Thelittleprintshopx 4%, VintageGlow 3% — PM. That spread is the finding: bundles are one engine, not the engine.

A — bundle-led digital (Lumiora 11mo, LoveDictionary 46mo). Lumiora: 323 listings, avg $7.80, floor $5.00; 6mo 3,468 units/$46,383 ≈ $93k/yr annualised; bundles = 84% of units, 94% of revenue; the $15+ tier (21 listings) out-earns the $5 tier (234 listings) ~8.6×; price rises with size ($11.99 at 30–99 items → $16.78 at 500–2,000) — LUM. LoveDictionary: ~91k EUR/6mo (~183k/yr), top-10 = 49% of units, 82% of revenue from the $20–40 mega tier (111 listings); "+1250 Nursery Prints" $34.40 = 473 units/6mo — PM. Recomputed DB: bundle-family listings are 60% of LoveDictionary's top-50, 54% of Lumiora's, vs a 6% median across 17 shops.

B — cheap-single volume (NorthPrints 73mo, VintageGlow 18mo). NorthPrints: 1,792 listings at one price ($5.66 on 80%), bundles 6%, no dominant aesthetic, only 79% zero, top-10 just 12% of units, worst revenue/listing in the study — 73 months and 1,795 listings to reach what LoveDictionary does in 46 with 1,205. VintageGlow at $4.99 pure volume declined 35% off its March peak — PM. Both coasting: NorthPrints recency 0.325 / −52% off March, VintageGlow 0.403 — ML. Recomputed DB: NorthPrints has the lowest zero-sale rate of all 17 (34.1%) and the 2nd-worst revenue per listing — breadth without a wedge.

C — physical mid-price (Thelittleprintshopx 74mo). 133k USD/6mo, 99.8% physical revenue, median $13.36, $10–14.99 = 73% of revenue, kitchen+bathroom humour 67%. Only 17.5% zero and top-10 just 25% of units — the healthiest catalogue in the study. Also #1 on momentum: RAV 6,410, recency 0.653, 17.88 avg 6mo sales/listing — PM, ML. A 74-month-old end state, not a launch state — PM.

D — hero listing + high-ticket physical (RadialPosters 16mo). 49k USD/6mo, ramped 0 → ~17–18k/mo inside 5 months. One personalised digital listing ("Choose Your Album") = 23% of revenue; physical avg $106.80 = 56% of revenue from 15% of units (7.5× digital) — PM. Recomputed DB: top sellers are that listing, its v2, then eight "5 Pack/3 Pack" album posters; only 48 of 420 listings had any 6mo sales. Its recency 1.000 is an artefact — the 12mo pull fell back to the 6mo data — ML.

Verdict on record: launch = A (bundle-backward, one aesthetic); endgame = D (personalised hero + physical); never drift into B; C is later-stage — PM, MEM 28 Sep. Sharda Roy independently: physical = ~10% of units but ~50% of revenue (~$57/unit vs ~$7.50 digital) — MEM 25 Sep.

2. The universals#

Power law. 73–91% of listings sell zero in 6 months, except mature C at 17.5% — PM; 29–95% across 17 shops — RP. Recomputed DB (Sales to Months=0): 71.1% overall, range 19.7% (Thelittleprintshopx) → 98.3% (PrintandGatherStudio), median 76%.

One dominant lane per shop (30–100% of revenue; even the "diversified" shop's top-2 = 47%) — PM. Holds in ML: Kitchen/Humour = 2,265 of Thelittleprintshopx's 6mo, SVG = 1,789 of DigitalStudioArtUA's, Music/Album = 100% of RadialPosters'.

One aesthetic, many niches. Lumiora spans 13 themes but ~79% of revenue ($36,473/$46,383) is one vintage/French-country/cottagecore identity; experiments died (gothic 6 listings, motivational 2→0, abstract 6→0, seasonal 15→0) — LUM. Alfie's rule is absolute and separate from niche: "pick 1 aesthetic and stick with it"; his branding FAQ tops its mistake list with "using random styles across listings"; "Fantasy + celestial = cohesive. Boho + cyberpunk = confusing brand message" — LUM quoting TR 3- Etsy Shop Branding/FAQs - Etsy Shop Branding.md. Counter-evidence: on a 2,505-shop wall-art corpus breadth does not hurt (1-style $2.03 rev/listing/mo vs $5.96 for 5+ styles) and 13 of 14 aesthetics clear 15k sales/6mo — LUM. Current shape locks boho as spine, gallery sets — SR.

Count-anchored titles. Winners lead with a number: "850+ Vintage Gallery Wall Bundle", "Bundle of +1250 Nursery Prints", "110 Kids Room Prints", "2,500–35,000 print", "Set of 6 Cocktail Prints" — RP, SR. Recomputed DB: only 12.1% of all 850 top-50 listings are count-anchored — most repeated formula among winners, not a rule. Contradicted by Alfie's own SEO rule (obvious keyword in first three words, then layer) — DIG §2.

Price ladder (digital). $2–5 Frame-TV/cheap singles = traffic floor · $5–8 singles = volume · $8–18 gallery sets = mid margin · $17–36 mega bundles = the money tier · $25–44 personalised = AOV tier · $56–107 physical = endgame — RP, SR. Recomputed DB: catalogue deciles $3.99/$5.00/$5.66/$6.36/$7.96 median/$8.00/$9.29/$12.61/$16.56; 21.1% of listings ≤$5, 12.8% ≥$15, 4.2% ≥$30. Top-50 winners sit higher: median $9.27, top decile $26.81. In the kids corpus, $500/mo needs 59 sales at a $9.99 mega bundle ($8.45 net) vs 286 at a $2.50 single ($1.75) — KPP §3. $3.50 digital floor clears from $4.46; the $5 gate is physical-only — KPP, MEM.

Physical carries revenue per unit (7.5× at RadialPosters; 99.8% at Thelittleprintshopx); digital-only caps ~$5.5–15k/mo in this set — PM. No big digital shop is still ramping (NorthPrints −52%, LoveDictionary −31%, VintageGlow −35% off March); only the two concentrated young shops were — PM.

3. The faith lane — both directions#

For. Dominant lane in three shops independently: DigitalStudioArtUA (faith/scripture in 1,581 of 1,678 titles; hero "Jesus Leaves the 99" re-cut as four listings, all top-10), ChristArtCrafts (one runaway "Follow Christ – Jesus Walking Path" = 59% of sales), PixelsToFramesCo (faith = #2 pillar, "Jesus Leaves the 99" #3 at 45/mo). "Jesus Leaves the 99" is a proven hero in two separate shops — RP, ML. Personalises well: custom Jesus portraits are the top-price sellers at $12–17, and cross into streetwear SVG — RP; ChristArtCrafts' #2–#5 listings are all custom-Jesus art (87/44/33/33 units) — ML. Recomputed DB, faith share of listings/sales: DigitalStudioArtUA 94.8%/94.4%, ChristArtCrafts 15.2%/86.2%, PixelsToFramesCo 35.9%/64.0%, PrintandGatherStudio 28.3/20.0, Lumiora 34.4/7.4, LoveDictionary 14.0/10.5; corpus-wide 21.4% of listings, only 9.0% of sales. Young sweep: 9 of 56 shops faith-led (FaithEverywhere $4,526/mo, TheMossyPrints $1,878, WhereFaithIs $1,803, JCSDesignWorks $1,810, MoonlineDigitals $1,184, DigiLabsy $983, ThePsalmistCottage $790, SaintlyCreationsArt, WeHeartCozy — Marian/saint oils) = $14,880/mo, 15.6% of the cohort's $95,419 — recomputed YW. PrintandGatherStudio's top 5 are a 5-way tie at 54 sales each including "Good Shepherd" and "Jesus Walking Sunrise" — ML. Evergreen and Q4-indexable: 13 DigitalStudioArtUA Christmas SVGs at $3.32–3.39 captured at zero sales = the window still open — SR §5**.

Against. In the 17-shop last-6-month family table Christian/Religious is 9th of 16 at 1,542 sales (3.4% of 45,443) — behind Gallery Wall 8,185, Vintage 7,758, Frame TV 3,176, Nursery 2,572, Kitchen 2,542, Bathroom 2,299, Custom 1,838, SVG 1,789 — ML. YWC's verdict: "evergreen and proven as a lane; not where current money is moving." Only 1 of the top 15 young shops by revenue is faith-led (recomputed YW). Momentum is negative where faith concentrates: ChristArtCrafts DECAYING 0.32×, DigitalStudioArtUA coasting (recency 0.341, 1.71 avg/listing — worst in the table), PixelsToFramesCo coasting at 0.215 — ML. Alfie gives zero endorsement of belief niches; his only adjacent content is a member proposing a Christian-books lane (20 Jan call) and Alfie answering "there's so many different type of Christians that can benefit from a single Christian book… 50-50 based on internal positive data" — TR January 20th.md. DIG §7 states it: "He gives no evidence that faith art outperforms any other lane."

Read: the most repeatable lane in the corpus and the best-evidenced personalised-digital sub-lane — but not where money moved Aug–Sep, and its two most concentrated shops are decaying.

5. Contradictions and caveats#

  1. Bundle-first vs bundle-last. The vault ships a mega bundle early / bundle-backward (LUM, PM, SR). Alfie's clipart ladder is the reverse: low-ticket packs weeks 1–2, mega bundles built from them weeks 3–4 — DIG §1, high confidence. A lone bundle has nothing to funnel traffic into.
  2. Listing volume. W1P = 1/day then 3+; SR = 2–4/day; KPP = a 50-listing sprint; Alfie's floor = 60 listings, 3/day, 20 days, organic — DIG §4. W1P's "never buy traffic, organic search only" also conflicts with MEM's ads playbook and with using ads as a CTR dashboard.
  3. Title order. Count-anchoring displaces the primary keyword from the front of a 140-char title — DIG §2–3.
  4. Pricing. $5 gate is physical-only per KPP/MEM, yet Lumiora's real floor is $5 and KPP recommends $2.49–3.99 entry singles; Alfie de-ranks low pricing — DIG §5. LEARN: ~95% of pulled listings are on sale, commonly 35% off — every sticker-price revenue figure above overstates realised revenue by the discount depth.
  5. The "young shops" sweep is mislabelled. 1 of 56 is under 6 months, zero at 6–7, median 10, 35 of 56 at 10+; the filter was age<=12 — YWC §1. "1,000+ sales pace" is lifetime; 28 of 56 miss 1,000 in the last 6 months — YWC §2. Sorted by 6mo sales while displaying revenue, hiding CreaPawsCo at rank 37 — YWC §3 (fixed with a second Notion view).
  6. Attribution error. Engine A/B/C/D is not in the 28 Sep Young Winners report (zero occurrences) — it comes from PM/MEM — YWC §8.
  7. Broken 6-month pulls. PrintandGatherStudio, PosterluxPrint, RadialPosters have sales12 == sales6 byte-for-byte and their 6mo totals undercount (fresh 30-day exceeds the whole 6mo window). Their "SURGING" verdicts are artefacts — ML. This undercuts the month-one speed proof in RP and SR §2/§6; only the fresh 30-day check (358 and 518 units) is trustworthy.
  8. Personalised = highest AOV, but the lane is decaying. RadialPosters 23% of revenue from one listing, UnspirationalLiving median $28.99 (RP) — yet UnspirationalLiving is DECAYING 0.33× and ChristArtCrafts 0.32× (ML). CreaPawsCo is a 1.5-listing outlier: 768 of 867 six-month sales in three listings, two near-duplicates, $59.88 sticker on a digital download → ~$27 realised, above the ladder's own AOV tier — YWC §4–5.
  9. IP risk sits where the money is. ArttHouses ($2,625/mo), EliteSportsStudio ($2,453), PixelscapeArtStudio ($2,053) are fan-art, unusable as models — YWC §7. Young sellers overall: 190/688 rows (27.6%) IP-flagged vs 16/584 (2.7%) established — KPP §9.
  10. Data age and currency. Everbee ~95% accurate, ~1 month lag; trends end Aug 2026; currencies native per shop (CAD/EUR/GBP/AUD/USD) with no FX conversion — PM, RP, SR. Sep 2026 not yet in the series (PM).
  11. Double-counting and classifier sensitivity. PixeluxArt ≈ PosterluxPrint are near-clones, one operator — RP §2. LoveDictionary's bundle share is 57% strict vs 78–83% including "Gallery Set" titles — PM. My recomputation puts top-50 bundle share at LD 60% / Lumiora 54% / RadialPosters 0% (my regex misses "5 Pack") — classifier choice moves the number, not the ranking.
  12. Kids corpus was corrected mid-run. The interim lane table double-counted listings matching two probe terms and annualised 6mo windows ×2; corrected figures are authoritative — KPP header notice. is_personalizable measured 0/1,272 — an honest "No" for that whole corpus.
  13. Claims we do NOT hold. "Personalised converts 2–3x" is in no record we have; "want-it-now" and "gifting is a niche" are not in the niche transcript (Loom video unreachable); Alfie verbatim is "crystal clear audience" + product, "there is no right answer" on niche vs product — MEM 16 Sep. Boss's own memory corrections: "Lunario Prints" = LumioraPrints; singles are not $2–3 (floor $5); "100–300 sales per print" is false (85% sell nothing); Lumiora is not "no vibe" — LUM §Verdict.
  14. Lumiora's own state is fragile. Two decaying winners carry it, Aug growth stalled (781 → 648 units), both flagship bundles fell in Aug, Aug/Sep's 21 new listings have zero traction — LUM. "Copy the playbook, not the shop's current state."
  15. Notion hygiene open. Young Winners Note 3/56 populated; Total sales (lifetime) sits beside Sales 6 mo (the pace) under confusing labels — YWC §10.
  16. Everbee folder-items endpoint was broken server-side; the 19-shop folder was recovered only because the boss exported the CSV himself — LUM, MEM 27 Sep.

6. Open questions for the boss#

  1. Launch order — bundle as listing #1 (current plan) or Alfie's packs-first-then-mega? These are different shops.
  2. Cadence floor — 50, 60, or 2–4/day from day one? W1P's 1/day is below every piece of evidence we hold.
  3. Ads standing rule — keep "organic only" (W1P) or adopt efficient-spending + ads-as-CTR-dashboard (MEM 8 Sep)?
  4. Title shape — [850+ Count] Vintage Gallery Wall Bundle… or [Christian wall art] Set of 5…? The rules genuinely collide.
  5. Faith in or out as the spine? Strongest repeatable lane and best personalised sub-lane, but 9th of 16 on current money with its two concentrated shops decaying. Evergreen faith, or evergreen humour/kitchen (what Engine C actually runs)?
  6. Which aesthetic, in one sentence? "Boho spine, gallery sets" is written down (SR) but never resolved into a palette/style/motif list.
  7. Personalised hero — build now or later? Engine D says design it early (PM); the only fresh 30-day evidence on the lane is 0.33× decay.
  8. Physical graduation trigger — clicks, sales, reviews? POD is "deferred, not cancelled" with no defined trigger.
  9. Entry price floor — $2.49–3.99 (KPP launch logic), $5 (Lumiora's real floor), or the $4.46 break-even line?
  10. Is Engine C a deliberate target? The healthiest catalogue in the data is a 74-month-old physical mid-price humour shop. Is a slow resilient $3–4k/mo acceptable, or is the $1k-by-November ladder binding?
  11. Which lane, finally? Digital wall art and kids' practical printables (party/games, 19.2/25) were both researched to completion and both are unbuilt. Two shops or one?
  12. Q4 timing — a late-Oct launch queues behind established shops already discounting in a seasonal category. Evergreen faith entry, or accept the queue?
  13. Everbee as directional — accept it for listing-volume pricing decisions, or buy real seller-statement numbers before committing the ladder?
  14. Adopt as standing rules? Review-velocity step for the zero-review hard mode; a design-elements column in the research DB; whole-shop bundle at ~100 listings pinned as featured.

0.2 The single most important caveat: the two corpora are not comparable samples#

Young Winners listings are all 1–12 months old (median 4 mo, max 12 mo). Shops17 listings run 1–74 months (median 12 mo). The YW shops are also young — 5-12 months old (median 10) — so the corpus is by construction a snapshot of what immature shops' catalogues look like. Shops17 shops are older ({S17_SHOPAGE_TXT}), so their listings have had longer to accumulate.

This means the headline gap (Shops17 units/listing 42.5 vs YW 2.8, a 15.1× difference) is not evidence that the Shops17 shops are better shops. It is mostly an age artefact: sales on a listing accumulate with time, and the two corpora are sampled at completely different points on that curve. Every comparison in this document that does not control for tenure is flagged.

1. The master yield table — shape × price band × corpus#

1.1 How shape is detected#

Shape is inferred from the listing title, not from any structured field (none exists in the data). The parser reads, in priority order: bundle/set/pack of N, then a bare N prints/posters/art phrase, then number-words (set of six). Counts are rejected if they look like a calendar year (1900–2100) or exceed 2,000. A listing is classified:

Shape Rule Corroborating evidence from the titles
mega-bundle Title says bundle (or mega / collection of / box) and the parsed count is ≥ 40, or the word mega appears. e.g. 35,000 Mega Bundle Wall Art Collection, Boys Room Wall Art Bundle 64 Prints. (The false positives 2026 prints ×14 and 911 prints ×9 were calendar years and a car model — see §0.3.)
bundle Title says bundle / box / collection of / mega with a count < 40, or no count at all. Commonest true evidence: bundle of 25 prints, bundle 64 prints.
set Title says set of N / set / gallery wall / series / collection, and there is a count or a set keyword. set of 3 ×328, set of 6 ×278, set of 2 ×163, set of 4 ×62, set of 9 ×46, set of 12 ×38.
single Everything else — one printable, no set/bundle language. Dominant class: 92.7% of YW, 89.3% of Shops17.

Independent audit of the classifier. Recounting directly from the raw CSV: 504 of the 26,429 YW titles match the bundle/mega trigger, while 520 listings carry a non-single shape (bundle 444 + mega-bundle 76). The 16-listing difference is fully explained: 13 reach mega-bundle through the set branch (set of 150, set of 200, set of 50 — large sets phrased without the word bundle) and 3 through the mega keyword or number-word paths. No listing is double-counted and the parser is deterministic.

Distribution of the most common count evidence in YW: set of 3 ×328, set of 6 ×278, set of 2 ×163, set of 4 ×62, set of 9 ×46, set of 12 ×38, 4 prints ×33, set of 10 ×32, set of 7 ×28, set of 8 ×27.

The median 49+ month listing has made 161. Do not forecast a launch off mature-listing benchmarks; nothing in this data says a new listing can be expected to behave like an old one inside 12 months.

5.3 Reading the tag structure#

  1. printable wall art + a delivery-format tag is the backbone. In YW the pair digital download + printable wall art appears on 151 of 1,850 selling listings (8.2%) at 1.56× lift. Slot 1 carries the niche, slot 2 carries the medium, and they travel together.
  2. The high-lift pairs are not optimisation wins, they are single shops. jesus + mary at 41.1× lift, holy face of jesus + mary at 41.1×, digital wall decor + printable home decor at 43.8× — these are the tag footprint of ThePsalmistCottage and WhereFaithIs. High lift with low absolute count means one shop's house style, not a market-wide pattern. Read lift and count together; either alone misleads.
  3. The one genuinely transferable pair is niche + medium, not niche + niche. Every pair with lift between 1.5× and 4× involves one aesthetic tag and one format tag (downloadable prints + printable wall art at 4.02×, nursery wall art + playroom decor in Shops17 at 18.1×). Two aesthetics competing for the same slot is the pattern that does not show up.
  4. north prints is a brand tag, not a keyword. It appears on 861 Shops17 selling listings (21.5%) — more than any non-format tag — and pairs with printable wall art at 2.28×. One shop tags its own brand. This is brand defence working, and it is also why Shops17 lane results are one-shop artefacts.

Even after filtering to market-wide tags, NorthPrints is still the largest shop for 6 of the top 10 (including the top survivor, oil painting, at 42.9% of its listings). Shops17 does not contain a market-wide tag signal; it contains one very good shop.
  • The market-wide tag effect that does survive is medium specificity, not subject matter. Among tags no single shop owns, the ones that outperform are oil painting (50 median units, 40.3% zero), art print (27), gallery wall prints (26), printable poster (26). Compare the two biggest tags in the entire corpus — printable wall art on 6,116 YW listings and digital download on 6,238 — both at a median of 0 units and a zero rate above 81%. Those are table stakes: every seller has them and they predict nothing.
  • Young Winners yields no tag finding at all. Its market-wide best tag is american flag print at a median of 1.5 units; everything else sits at 1.0 or below, against a corpus median of 0. The top of the unfiltered list is EliteSportsStudio's football cluster (gifted footbal 100% one shop, footbal poster 100%, retro stamp poster 90% PrintsInTheLoft) — i.e. shop identity, not keyword performance. With a corpus mean of 2.82 units/listing and 78.8% of listings at zero, YW has too little signal to rank tags. Reporting a YW 'best tag' off this table would be misleading.

The one transferable structural finding from §5.3 survives all of this: slot discipline, not tag choice. Niche tag and medium tag travel together (lift 1.5–4×), and two aesthetics competing for the same slot is the pattern that never appears in the high-yield listings.

7.5 What actually killed them — the pattern, stated precisely#

Not price. Dead-shop listings had a higher median price than live-shop listings ($10.45 vs $6.49, Cliff's delta 0.185, p < 0.001). Three of the four sat at a single price point ($12.00, $4.98, $10.45) — a strategy of one-price positioning, which is a positioning failure not a discounting failure.

Not listing age. Dead-shop listings were younger (median 3 mo vs 5 mo). They had not yet had the 12–24 months that §2.2 shows is where the survival curve inflects. They closed before the payback window opened.

Concentration in one lane. Each of the four shops was a single-lane bet: vintage/retro (BloomJetsetPrints, 96 of 144 listings), nursery/kids (LinoaVintage 450 of 468; ThePlumAtelier 835 of 983), and PureVintagePrints at a single $4.98 price point. nursery/kids is the worst of the three largest YW lanes by survival — 84.1% of its listings sell nothing, against 77.5% for vintage/retro (10,805 listings) and 78.0% for wall art (3,937 listings) — and it is significantly below the YW average in the rank test (Cliff's delta -0.048, p < 0.001). Two of the four dead shops were all-in on the cohort's weakest high-volume lane, and all four closed before month 12. That is the recurring shape.

Catalogue size without a refresh loop. 144 / 468 / 804 / 983 listings. PureVintagePrints ran 804 listings on a single $4.98 price point: 3,882 in-corpus units across the whole catalogue, i.e. 4.83 units and $27 per listing — the lowest revenue per listing of the four. §6.7 shows catalogue size is monotonically linked to per-listing dilution (Spearman ρ = -0.422), and 804 listings is well into the diluting range.

The counter-example that matters. ThePlumAtelier is the hardest of the four to explain away: $88/listing, 26 mega-bundles, 50 bundles, the highest revenue per listing of any shop in either corpus including the mega-bundle strategy from §1.5. It still died at 11 months. Executing the shape ladder correctly does not protect you from closing before the survival curve inflects. That is the strongest argument in this document for treating month 12 as a hard decision point rather than an automatic continuation.

8. What the numbers support, and what they do not#

8.1 Findings strong enough to act on#

  1. Climb the shape ladder. Units/listing rises monotonically single → set → bundle → mega-bundle in both corpora (YW 2.50 → 6.05 → 7.05 → 19.83; Shops17 35.87 → 90.18 → 73.59 → 241.38). Revenue per listing rises faster still (mega-bundle ÷ single = 18.7× in YW, 19.2× in Shops17). Large effect sizes, p < 0.001, replicated independently.

  2. Treat the first 12 months as the decision window. The zero-sale share goes 88.0% → 42.6% between month 3 and month 24, and the monthly sales rate is flat from the start (§3.2). Listings do not trickle. All four dead shops closed at 8–12 months, before the survival curve inflects.

  3. Catalogue size dilutes. Median units/listing falls from 44.96 (≤100 listings) to 1.66 (900–1,500 listings), Spearman ρ = -0.422. This is a shop-level decision, not a listing-level one.

  4. Concentration is extreme. Top 10% of listings = 93.2% of YW units; top 1% = 39.9%. Do not plan on portfolio averages.

  5. Price for revenue, not volume. $15–19 is the units peak in both corpora (2.38× / 3.17× corpus average). $30+ is below average on units but 4.1× on revenue in YW. Premium works because of the price multiplier, and §6.6 shows it only works on listings with proven demand.

  6. Do not confuse the two failure modes. Catalogue bloat (§6.7: median units/listing 44.96 at ≤100 listings → 1.66 at 900–1,500, ρ = -0.422) and closing too early (§7: all four dead shops shut at 8–12 months, with listings at median age 3 months) are different problems with opposite fixes — one needs fewer listings, the other needs more time. The data shows both occurred in this cohort, sometimes in the same shop (LuxFrameStudio: 1,424 listings at 11 months).

  7. The dead shops refute the discounting hypothesis. Their median listing price was higher than live shops' ($10.45 vs $6.49, δ = 0.185, p < 0.001), and three of the four sat on a single price point. Whatever ended these shops, it was not selling cheap (§7.6).

8.2 Findings that are suggestive but NOT safe to act on#

  1. Lane selection. Only 2 of 11 YW lanes beat their complement at p < 0.05 with any real effect size (gallery wall set, δ = 0.1804; faith/christian, δ = 0.1049), while 5 of 11 are significantly below it — including the two largest lanes. And two lanes flip sign between corpora (faith/christian, coastal/landscape). Per §5.4, 43% of YW tags and 77% of Shops17 tags are carried >50% by a single shop, so lane and tag rankings in this pack are shop rankings in disguise. The keyword-derived taxonomy (§2.4) is also a fixed rule set I wrote, not ground truth.

  2. Tag strategy. The co-occurrence structure is real (niche tag + medium tag travel together at lift 1.5–4×) but the high-lift pairs are single-shop house styles, and §5.4 showed the apparent Shops17 tag signal is one shop (NorthPrints is the largest shop for 6 of the top 10 market-wide tags). Separately, with 94.6% of YW and 97.8% of Shops17 listings already carrying all 13 tags, the data cannot estimate the effect of adding tags — only of omitting them, which is a selection effect. There is no defensible 'best tag' answer here.

  3. Premium pricing at launch. §6.6 shows $30+ listings underperform sub-$5 at 0–3 months (0.55 vs 1.34 units) but this is observational: expensive listings may be expensive because the shop thinks they're proven. No causal claim is available from this data.

8.3 Questions this data cannot answer#

  1. How long until a shop matures. Shop age and listing tenure are perfectly collinear with corpus membership; the YW arm has an empty >12-month cell. Needs a shop-level panel.
  2. What causes the dead shops to die. Four cases, no control, all closed at 8–12 months. The pattern in §7.5 is consistent but n = 4 and every dead shop is also a YW shop, so "immature shop" and "closed" are not separable.
  3. Whether price or design causes sales. Price is set by the seller in response to perceived demand. Every price band comparison here is contaminated by that selection.

  4. Tag content effects. With 13 tags on nearly every listing there is no variation left to exploit, and the co-occurrence lift is dominated by individual shops' house styles.

  5. Causation anywhere. Every design here is observational on estimated sales. Shops choose their price, shape mix, lane and catalogue size in response to what they expect to sell, so the direction of every effect in this document is unidentified. §8.1 lists what the data supports, not what it proves.

  6. Whether the corpus is representative of the market at all. These are 73 shops surfaced by a Young-Winners sweep plus 17 starred shops. master_shops_final.csv records 482,528 lifetime sales for NorthPrints against 74,416 for all 56 YW shops combined — the sampling frame is not a market sample, and no weighting back to a market distribution is possible from these files.

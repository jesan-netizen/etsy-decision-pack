Faith vs Vintage — the contested lane pair, measured live

The one pair the pack argues about, priced from live page one: buy-box depth, cart pressure and what each lane's page one actually holds.

Captured: 2026-10-02 03:05–04:35 HKT · Method: live Etsy SERPs, live listing pages and live /shop/<name>/reviews pages, all rendered in the VPS headed Chrome over CDP in the default profile context with locale_override=USD|en-US|US. Every figure below was read off a live page; no web_search or cached source contributed a number.

Coverage: 8 SERPs (4 figurative/painterly faith, 2 vintage/antique, 2 explicit digital-download intent) · 112 page-one listings opened individually · 111 shop pages measured for size. Prices are the buy-box price the buyer actually pays, which is the default variant — not the card teaser.

TL;DR for the pack: the digital figurative lane is real and open, but it lives under download-intent queries at a $10.50 median, not under the painterly-imagery queries (55 of 56 slots there are physical product at a $47 median). Vintage offers no pricing relief — its slot #1 is a 600-print digital bundle at $12.82. Five shops with 0 Sales and no star line hold page-one slots, so a new shop is placeable; but 75% of page one is discounted, badges are already taken by 24%/37% Bestseller/Star Seller, and the closest live comparable (BibleSketches, 96,807 sales) sells individual figurative scripture scenes at $9.94.

This is the finding that matters most for the brief. The figurative/painterly queries almost never serve digital intent: 55 of 56 opened slots on the four figurative queries are physical canvas/print, and only one is a digital download. A digital shop optimising for painterly jesus art or figurative faith wall art is competing against sellers shipping framed product, at a $47.34 physical median — and its own $10 download has no place on that page. The digital demand in this niche lives under the explicit digital queries, where the median is $10.50 and every slot is a volume bundle. So the two candidate lanes are not alternatives on price: they are two different search intents, and the digital figurative shop has to win the explicit-download intent, not the painterly-imagery intent.

3. The digital-download lane — what a new shop actually competes with#

  • 28 page-one slots opened across the two explicit digital queries; buy-box median $10.50, IQR $6.87–$14.42, range $3.19–$62.00.
  • 73 distinct shops hold those slots. Median shop: 1,908 sales / 202 reviews.

Bundle depth is the visible unit of competition#

  • 9 of the 40 top-20 slots across the two digital queries state an explicit print count in the title. Median stated bundle size 10. The largest claims on these pages are ThatHipPrint "bundle of 250" ($13.16 buy-box) and VintageDiscoPrints "600+ Rustic Gallery Wall Prints" ($12.82, #1 on antique wall art print); the median is a modest Set of 9/Set of 10.
  • The common shape is a 9-to-12 print gallery set: 5 slots, median $10.50 (range $6.87–$26.99). That is the price/format buyers have been trained to expect here, and it is the cheap templated end of the digital page. A single-artist figurative range competes against it by being visibly different — coherent style, a real series, named compositions — not by undercutting $6.87.
  • The extreme claims are volume plays at a low headline price ($13 for 250, $12.82 for 600+), which is the opposite of a per-print-value argument: they monetise catalogue size, not craft. Against that, BibleSketches sells individual compositions at $9.94 with titles like "Jesus Feeds the 5000, DIGITAL PRINT" — which is the closest live analogue to a figurative-painterly single-artist range, and it ranks on both digital pages at a $9.94 price point with 96,807 shop sales. That is the most directly comparable business on this board.

Zero-sales shops holding digital page-one slots#

5 shops report 0 Sales and omit the star line entirely on /shop/<name>/reviews — Etsy's signature of a shop that has never sold: CherrylHardyArt, ElaraStudioAI, FaithLuxeStudio, LivingVesselDesign, Violetandvalorshop. CherrylHardyArt is the interesting one: 0 sales, 0 reviews, ranking slot #1 on figurative faith wall art with a $2,395 original acrylic painting — an unsold shop does get placed when the listing matches the query exactly.

Of those, 4 hold a slot on one of the two digital SERPs (ElaraStudioAI, FaithLuxeStudio, LivingVesselDesign, Violetandvalorshop). Caveats stated plainly: this is a single-day snapshot, a shop with a handful of sales can render as 0 on one view, and ElaraStudioAI / Violetandvalorshop / FaithLuxeStudio / LivingVesselDesign carry AI-styled shop names in a prints niche — itself a naming pattern worth noting. The conclusion is narrow but real: the digital lane is not closed to a shop with no sales history.

4. The vintage lane — one-of-a-kind physical stock, with digital bundles taking slot #1#

  • Buy-box median across the two vintage/antique queries: $46.64 (IQR $14.74–$103.64, range $7.99–$1310.06). Split by format the picture is clearer: the 19 physical slots sit at a $95.20 median, the 9 digital slots at $12.82 — i.e. 9.1x the $10.50 median of the explicit digital lane.
  • 14/28 of the opened vintage slots have zero reviews; 4 carry a Bestseller badge. Only 20/130 of the 130 cards on these two SERPs carry a Digital download badge.
  • The physical inventory is one-of-a-kind or original work that cannot be manufactured on demand: CherrylHardyArt "Pandora's Book – Original Acrylic Painting" at a $2,395 buy-box, TheTextileTreasury at $1,310, DeadLowVintage $210, VintagelyGreen $84.95, RareMapsandPrints $103 of antique maps. A brand-new digital shop holds none of this stock.
  • The #1 slot on both vintage queries is a digital download, priced at digital money. vintage botanical wall art opens with AnnaPrintablesCo "Light Naturals | A Light Vintage Aesthetic Set of 9 Gallery Prints" at a $10.99 card / buy-box price (16,368 sales, 1,676 reviews, 136 on the listing), and antique wall art print opens with VintageDiscoPrints "600+ Rustic Gallery Wall Prints" at $12.82. So the vintage queries are not sealed against digital sellers — they are won by them, at $11–13, by the largest catalogue on the page. That is the single most useful competitive fact in this lane: the way onto a "vintage" page one is a 600-print bundle, not a curated single piece.
  • Vintage is not a pricing escape hatch. The digital slots that do appear on those SERPs (AnnaPrintablesCo $10.99, VividArtPrint $8.45, GothHousePrintery $7.99, all with In 20+ carts) are priced against the $10.50 digital lane, not against the $95 physical one. Choosing vintage means choosing the same $10 catalogue race with one-of-a-kind listings stacked above you — and losing the impression of the higher ticket.

7. Zero-review slots — the launch gate#

  • Of 112 opened page-one listings, 46 (41%) have zero reviews.
  • Bestseller badge: 27/112 (24%). Star Seller badge: 41/112 (37%). Both are cumulative-sales and service-history badges, unreachable at launch — and they are what a buyer uses to justify skipping you.
  • Zero-review slots still transact. Overall median buy-box on a zero-review page-one listing is $52.50, but that figure is dragged up by physical originals — split by format it is $12.00 across the 13 digital zero-review slots and $85.00 across the 33 physical ones.
  • Only 4 of the 46 zero-review slots carry In 20+ carts, and 3 of those 4 are digital (MadetoDwellPrints $6.40, VividArtPrint $8.45, 2DBox $8.22; the fourth is physical, HomeGlamGifts $25.59). The cart-pressure badge is the incumbents' signal, and it is thin on the digital pages where a new shop would compete.

8. Recommendation for the launch decision#

  1. Vintage is not a distinct lane for a digital shop — it is the same catalogue race with one-of-a-kind stock stacked above you. Of 28 opened vintage slots, 19 are physical at a $95.20 median (original paintings, antique maps, textile lots: CherrylHardyArt $2,395, TheTextileTreasury $1,310) — inventory a new shop cannot hold. The 9 digital slots sit at $12.82, and critically the #1 slot on both vintage queries is a digital download (AnnaPrintablesCo "Set of 9" at $10.99; VintageDiscoPrints "600+ Rustic Gallery Wall Prints" at $12.82). Vintage therefore offers no pricing relief; it demands the biggest catalogue on the page to hold slot 1.
  2. Go figurative faith, but target the download intent, not the imagery intent. 55 of 56 slots on painterly jesus art / renaissance jesus wall art / figurative faith wall art / religious art print are physical product at a $47.34 median. A digital figurative shop cannot be ranked there. The addressable page is christian wall art digital download and biblical art print digital download.
  3. Treat the ad-free SERP as contested territory, not an open door. figurative faith wall art carries zero ads, but 15 of its 65 slots belong to FramedGraceHome and 14 to Riotatelier — one shop owning 14 consecutive slots on 44 sales / 8 reviews. The page is uncontested commercially and tightly held competitively. Use it to read what a figurative painterly range is priced at by physical sellers ($47 median, original paintings to $2,395), and enter through the download-intent page instead.
  4. Price and discount for the download page. Digital page-one median $10.50, IQR $6.87–$14.42; 75% of all 112 opened listings are discounted (median 32% off, list median $35.99). A launch listing at full list price advertises above almost every competitor.
  5. Model the range on BibleSketches, not on the bundle sellers. Bestseller sits on 24% of opened page-one listings and Star Seller on 37% — both unreachable at launch, and 41% of opened slots have zero reviews, so the review gate is survivable. The winning shape on the download page is named individual compositions at $9.94-$12 ("Jesus Feeds the 5000, DIGITAL PRINT"), not a 250-print catalogue: BibleSketches holds 3 slots that way on 96,807 sales, and VisionofWallart holds 2 slots on just 26 shop reviews at $4.49. Four shops holding digital page-one slots report 0 Sales and no star line at all. New shops do get placed — with tight title vocabulary, a visible discount, and no reliance on badges.
  6. Write titles in the winning vocabulary. Top style tokens on page one: painting 109, gallery 69, botanical 59, neutral 55, renaissance 28, minimalist 48, figurative 22, oil 31. Content anchors: scripture 66, verse 67, shepherd 31. Pair the style words with the scripture anchor and an explicit digital download / printable term — the download intent is what the ranking filter keys on.

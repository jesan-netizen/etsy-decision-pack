Alfie's Doctrine

1. Etsy Ads — the hard numbers#

Gating, all three: shop open a few weeks, ≥2 organic sales (5 ideal), ≥25 listings ("non-negotiable") — ads before social proof tell Etsy "you've got a terrible conversion rate." What to advertise: only high buyer-intent signals — "if we pay for 10 clicks, at least one will buy." 10–20 listings max, never more than 3 pages, screened on favourites/sales/visits. [V] ADS3, ADS5, ADS6

The 60-day test (3 × 9 listings) — the corpus's most decision-relevant data: 60-day orders bestsellers 110, normal 29, new 32; bestseller clicks ~5× normal; CPC lower for bestsellers AND new listings, normal worst; ROAS 2.06 bestsellers, 2.15 new. Never advertise average performers. [V] ADS7

Ad-strategy verdict. Efficient spending is most profitable: avg CPC 55¢, best ROAS. Greater visibility: CPC 71¢, ROAS 1.79 — more orders, less profit; reserve for Q4 on high-margin products. Lower click cost: zero benefit. [V] ADS7

ROAS bars conflict across his own files: ADS4/ADS5 digital ≥1.1 profit, 1.0 break-even, POD 3.5+ (ideally 4); ADS7 "at least 2… digital above 1.2"; member practice kill at 10 clicks under 1.7 (GC 16-08-2026). Use 1.1 kill-floor, 2.0 scaling target.

Budget: start $3/day; never raise until spend hits the cap daily AND ROAS is positive — if Etsy won't spend it, the catalog isn't good enough. Most shops can't hold $5/day; then $10, wait, repeat. On-call escalation: $1 → $3/day. [V] ADS5; QP CTR: target 1–3%; <0.8% at 500+ views is a problem. CPC >40–50¢ means Etsy distrusts the product. [V] ADS4, ADS5

2. Q4 doctrine#

Shape: up to 75% of a year's sales in the last 25%. Conversion rate is the mechanism — his mug shop runs ~7%, hit 12.1% Nov–Dec; a 3% shop drifts to 5–6%. [V] Q4C

September is the work month, not the selling month: "September is the month you work hardest, or you come out of Q4 wishing you'd done 3× more." Rankings formed in September carry Oct–Dec; a late-Nov listing competes against listings already wearing a bestseller tag. [V] L26 §1

Hard rules: never delete or deactivate listings in Q4, including zero-sale renewals (January is the cleanup); don't chop borderline ads in October — leave them to see November; conversion rates double across all of Etsy in Nov–Dec, his ads going ROAS ~2.2 → over 4 untouched, with the stated regret that he should have raised budgets hard in late October. [V] QP; L26 §2

"Q4 product" ≠ "Christmas product." His surprise winners were a shark mug and a dinosaur product — traffic was entirely gift-intent ("gift for shark lover", "diving gift"). ~80% of September listings should be Q4- or gift-specific; rest evergreen. [V] QP

Lead time: physical seasonal 2–3 months ahead, digital 3–4 months. Volume target: 301 → 400 by end of September in his example; beginners stay at 1–2 product types. [V] Q4C September discount: accept ~5% less profit so products sit high on 1 October; digital equivalent is a visible strikethrough. Reviews are the gate: zero reviews = "absolute hard mode", 3–4 reviews = social proof; message every customer from the last two months pre-Q4. [V] Q4C; L26 §§4, 8 Personalisation is a Q4 lever; add urgency phrasing ("order before 10 December"). Digital sellers skip shipping profiles. Psychology: flat sales are normal; 100+ listings minimum before judging yourself. [V] Q4C; WSSL

3. Pricing doctrine#

Sunrise pricing. List low → conversion and sales velocity climb; velocity is the number-one ranking factor. He explicitly rejects break-even pricing — start at a small profit. [V] TIPS Digital form: baseline price → first 2 sales on that one product → raise to final. Applied one product at a time; a member who raised shop-wide was told to undo it. Final price = a margin you're happy with, not a set percentage. [V] SEP9 Physical contrast: ~$1 profit until 2 sales, then $5/sale minimum, $10–20 on larger canvases; his first 10–12 listings earned nothing. "Sorry, digital product sellers." [V] L26 §3

The blunt digital price doctrine (Joy: 219 listings, 3 months, zero sales). £4.19 (~$5) for a single JPEG was "prohibiting" sales — "shoppers can see a lot more value at a lot lower price." Fix: make 10–20 images per subject, list each individually at ~$2, then bundle and charge slightly more. "Sometimes you're better off making 10 sales at $2 than zero sales at $5." [V] JOY Craig's calibration: search the term, find the most expensive listing (top anchor) and the cheapest, start at break-even and walk toward the middle, then toward the top once it sells. [V] JOY

Digital price ladder: singles ~$2; $3–4 singles named as the slow trap [V] JOY, GC 16-07-2026 · mid bundles $10–12 — "that's what's going to give you the revenue" [V] GC 16-08-2026 · mega/seasonal bundle 500+ PNGs at $15–20, "10–20 sales a day with that product alone" [V] JUL1 · whole shop bundle $40 floor, "start at 39.99" [V] GC 16-08-2026 · a live member's shop: average $8–10, bestsellers $40–$50 [V] AUG5 · at $50–60, 2–3 sales/day is full-time income vs 100+/day at $2–3 [V] GC 18-12-2025 · bundles also buy ad headroom before break-even [V] GC 16-08-2026. $1 Deals section (digital only): new or long-dead products, 5–10% of listings, never more. [V] TIPS

4. Listing cadence#

  • One listing a day is good numbers — provided every listing is researched and targeted (his answer to two members). September is quality over quantity; never sacrifice quality for count. [V] QP; L26 §9
  • Digital needs more volume than POD and takes longer — 2+ months of listing; 58 digital wall art listings is "really low." Judge by output, not calendar time. [V] QP; L26 §9
  • 20-minute rule: if a design isn't good enough in 20 minutes, the concept is wrong — ship it anyway. Systemise listing text: fixed title/description template, only design-specific keywords change; base tags preloaded so 3–4 slots change per listing. [V] TIME
  • 70/30 split: 70% research-backed, 30% what you want to make — but no huge disparities. A scenic-nature shop that suddenly posts a dragon "could look a little bit" off. This binds any niche. [V] GC 16-02-2026

5. Mockup and presentation rules#

Hierarchy. Four image types: product photo, lifestyle, info card, infographic; wall art lifestyle = art on a mantelpiece. Default sequence: thumbnail (attract) → lifestyle → clarification → evaluation (does it fit me) → risk/reassurance LAST → how to order (last two swap for a wallet-in-hand buyer). Artwork warrants more gallery images than mugs. [V] MC Hard caps: one idea per card, scannable in seconds, no walls of text, no tiny or script fonts, design for phone. 80% of effort goes into the thumbnail. Infocards "cannot fix an unattractive thumbnail, poor photography, confusing variations, or weak positioning and pricing." Buyers don't read descriptions — Sharda: "99% of buyers… do not read the descriptions… they rely on the listing photos." Digital-specific: spell out what they get, how they download it, and that it's automatic. [V] MC, SHA; L26 §13

Mockup doctrine, three layers: 1. Sharda (wall-art specialist): vary it — living room, bedroom, far, close, a hand holding it, on a table, small frame to large frame — "whatever variety I can show, still keeping the look and feel premium," and don't repeat the same mockup across listings. [V] SHA 2. Alfie (narrowing): group mockups (light/dark), measure CTR and sales per group, and if no pattern emerges cut to exactly three — A, B, C — and test every future product. [V] MC 3. Q4 overlay: Q4-specific mockups from 1 September — a blurred Christmas tree in the background, even on non-Christmas products — is how a new shop beats a ranked shop; 4–5-year-old shops are losing because presentation hasn't moved on. Match the mockup household to the art: dark moody art should not sit in a bright Scandinavian room. [V] QP; L26 §6

Workflow: AI-generate → Fiverr converts to PSD (~$4) → reuse forever with QuickFlow batch scripts. On a live listing, never touch what is selling — swap only low performers. Off-brand mockups are fixable: change only the background. Hands in mockups convert better (community poll). Banner: a carousel of the actual art, not a logo; match banner/logo/infographic colours and fonts. [V] L26 §6; MC; GOOD; JOY Thumbnail noise self-sabotages: four lines of spec text on a thumbnail ("watercolor, clip art, 300 DPI, HD quality") — "a lot of that information you just really don't need." [V] GC 16-02-2026

6. Bundles — the loudest signal in the pack#

  • Priority #1 for digital sellers is ONE page-one-ranking mega bundle, better than the competition's; September builds the smaller bundles feeding it. "One high-quality page-one product beats ten average ones on page 3-4." [V] QP; L26 §12
  • The ladder: singles → themed packs ($10–12) → mega/seasonal ($15–20) → whole shop ($40+). "Think of everything as pushing the customer to the next level." His clipart shop: ~115 listings, good revenue because listings drove to bundles, which drove to the whole shop bundle. Etsy has no real cross-sell tools, so the mechanism is infographic cards: buy-2/buy-3 X% off plus a card funnelling to the whole shop bundle, on every low-ticket listing. Pattern extraction: existing tiger/lion/giraffe sets become a safari pack as a new listing over the same assets. [V] GC 16-08-2026; L26 §12
  • Whole shop bundle is priority #1, above seasonal: "treat your whole shop bundle as the number one product that we're selling." Delivery: one Drive folder of product folders; the buyer gets the parent link and cannot traverse upward. Bundle breadth beats narrowness: "middle tier to broad" — one general Baby Shower bundle, not lemon-vs-citrus variants. [V] JUL1, JUL15

7. Wall art and digital specifically#

  • Wall art / Frame TV is under-saturated vs POD. Mugs "so saturated… a ton of new shops every week," but "frame TV and printable wall arts are still growing and not saturated." Midjourney is the wall-art generator — "people are expecting the paintings feeling." (Alfie uses Leonardo for clipart.) [V] SHA
  • Catalogue volume is the gate: Sharda's ~550 listings produced results from the top 50; the other 500 sat there. "If you stick yourself to just 50 listings… you don't understand what your buyer is looking for." [V] SHA
  • Digital wall art compounds late then fast: Sharda ~$25 after months, then $19k in a month. [V] QP
  • Digital levers differ: no shipping profile or production buffer — the levers are the strikethrough discount, download-clarity infographics and mockup quality. Personalised wall art is structurally giftable, so gift tags ("gift for dad", secret Santa) matter as much as the seasonal art. [V] Q4C; L26 §§4, 5, 13

8. CONTRADICTIONS against the current plan#

1. Bundle-first launch — CONTRADICTED, hard; the biggest divergence in the pack. The doctrine is the reverse: singles are the raw material; the mega bundle is what you build in September to be ready for the Q4 peak, and a bundle with no catalogue has nothing to bundle. "You've just got to get the quantity there first, to build"; wall art needs 2+ months of consistent listing and 58 listings is "really low." The bundle only pays once singles exist: "you'll see patterns in your own listings… you could then do a safari animal set." Against bundle-first: "Spend the whole of September doing all of the smaller bundles so that I then had a really high quality mega bundle product." Expect a silent period after a bundle-first launch — do not read it as a niche failure. [V] QP; L26 §§1, 9, 12; GC 16-08-2026; JUL1

2. Painterly no-text style — PARTIALLY SUPPORTED, with one direct caution. Supported: Midjourney "because people are expecting the paintings feeling"; painterly is a named, prompts-friendly style (Kittl's "ultra realistic painterly style", warm palette, brush-stroke texture; a member used "a painterly, kind of like Van Gogh background"); the 70/30 rule permits distinctive work when research-adjacent. Caution: the hardest presentation rule in the corpus is that the thumbnail must be clean — four lines of spec text was called out as self-sabotage. "No text" is right for the art and must not become licence for typographic clutter on the listing images. The mockup-match rule also cuts against a uniform house style: dark moody art should not sit in a bright Scandinavian room, with mockups matching the design's vibe while infographics stay constant across the brand. A tight painterly range is defensible; a wide range with random staging is not. [V] SHA; Kittl/Artwork; GC 16-07-2026; GC 16-02-2026; MC; QP

3. Prices US$22.05 / 12.99 / 9.99 — CONTRADICTED on the top price; the lower two defensible with re-scoping. Every digital number Alfie quotes sits far below 22.05: singles ~$2 (his explicit fix for a zero-sale wall art shop), $3–4 singles named as the slow trap, mid bundles $10–12, mega $15–20, whole shop $40 floor; a live member's shop averages $8–10 with bestsellers at $40–50. The £4.19 single was diagnosed as the cause of a 219-listing, zero-sale shop on value-per-price versus competitors. At $22.05 we ask the buyer to beat the whole-shop tier on a single image; $12.99 works only at the top of the $10–12 bundle band and $9.99 only if it genuinely is a bundle. Recommendation: demote 22.05 to the whole-shop tier (~$40+) and re-cut as ~$2–4 single / ~$10–12 bundle / ~$15–20 mega / ~$40+ whole shop. [V] JOY; GC 16-07-2026; GC 16-08-2026; JUL1; AUG5; GC 18-12-2025

4. Faith niche — NOT a contradiction, but a governed choice. Nothing in the corpus advocates or forbids a faith niche. The binding constraint is the cohesion rule: a majority-faith shop that suddenly posts a dragon "could look a little bit" off. Faith members are advised normally — one member's shop is "faith-based and also seasonal", treated as a coherent sellable pairing. The real risk is seasonal dilution: with ~80% of September listings needing to be Q4/gift-specific, a pure faith niche must find its gift bridge (gift-for-her, housewarming, Christmas scripture) or miss the window. Our gift-angle tagging is the right mitigation. [V] GC 16-02-2026; GC 16-01-2026; QP

5. Loss-leader tension. A discounted launch is defensible; a permanently under-priced catalogue is not. He tolerates loss leaders in other people's shops but not for his own members — "I'd rather you just make the highest quality product on the market… I only actually made profit on five of them." [V] SEP9

9. What to copy#

  • Seasonal lane, 20.9% of lane-keyword hits. Seasonality is a reskin engine, not a lane: the same template re-dated for the next occasion is how a flat catalogue keeps a revenue line without new design work.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 4.0% of listings and 7.2% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $4.98 median as a deliberate floor. Everything sits at the bottom of the ladder; the folder's own teardown puts digital-only at a $5.5-15k/mo ceiling in this set, so this is the traffic model, not the money model.
  • Worked power law. Top 50 = 27.2% of units, top 5 = 17.2%. A handful of listings carry the shop while the catalogue does the discoverability work.
  • Two-family lane: holiday / seasonal leads, vintage / retro second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • This shop is closed (status: DEAD (closed, verified 30 Sep), verified 30 Sep). Treat it as a post-mortem, not a model. 804 listings and 3,337 six-month sales say the playbook ran and the shop still did not survive - read the what was copied column for the mechanics and ignore the outcome.
  • Cheap-single trap. $4.98 median across 804 listings is the slowest, most decay-prone model in the folder (NorthPrints took 73 months and 1,795 listings to reach what LoveDictionary does with 1,205 in 46). Volume feels productive and is the weakest shape in the data.
  • Tag diffusion. Only 8.6% of filled tag slots fall in the top 10 tags across 804 listings - 4,637 distinct tags for 804 listings. No phrase is being defended, so the shop is paying for 13 slots per listing and defending none of them.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (7.2% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads cottagecore / farmhouse (19.7% of its style markers) inside the holiday / seasonal lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 84.6% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 84.6% dead tail on 804 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 27.2% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $4.98), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 371 units per month of age over 9 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The biggest lesson is that it closed. Mechanics transfer between shops; the outcome does not. Copy the shape, not the size, and monitor refresh rate rather than listing count.
  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

single-print, CLOSED - $3,292/mo (master CSV). Engine B (single-print volume digital) in the folder framework. Mechanics worth lifting: the holiday / seasonal lane discipline and the 7.2%-of-units bundle ratio. Do not lift the catalogue size - 804 listings and 3,337 six-month sales did not keep the shop open.

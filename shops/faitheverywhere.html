9. What to copy#

  • Faith lane, 79.1% of lane-keyword hits. The lane is evergreen in this folder - it holds a 6-month family share of 1,542 units across the 17 starred shops - and it personalises at a higher price than the median print.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 1.2% of listings and 0.0% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • Count-anchored titles. 19.6% of the catalogue leads with a number plus a noun (3 of the top 20 do). The count hook is the most consistent title formula across all 17 shops in the folder.
  • $10 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 48.2% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 89.7% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: faith / christian leads, humour / typography quote second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Catalogue duplication: 84.9% of listings sit in near-duplicate title groups (77 groups, 281 listings; same first 45 characters of the title). Whatever this does to ranking, it is not something to copy, and near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit.
  • 89.7% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.
  • No bundle ladder and a young catalogue. Bundles are 0.0% of units and 42.0% of listings are under 3 months old - the listings are too new to have been refreshed and there is no set tier to catch the buyers who want more than one print.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (0.0% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads typography / quote-led (79.3% of its style markers) inside the faith / christian lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 89.7% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 89.7% dead tail on 331 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 48.2% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $9-13 band (this shop's median is $10), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 156 units per month of age over 12 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • Do not copy the listing duplication (84.9% of listings in near-identical title groups). Near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit, not a ranking trick.

12. Verdict#

single-print, hero-concentrated - $4,526/mo (master CSV). Long-tail digital catalogue - not one of the folder A/B/C/D engines, because the defining feature is the dead tail rather than a bundle ladder, a price tier or a physical line. A hero shop, not a catalogue shop: the top 50 listings carry 48.2% of units while 89.7% of the catalogue sells nothing. Design for the hero, run the rest as discovery.

9. What to copy#

  • A 20-listing catalogue, not a catalogue model. At this size the whole shop is a handful of products and every share below is a share of a tiny denominator. Read the top-5 listings and the price point; ignore any 'catalogue strategy' framing.
  • Bundle-backward, executed. 51.1% of measured units come from the 30.0% of listings that are bundles, and 6 of the top 20 listings by units are bundles. That is the Lumiora / LoveDictionary / HarmonyPixels shape: design the mega set first, let the singles feed it.
  • Count-anchored titles. 30.0% of the catalogue leads with a number plus a noun (6 of the top 20 do). The count hook is the most consistent title formula across all 17 shops in the folder.
  • $19.96 median - above the volume band. Buyers here are paying for a specific room, a specific style, or personalisation. The $17-36 tier is the money tier in the folder's price ladder.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 20 listings = 98.3% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 35.0% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Personalisation is the AOV engine. 95.0% of listings are custom/personalised and they take 100.0% of units. The folder's read: personalised digital is the bridge between cheap printables and the physical endgame.
  • Two-family lane: personalised / pet leads, humour / kitchen-bathroom second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Personalised lane, decayed. 100.0% of units are custom listings, but the median listing is 11.0 months old - the folder's fresh 30-day check rates the two personalised shops in it at 0.32-0.33x their own six-month pace. The shape is the highest-AOV pattern found and also the one decaying fastest.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Build the mega bundle first and let the singles feed it. This shop gets 51.1% of its units from the 30.0% of listings that are sets. The order matters: design the 200-print set, then extract the 20 component singles from it, then bundle again at 5.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • This shop earns $1,822.07 per listing without committing to an aesthetic. The corpus-wide comparison: shops with no named aesthetic earn far less per listing than shops that name one. Its lane (personalised / pet) is the borrowable part, not its undefined style.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 35.0% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 35.0% dead tail on 20 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 20 listings take 98.3% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $14-20 money band (this shop's median is $19.96), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 72 units per month of age over 12 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

bundle-led, personalisation-led, hero-concentrated - $6,074/mo (master CSV). Engine A (bundle-led digital) in the folder framework. Too small to call a model: 20 listings, top 5 = 92.5% of units, so the whole business is a few products. Copy the price point and the offer shape, nothing structural.

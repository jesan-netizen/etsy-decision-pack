9. What to copy#

  • Vintage-family lane, 25.6% of lane-keyword hits. Vintage/Antique is the largest recent family in the folder at 7,758 six-month units - the deepest proven demand pool in this data.
  • Bundle-backward, executed. 84.5% of measured units come from the 21.3% of listings that are bundles, and 19 of the top 50 listings by units are bundles. That is the Lumiora / LoveDictionary / HarmonyPixels shape: design the mega set first, let the singles feed it.
  • Count-anchored titles. 16.0% of the catalogue leads with a number plus a noun (13 of the top 20 do). The count hook is the most consistent title formula across all 17 shops in the folder.
  • $5.8 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 82.7% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 6.3% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • 6.3% of listings have zero measured sales - the lowest dead-tail share in this set. That is the sign of a catalogue that is being refreshed, not merely expanded.
  • Aged, seasoned catalogue. Median listing age 30.0 months across 300 listings; the winners have had years to compound.
  • Two-family lane: botanical / floral leads, abstract / minimal second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Nothing in this shop's own numbers trips a structural warning. The risks here are the generic ones: fee stack on low price points, and the fact that a single-lane shop has no second leg when the lane cools.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Build the mega bundle first and let the singles feed it. This shop gets 84.5% of its units from the 21.3% of listings that are sets. The order matters: design the 200-print set, then extract the 20 component singles from it, then bundle again at 5.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads abstract / geometric / bauhaus (34.5% of its style markers) inside the botanical / floral lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 6.3% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 6.3% dead tail on 300 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 82.7% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $5.8), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 676 units per month of age over 39 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

bundle-led, hero-concentrated, low dead-tail - USD 13,281 in 30 days (Everbee, USD). Engine A (bundle-led digital) in the folder framework. The launch shape: bundles carry 84.5% of units and the catalogue is still refreshing.

9. What to copy#

  • Nursery/kids lane, the dominant family at 38.8% of lane-keyword hits. Nursery is the second-deepest proven family in the folder (2,572 six-month units across the 17) and it takes a price premium because buyers are decorating a room, not buying one print.
  • Bundles as the margin tier, not the whole shop. 44.6% of units from 39.1% of listings, with 22 bundles in the top 50 - the ladder is working without the tail being dead weight.
  • $16.56 median - above the volume band. Buyers here are paying for a specific room, a specific style, or personalisation. The $17-36 tier is the money tier in the folder's price ladder.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 60.6% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 71.8% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: nursery / kids leads, vintage / retro second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Nothing in this shop's own numbers trips a structural warning. The risks here are the generic ones: fee stack on low price points, and the fact that a single-lane shop has no second leg when the lane cools.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Bundles are the margin tier here, not the whole shop (44.6% of units from 39.1% of listings). Build a $5-8 single tier for traffic and a $17-36 set tier for margin, and check monthly that the set tier is taking unit share.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads cottagecore / farmhouse (54.0% of its style markers) inside the nursery / kids lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 71.8% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 71.8% dead tail on 376 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 60.6% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $14-20 money band (this shop's median is $16.56), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 453 units per month of age over 38 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • Do not copy the listing duplication (35.6% of listings in near-identical title groups). Near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit, not a ranking trick.

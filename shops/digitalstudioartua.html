9. What to copy#

  • SVG / apparel lane, 47.2% of lane-keyword hits. SVG/Cut-file = 1,789 six-month units in the folder and the lowest price band here - a volume-and-breadth play, not a margin play.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 4.3% of listings and 0.8% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $3.37 median as a deliberate floor. Everything sits at the bottom of the ladder; the folder's own teardown puts digital-only at a $5.5-15k/mo ceiling in this set, so this is the traffic model, not the money model.
  • Worked power law. Top 50 = 33.1% of units, top 5 = 22.4%. A handful of listings carry the shop while the catalogue does the discoverability work.
  • Two-family lane: svg / apparel leads, faith / christian second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Cheap-single trap. $3.37 median across 1,862 listings is the slowest, most decay-prone model in the folder (NorthPrints took 73 months and 1,795 listings to reach what LoveDictionary does with 1,205 in 46). Volume feels productive and is the weakest shape in the data.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (0.8% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads minimalist line / single subject (19.7% of its style markers) inside the svg / apparel lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 83.3% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 83.3% dead tail on 1,678 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 33.1% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a sub-$3.50 traffic floor (this shop's median is $3.37), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 697 units per month of age over 30 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

13. Sources and caveats#

  • Listing units here are lifetime (Est. Total Sales), because shops17_listings.csv has no 6-month window. Percentages of units are therefore lifetime shares, not recent-velocity shares.
  • Cross-reference (28 Sep 2026 Momentum Layer note, not from these CSVs): 2,867 units in 6 months, 12-month 8,402, state coasting, recent-family split SVG/Cut-file 1,789 + Christian/Religious 706.
  • Prices are the CSV's Price (hidden) column. Everbee estimates, ~1 month lag; the folder notes ~95% accuracy.
  • Row-count gap, disclosed: master_shops_final.csv reports 1,862 listings for this shop, but the listing CSV carries 1,678 rows (-184). Every listing-level share in this file uses the 1,678-row denominator, because that is what the listing columns actually cover. Treat the master figure as the shop-level count and the row figure as the analysable sample - the two are not the same population.
  • Bundle share, count-anchoring, duplicate-title and IP figures are computed here from the title and tag columns of the listing CSVs using the classifiers stated in each section. They are not Everbee fields, so they are estimates with a stated rule, not ground truth.
  • Prior shop comment carried in master_shops_final.csv: "890 sales in 30 days at a USD 5.02 implied average, the cheapest basket here, on 1,862 listings. Conversion 4.39 is the second highest, and 22.8 views per lifetime sale is the most efficient traffic in the group."

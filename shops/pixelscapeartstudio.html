9. What to copy#

  • A 14-listing catalogue, not a catalogue model. At this size the whole shop is a handful of products and every share below is a share of a tiny denominator. Read the top-5 listings and the price point; ignore any 'catalogue strategy' framing.
  • Nursery/kids lane, the dominant family at 50.6% of lane-keyword hits. Nursery is the second-deepest proven family in the folder (2,572 six-month units across the 17) and it takes a price premium because buyers are decorating a room, not buying one print.
  • Bundle-backward, executed. 100.0% of measured units come from the 85.7% of listings that are bundles, and 12 of the top 14 listings by units are bundles. That is the Lumiora / LoveDictionary / HarmonyPixels shape: design the mega set first, let the singles feed it.
  • Count-anchored titles. 85.7% of the catalogue leads with a number plus a noun (12 of the top 20 do). The count hook is the most consistent title formula across all 17 shops in the folder.
  • $14.25 median - above the volume band. Buyers here are paying for a specific room, a specific style, or personalisation. The $17-36 tier is the money tier in the folder's price ladder.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 14 listings = 100.0% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 42.9% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: nursery / kids leads, humour / kitchen-bathroom second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • IP risk, quantified: 35.7% of listings and 66.6% of units carry a licensed term (pokemon, toy story, winnie the pooh). The folder's own finding is that 27.6% of young-shop rows ride licensed IP, that velocity is bought with takedown risk, and that zero-IP is the house rule. Do not model this revenue.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Build the mega bundle first and let the singles feed it. This shop gets 100.0% of its units from the 85.7% of listings that are sets. The order matters: design the 200-print set, then extract the 20 component singles from it, then bundle again at 5.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • This shop earns $880.01 per listing without committing to an aesthetic. The corpus-wide comparison: shops with no named aesthetic earn far less per listing than shops that name one. Its lane (nursery / kids) is the borrowable part, not its undefined style.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 42.9% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 42.9% dead tail on 14 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 14 listings take 100.0% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $14-20 money band (this shop's median is $14.25), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 86 units per month of age over 10 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • This shop's subject matter is licensed (35.7% of listings, 66.6% of units). You can learn its mechanics and none of its subjects. Rebuild the lane with clean IP.
  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

IP-riding, bundle-led, hero-concentrated - $2,053/mo (master CSV). Engine A (bundle-led digital) in the folder framework. The revenue is real but it is licensed-IP revenue (66.6% of units): unusable as a model and unsafe to copy. Rebuild the lane, not the shop.

9. What to copy#

  • Nursery/kids lane, the dominant family at 20.9% of lane-keyword hits. Nursery is the second-deepest proven family in the folder (2,572 six-month units across the 17) and it takes a price premium because buyers are decorating a room, not buying one print.
  • Bundle-backward, executed. 73.1% of measured units come from the 20.7% of listings that are bundles, and 38 of the top 50 listings by units are bundles. That is the Lumiora / LoveDictionary / HarmonyPixels shape: design the mega set first, let the singles feed it.
  • $12.61 median - above the volume band. Buyers here are paying for a specific room, a specific style, or personalisation. The $17-36 tier is the money tier in the folder's price ladder.
  • Worked power law. Top 50 = 36.3% of units, top 5 = 24.8%. A handful of listings carry the shop while the catalogue does the discoverability work.
  • Two-family lane: nursery / kids leads, coastal / beach second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Nothing in this shop's own numbers trips a structural warning. The risks here are the generic ones: fee stack on low price points, and the fact that a single-lane shop has no second leg when the lane cools.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Build the mega bundle first and let the singles feed it. This shop gets 73.1% of its units from the 20.7% of listings that are sets. The order matters: design the 200-print set, then extract the 20 component singles from it, then bundle again at 5.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads coastal / coastal-grandmother (25.5% of its style markers) inside the nursery / kids lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 67.2% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 67.2% dead tail on 1,154 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 36.3% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $9-13 band (this shop's median is $12.61), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 820 units per month of age over 46 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

  • Everbee figures for this shop are in EUR, native. No FX conversion is applied, so cross-shop revenue comparison is approximate.
  • Row-count gap, disclosed: master_shops_final.csv reports 1,205 listings for this shop, but the listing CSV carries 1,154 rows (-51). Every listing-level share in this file uses the 1,154-row denominator, because that is what the listing columns actually cover. Treat the master figure as the shop-level count and the row figure as the analysable sample - the two are not the same population.

9. What to copy#

  • Vintage-family lane, 56.8% of lane-keyword hits. Vintage/Antique is the largest recent family in the folder at 7,758 six-month units - the deepest proven demand pool in this data.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 0.0% of listings and 0.0% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $10 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 74.3% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 94.9% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.

10. What to avoid#

  • Catalogue duplication: 61.4% of listings sit in near-duplicate title groups (48 groups, 218 listings; same first 45 characters of the title). Whatever this does to ranking, it is not something to copy, and near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit.
  • 94.9% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.
  • No bundle ladder and a young catalogue. Bundles are 0.0% of units and 51.0% of listings are under 3 months old - the listings are too new to have been refreshed and there is no set tier to catch the buyers who want more than one print.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (0.0% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads vintage / antique oil painting (50.6% of its style markers) inside the vintage / retro lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 94.9% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 94.9% dead tail on 355 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 74.3% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $9-13 band (this shop's median is $10), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 82 units per month of age over 11 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • This shop's subject matter is licensed (5.9% of listings, 4.7% of units). You can learn its mechanics and none of its subjects. Rebuild the lane with clean IP.
  • Do not copy the listing duplication (61.4% of listings in near-identical title groups). Near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit, not a ranking trick.
  • A 94.9% dead tail with almost no bundles is the profile of a catalogue that expanded without ever building the money tier. That is the specific failure to design against.
  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

single-print, hero-concentrated - $1,505/mo (master CSV). Hero-plus-dead-tail: one runaway listing over a catalogue that mostly sells nothing. A hero shop, not a catalogue shop: the top 50 listings carry 74.3% of units while 94.9% of the catalogue sells nothing. Design for the hero, run the rest as discovery.

9. What to copy#

  • Vintage-family lane, 30.2% of lane-keyword hits. Vintage/Antique is the largest recent family in the folder at 7,758 six-month units - the deepest proven demand pool in this data.
  • Bundles as the margin tier, not the whole shop. 27.1% of units from 7.0% of listings, with 6 bundles in the top 50 - the ladder is working without the tail being dead weight.
  • $4 median as a deliberate floor. Everything sits at the bottom of the ladder; the folder's own teardown puts digital-only at a $5.5-15k/mo ceiling in this set, so this is the traffic model, not the money model.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 51.9% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 93.8% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: vintage / retro leads, abstract / minimal second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • 93.8% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.
  • Tag diffusion. Only 6.2% of filled tag slots fall in the top 10 tags across 512 listings - 2,297 distinct tags for 512 listings. No phrase is being defended, so the shop is paying for 13 slots per listing and defending none of them.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Bundles are the margin tier here, not the whole shop (27.1% of units from 7.0% of listings). Build a $5-8 single tier for traffic and a $17-36 set tier for margin, and check monthly that the set tier is taking unit share.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads minimalist line / single subject (17.0% of its style markers) inside the vintage / retro lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 93.8% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 93.8% dead tail on 512 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 51.9% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a sub-$3.50 traffic floor (this shop's median is $4), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 101 units per month of age over 11 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

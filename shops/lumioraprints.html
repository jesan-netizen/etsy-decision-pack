9. What to copy#

  • Frame-TV art lane, 41.8% of lane-keyword hits. Frame TV / TV art = 3,176 six-month units in the folder. At a $5 median it is a traffic floor, and it works because the buyer already owns the frame.
  • Bundle-backward, executed. 85.4% of measured units come from the 27.9% of listings that are bundles, and 32 of the top 50 listings by units are bundles. That is the Lumiora / LoveDictionary / HarmonyPixels shape: design the mega set first, let the singles feed it.
  • Count-anchored titles. 26.9% of the catalogue leads with a number plus a noun (18 of the top 20 do). The count hook is the most consistent title formula across all 17 shops in the folder.
  • $5 median as a deliberate floor. Everything sits at the bottom of the ladder; the folder's own teardown puts digital-only at a $5.5-15k/mo ceiling in this set, so this is the traffic model, not the money model.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 60.0% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 82.4% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: frame tv art leads, holiday / seasonal second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Nothing in this shop's own numbers trips a structural warning. The risks here are the generic ones: fee stack on low price points, and the fact that a single-lane shop has no second leg when the lane cools.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Build the mega bundle first and let the singles feed it. This shop gets 85.4% of its units from the 27.9% of listings that are sets. The order matters: design the 200-print set, then extract the 20 component singles from it, then bundle again at 5.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads vintage / antique oil painting (24.8% of its style markers) inside the frame tv art lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 82.4% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 82.4% dead tail on 323 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 60.0% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $5), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 334 units per month of age over 11 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

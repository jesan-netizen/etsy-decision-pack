9. What to copy#

  • Vintage-family lane, 19.6% of lane-keyword hits. Vintage/Antique is the largest recent family in the folder at 7,758 six-month units - the deepest proven demand pool in this data.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 2.1% of listings and 1.8% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $4.99 median as a deliberate floor. Everything sits at the bottom of the ladder; the folder's own teardown puts digital-only at a $5.5-15k/mo ceiling in this set, so this is the traffic model, not the money model.
  • Worked power law. Top 50 = 23.5% of units, top 5 = 16.6%. A handful of listings carry the shop while the catalogue does the discoverability work.
  • Two-family lane: vintage / retro leads, botanical / floral second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Cheap-single trap. $4.99 median across 804 listings is the slowest, most decay-prone model in the folder (NorthPrints took 73 months and 1,795 listings to reach what LoveDictionary does with 1,205 in 46). Volume feels productive and is the weakest shape in the data.
  • Tag diffusion. Only 7.5% of filled tag slots fall in the top 10 tags across 804 listings - 4,723 distinct tags for 804 listings. No phrase is being defended, so the shop is paying for 13 slots per listing and defending none of them.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (1.8% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads botanical / floral study (20.9% of its style markers) inside the vintage / retro lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 57.6% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 57.6% dead tail on 809 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 23.5% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $4.99), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 1,092 units per month of age over 18 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

13. Sources and caveats#

  • Cross-reference (28 Sep 2026 Momentum Layer note, not from these CSVs): 7,333 units in 6 months, 12-month 18,181, state mixed, recent-family split Vintage/Antique 3,040 + Minimalist 970.
  • Prices are the CSV's Price (hidden) column. Everbee estimates, ~1 month lag; the folder notes ~95% accuracy.
  • Row-count gap, disclosed: master_shops_final.csv reports 804 listings for this shop, but the listing CSV carries 809 rows (+5). Every listing-level share in this file uses the 809-row denominator, because that is what the listing columns actually cover. Treat the master figure as the shop-level count and the row figure as the analysable sample - the two are not the same population.
  • Bundle share, count-anchoring, duplicate-title and IP figures are computed here from the title and tag columns of the listing CSVs using the classifiers stated in each section. They are not Everbee fields, so they are estimates with a stated rule, not ground truth.

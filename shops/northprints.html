9. What to copy#

  • Vintage-family lane, 24.9% of lane-keyword hits. Vintage/Antique is the largest recent family in the folder at 7,758 six-month units - the deepest proven demand pool in this data.
  • Bundles as the margin tier, not the whole shop. 23.5% of units from 11.8% of listings, with 13 bundles in the top 50 - the ladder is working without the tail being dead weight.
  • $5.66 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Flat distribution. Top 50 = 13.9% of units, top 5 = 8.9%, with only 32.0% of listings dead - no hero carries the shop, which is the healthiest shape in the folder and the hardest to build on purpose.
  • Two-family lane: vintage / retro leads, botanical / floral second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Nothing in this shop's own numbers trips a structural warning. The risks here are the generic ones: fee stack on low price points, and the fact that a single-lane shop has no second leg when the lane cools.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Bundles are the margin tier here, not the whole shop (23.5% of units from 11.8% of listings). Build a $5-8 single tier for traffic and a $17-36 set tier for margin, and check monthly that the set tier is taking unit share.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads cottagecore / farmhouse (20.5% of its style markers) inside the vintage / retro lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 32.0% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 32.0% dead tail on 1,792 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 13.9% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $5.66), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 4,845 units per month of age over 73 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

bundle-assisted - CAD 5,966 in 30 days (Everbee, CAD). Engine B (single-print volume digital) in the folder framework - cheap, broad, no hero carrying it. Steady and workable, with no single structural lesson. Use it for lane and tag reference.

9. What to copy#

  • Vintage-family lane, 27.8% of lane-keyword hits. Vintage/Antique is the largest recent family in the folder at 7,758 six-month units - the deepest proven demand pool in this data.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 5.2% of listings and 5.6% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $4.99 median as a deliberate floor. Everything sits at the bottom of the ladder; the folder's own teardown puts digital-only at a $5.5-15k/mo ceiling in this set, so this is the traffic model, not the money model.
  • Flat but dead. Top 50 = 15.7% of units, top 5 = 9.5%, and 70.3% of listings sell nothing. The distribution is flat because there is little to distribute - this is a thin shop with a long tail of unsold listings, not a healthy many-winners catalogue.
  • Two-family lane: vintage / retro leads, botanical / floral second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Catalogue inflation without refresh. 1,648 listings at 0.75 units per listing is volume that is not converting. Expansion is not the lever here; refresh and curation are.
  • Cheap-single trap. $4.99 median across 1,648 listings is the slowest, most decay-prone model in the folder (NorthPrints took 73 months and 1,795 listings to reach what LoveDictionary does with 1,205 in 46). Volume feels productive and is the weakest shape in the data.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (5.6% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads botanical / floral study (30.7% of its style markers) inside the vintage / retro lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 70.3% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 70.3% dead tail on 1,500 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 15.7% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $4.99), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 124 units per month of age over 9 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • Do not mistake catalogue size for progress. 1,500 listings at 0.75 units each is expansion without conversion.

12. Verdict#

single-print - $889/mo (master CSV). Engine B (single-print volume digital) in the folder framework. Catalogue size is not the achievement here (1,648 listings at 0.75 units each); refresh rate is. Treat the listing count as a warning.

13. Sources and caveats#

  • Listing units here are 6-month (Est. Sales Last 6 Mo) from yw_listings.csv. The master CSV's Sales 6 mo and Rev / mo (est) columns are the shop-level versions of the same window.
  • Prices are the CSV's Price (hidden) column. Everbee estimates, ~1 month lag; the folder notes ~95% accuracy.
  • Row-count gap, disclosed: master_shops_final.csv reports 1,648 listings for this shop, but the listing CSV carries 1,500 rows (-148). Every listing-level share in this file uses the 1,500-row denominator, because that is what the listing columns actually cover. Treat the master figure as the shop-level count and the row figure as the analysable sample - the two are not the same population.
  • Bundle share, count-anchoring, duplicate-title and IP figures are computed here from the title and tag columns of the listing CSVs using the classifiers stated in each section. They are not Everbee fields, so they are estimates with a stated rule, not ground truth.
  • Prior shop comment carried in master_shops_final.csv: "Cottagecore, botanical and moody in one shop, 1,119 sales in six months, 889 a month, catalogue listed at 1,500 to 1,648 across the two records. Copy the pet portrait crossover inside a botanical shop. Avoid a catalogue past sales support."

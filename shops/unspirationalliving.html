9. What to copy#

  • Humour lane, 43.6% of lane-keyword hits. Kitchen/Humour plus Bathroom/Humour = 2,542 + 2,299 six-month units in the folder, and both are room-specific, so titles can name the room and stay honest.
  • Bundle-backward, executed. 56.5% of measured units come from the 9.6% of listings that are bundles, and 11 of the top 50 listings by units are bundles. That is the Lumiora / LoveDictionary / HarmonyPixels shape: design the mega set first, let the singles feed it.
  • $28.99 median - physical-tier pricing on the catalogue. The folder's evidence is that physical prints carry roughly 7.5x the revenue per unit of digital (RadialPosters), so this is the high-AOV shape, not a discounting shape.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 71.3% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 68.7% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Personalisation is the AOV engine. 56.2% of listings are custom/personalised and they take 86.4% of units. The folder's read: personalised digital is the bridge between cheap printables and the physical endgame.
  • Two-family lane: humour / kitchen-bathroom leads, personalised / pet second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Personalised lane, decayed. 86.4% of units are custom listings, but the median listing is 8.0 months old - the folder's fresh 30-day check rates the two personalised shops in it at 0.32-0.33x their own six-month pace. The shape is the highest-AOV pattern found and also the one decaying fastest.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Build the mega bundle first and let the singles feed it. This shop gets 56.5% of its units from the 9.6% of listings that are sets. The order matters: design the 200-print set, then extract the 20 component singles from it, then bundle again at 5.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads humour / pun novelty (83.7% of its style markers) inside the humour / kitchen-bathroom lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 68.7% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 68.7% dead tail on 249 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 71.3% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $20+ high-ticket band (this shop's median is $28.99), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 135 units per month of age over 15 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

bundle-led, personalisation-led, hero-concentrated - USD 3,515 in 30 days (Everbee, USD). Engine A (bundle-led digital) in the folder framework. The launch shape: bundles carry 56.5% of units and the catalogue is still refreshing.

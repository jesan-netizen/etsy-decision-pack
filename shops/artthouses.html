9. What to copy#

  • Vintage-family lane, 19.4% of lane-keyword hits. Vintage/Antique is the largest recent family in the folder at 7,758 six-month units - the deepest proven demand pool in this data.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 3.5% of listings and 0.0% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $9.5 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 74.5% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 96.6% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: vintage / retro leads, holiday / seasonal second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • IP risk, quantified: 8.5% of listings and 27.3% of units carry a licensed term (anime, barbie, tolkien). The folder's own finding is that 27.6% of young-shop rows ride licensed IP, that velocity is bought with takedown risk, and that zero-IP is the house rule. Do not model this revenue.
  • Catalogue duplication: 64.5% of listings sit in near-duplicate title groups (161 groups, 458 listings; same first 45 characters of the title). Whatever this does to ranking, it is not something to copy, and near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit.
  • 96.6% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.
  • No bundle ladder and a young catalogue. Bundles are 0.0% of units and 42.1% of listings are under 3 months old - the listings are too new to have been refreshed and there is no set tier to catch the buyers who want more than one print.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (0.0% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads typography / quote-led (23.7% of its style markers) inside the vintage / retro lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 96.6% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 96.6% dead tail on 710 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 74.5% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $9-13 band (this shop's median is $9.5), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 166 units per month of age over 10 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • This shop's subject matter is licensed (8.5% of listings, 27.3% of units). You can learn its mechanics and none of its subjects. Rebuild the lane with clean IP.
  • Do not copy the listing duplication (64.5% of listings in near-identical title groups). Near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit, not a ranking trick.
  • A 96.6% dead tail with almost no bundles is the profile of a catalogue that expanded without ever building the money tier. That is the specific failure to design against.
  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

IP-riding, single-print, hero-concentrated - $2,625/mo (master CSV). Hero-plus-dead-tail: one runaway listing over a catalogue that mostly sells nothing. The revenue is real but it is licensed-IP revenue (27.3% of units): unusable as a model and unsafe to copy. Rebuild the lane, not the shop.

9. What to copy#

  • A signed-sports catalogue, and the folder's clearest do-not-model. Name-specific tags are the discovery mechanism here, but the names are licensed. Read the tag mechanics, take none of the subjects.
  • Bundles as the margin tier, not the whole shop. 47.1% of units from 25.6% of listings, with 18 bundles in the top 50 - the ladder is working without the tail being dead weight.
  • $7.16 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 49.0% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 82.7% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: sports / fan art leads, humour / typography quote second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • IP risk, quantified: 51.5% of listings and 38.5% of units carry a licensed term (arsenal, barcelona, bayern, bodybuilder, chelsea, juventus, liverpool, manchester). The folder's own finding is that 27.6% of young-shop rows ride licensed IP, that velocity is bought with takedown risk, and that zero-IP is the house rule. Do not model this revenue.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Bundles are the margin tier here, not the whole shop (47.1% of units from 25.6% of listings). Build a $5-8 single tier for traffic and a $17-36 set tier for margin, and check monthly that the set tier is taking unit share.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • This shop earns $35.95 per listing without committing to an aesthetic. The corpus-wide comparison: shops with no named aesthetic earn far less per listing than shops that name one. Its lane (sports / fan art) is the borrowable part, not its undefined style.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 82.7% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 82.7% dead tail on 266 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 49.0% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $7.16), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 107 units per month of age over 9 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • This shop's subject matter is licensed (51.5% of listings, 38.5% of units). You can learn its mechanics and none of its subjects. Rebuild the lane with clean IP.
  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

IP-riding, bundle-assisted, hero-concentrated - $1,595/mo (master CSV). Bundle-assisted digital - between the folder A and B engines: bundles are the margin tier but the singles still carry most of the catalogue. The revenue is real but it is licensed-IP revenue (38.5% of units): unusable as a model and unsafe to copy. Rebuild the lane, not the shop.

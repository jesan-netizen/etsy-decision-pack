9. What to copy#

  • Vintage-family lane, 21.1% of lane-keyword hits. Vintage/Antique is the largest recent family in the folder at 7,758 six-month units - the deepest proven demand pool in this data.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 2.2% of listings and 0.0% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $6.6 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Worked power law. Top 50 = 35.8% of units, top 5 = 23.9%. A handful of listings carry the shop while the catalogue does the discoverability work.
  • Two-family lane: vintage / retro leads, landscape / nature second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • 97.1% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.
  • Catalogue inflation without refresh. 1,306 listings at 0.48 units per listing is volume that is not converting. Expansion is not the lever here; refresh and curation are.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (0.0% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads botanical / floral study (22.7% of its style markers) inside the vintage / retro lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 97.1% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 97.1% dead tail on 1,306 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 35.8% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $6.6), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 63 units per month of age over 10 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • Do not mistake catalogue size for progress. 1,306 listings at 0.48 units each is expansion without conversion.
  • A 97.1% dead tail with almost no bundles is the profile of a catalogue that expanded without ever building the money tier. That is the specific failure to design against.
  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

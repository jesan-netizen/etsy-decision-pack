9. What to copy#

  • Frame-TV art lane, 48.5% of lane-keyword hits. Frame TV / TV art = 3,176 six-month units in the folder. At a $4.16 median it is a traffic floor, and it works because the buyer already owns the frame.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 0.2% of listings and 0.0% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $4.16 median as a deliberate floor. Everything sits at the bottom of the ladder; the folder's own teardown puts digital-only at a $5.5-15k/mo ceiling in this set, so this is the traffic model, not the money model.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 96.3% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 97.3% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: frame tv art leads, landscape / nature second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • 97.3% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (0.0% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads cottagecore / farmhouse (27.9% of its style markers) inside the frame tv art lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 97.3% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 97.3% dead tail on 410 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 96.3% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $4.16), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 84 units per month of age over 9 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • A 97.3% dead tail with almost no bundles is the profile of a catalogue that expanded without ever building the money tier. That is the specific failure to design against.
  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

single-print, hero-concentrated - $524/mo (master CSV). Engine B (single-print volume digital) in the folder framework. A hero shop, not a catalogue shop: the top 50 listings carry 96.3% of units while 97.3% of the catalogue sells nothing. Design for the hero, run the rest as discovery.

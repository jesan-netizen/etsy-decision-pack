9. What to copy#

  • Humour lane, 17.8% of lane-keyword hits. Kitchen/Humour plus Bathroom/Humour = 2,542 + 2,299 six-month units in the folder, and both are room-specific, so titles can name the room and stay honest.
  • Bundles as the margin tier, not the whole shop. 44.1% of units from 29.4% of listings, with 22 bundles in the top 50 - the ladder is working without the tail being dead weight.
  • $9.29 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Worked power law. Top 50 = 31.4% of units, top 5 = 21.8%. A handful of listings carry the shop while the catalogue does the discoverability work.
  • Personalisation is the AOV engine. 15.6% of listings are custom/personalised and they take 34.8% of units. The folder's read: personalised digital is the bridge between cheap printables and the physical endgame.
  • Two-family lane: humour / typography quote leads, vintage / retro second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Nothing in this shop's own numbers trips a structural warning. The risks here are the generic ones: fee stack on low price points, and the fact that a single-lane shop has no second leg when the lane cools.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Bundles are the margin tier here, not the whole shop (44.1% of units from 29.4% of listings). Build a $5-8 single tier for traffic and a $17-36 set tier for margin, and check monthly that the set tier is taking unit share.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads typography / quote-led (35.3% of its style markers) inside the humour / typography quote lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 48.2% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 48.2% dead tail on 1,416 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 31.4% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $9-13 band (this shop's median is $9.29), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 241 units per month of age over 42 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

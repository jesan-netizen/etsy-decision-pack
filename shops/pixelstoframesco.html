9. What to copy#

  • Frame-TV art lane, 30.4% of lane-keyword hits. Frame TV / TV art = 3,176 six-month units in the folder. At a $6.92 median it is a traffic floor, and it works because the buyer already owns the frame.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 5.3% of listings and 3.0% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $6.92 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 47.2% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 47.9% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: frame tv art leads, holiday / seasonal second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • No bundle ladder and a young catalogue. Bundles are 3.0% of units and 27.0% of listings are under 3 months old - the listings are too new to have been refreshed and there is no set tier to catch the buyers who want more than one print.
  • Tag diffusion. Only 9.8% of filled tag slots fall in the top 10 tags across 1,047 listings - 4,625 distinct tags for 1,047 listings. No phrase is being defended, so the shop is paying for 13 slots per listing and defending none of them.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (3.0% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads cottagecore / farmhouse (23.4% of its style markers) inside the frame tv art lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 47.9% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 47.9% dead tail on 1,047 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 47.2% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $6.92), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 367 units per month of age over 18 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

single-print, hero-concentrated - CAD 3,486 in 30 days (Everbee, CAD). Mid-weight digital catalogue: a 47.9% dead tail, top 10 listings at 47.2% of units, little bundle contribution. No engine feature dominates - it runs on catalogue size and lane fit. A hero shop, not a catalogue shop: the top 50 listings carry 47.2% of units while 47.9% of the catalogue sells nothing. Design for the hero, run the rest as discovery.

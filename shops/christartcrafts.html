9. What to copy#

  • Vintage-family lane, 15.9% of lane-keyword hits. Vintage/Antique is the largest recent family in the folder at 7,758 six-month units - the deepest proven demand pool in this data.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 4.7% of listings and 0.8% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $7 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 81.3% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 96.3% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: landscape / nature leads, botanical / floral second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • 96.3% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.
  • No bundle ladder and a young catalogue. Bundles are 0.8% of units and 24.8% of listings are under 3 months old - the listings are too new to have been refreshed and there is no set tier to catch the buyers who want more than one print.
  • Tag diffusion. Only 8.2% of filled tag slots fall in the top 10 tags across 903 listings - 4,941 distinct tags for 903 listings. No phrase is being defended, so the shop is paying for 13 slots per listing and defending none of them.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (0.8% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads landscape / nature vista (22.4% of its style markers) inside the landscape / nature lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 96.3% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 96.3% dead tail on 896 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 81.3% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $7), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 150 units per month of age over 9 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • A 96.3% dead tail with almost no bundles is the profile of a catalogue that expanded without ever building the money tier. That is the specific failure to design against.
  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

single-print, hero-concentrated - USD 1,674 in 30 days (Everbee, USD). Hero-plus-dead-tail: one runaway listing over a catalogue that mostly sells nothing. A hero shop, not a catalogue shop: the top 50 listings carry 81.3% of units while 96.3% of the catalogue sells nothing. Design for the hero, run the rest as discovery.

  • Cross-reference (28 Sep 2026 Momentum Layer note, not from these CSVs): 991 units in 6 months, 12-month 1,328, state accelerating, recent-family split Christian/Religious 707 + Nursery/Kids 87.

  • Row-count gap, disclosed: master_shops_final.csv reports 903 listings for this shop, but the listing CSV carries 896 rows (-7). Every listing-level share in this file uses the 896-row denominator, because that is what the listing columns actually cover. Treat the master figure as the shop-level count and the row figure as the analysable sample - the two are not the same population.
  • Bundle share, count-anchoring, duplicate-title and IP figures are computed here from the title and tag columns of the listing CSVs using the classifiers stated in each section. They are not Everbee fields, so they are estimates with a stated rule, not ground truth.

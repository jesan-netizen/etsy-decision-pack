Etsy case files - index

75 shops. Compiled 2 Oct 2026 from /home/ubuntu/etsy_phase2/*.csv only (master_shops_final.csv, yw_listings.csv, shops17_listings.csv, everbee_starred_shops.csv, everbee_starred_shops_comments.csv, young_winners_comments.csv, dead_shop_listings.csv).

Every number in every case file is read from those CSVs or computed from their columns by a rule stated in the file that uses it. No figure is hand-estimated. Currencies are native per shop - no FX conversion is applied, so cross-shop revenue comparison is approximate.

How to read a case file##

Section What it is
Engine line Folder A/B/C/D classification, derived from bundle share, median price, digital ratio and dead-tail share
2. Lane, aesthetic, format The three independent axes, each with its own confidence note
4. Price distribution Deciles, the real band histogram, modal price, IQR ratio, and what the price curve means
5. Top listings Up to 15, with share of the shop's units, revenue proxy, and the bundle / count-anchor flags
8. Trajectory Velocity normalised by shop age, plus the listing-age histogram. Cross-sectional, not a time series
11. What a new shop could learn The mechanic, the lane, the operating numbers and the specific trap, all from this shop own figures
Scorecard Shop level, from master_shops_final.csv and everbee_starred_shops.csv
3. Shape of the book Listing-level roll-up, zero-sale share, top-5/top-10 unit concentration, revenue proxy
6. Bundle share Bundle-titled listings and the unit share they carry, computed here from titles by a stated classifier
7. Tag strategy Counted from the 13 tag slots: slots filled, distinct tags, top-10 coverage, average reuse
9 / 10. Copy / avoid Each bullet anchored to a counted figure from that shop
12. Verdict One line, built from the engine classification and this shop's own numbers
13. Sources and caveats The units basis, the currency, the row-count gap, and the prior folder comment

Two different units bases exist across this set, and every file says which one it uses:

  • Young Winners shops (56) - listing units are Est. Sales Last 6 Mo. A real 6-month window.
  • Starred shops (17) - shops17_listings.csv carries only Est. Total Sales (lifetime). Every listing-level share in those files is therefore a lifetime share, not a recent-velocity share. Where the 28 Sep Momentum Layer note has a 6-month figure, it is quoted as an attributed cross-reference only.

Engine distribution##

The folder framework (Print Shops Pattern Map, 28 Sep) has four engines: A bundle-led digital, B single-print volume digital, C physical mid-price, D hero listing + high-ticket hybrid. Applying it to all 75 shops leaves a long tail that the four-engine set does not describe, so the classifier below names those shapes explicitly rather than forcing them into A/B/C/D. The label on each case file is derived from bundle share, median price, digital ratio, dead-tail share and top-10 concentration - all counted, none hand-assigned.

Shape Shops
Engine B (single-print volume digital) 25
Engine A (bundle-led digital) 13
Long-tail digital 11

Hero-plus-dead-tail 6
Mid-weight digital 5
Engine C (physical mid-price winner) 1
Personalisation-led 1
Engine D (hero listing + high-ticket hybrid) 1

The four that matter for a launch decision: Engine A is the only engine the two young risers in the folder share (LumioraPrints, RadialPosters), and it is the folder's recommended launch state. Engine B is the cheapest and slowest. Engine C is a 74-month-old shop's state, not a launch state. Engine D is the endgame. The "long-tail", "mid-weight" and "hero-plus-dead-tail" labels mark shops with no dominant mechanic - useful as lane reference, not as a blueprint.

Cross-reference: the direction catalogue##

These 75 shops sit inside a larger direction analysis. A direction there is three axes - product lane + aesthetic + shape strategy - classified on every one of the 40,275 listing rows in the corpus, not per shop. That document is the input to a per-direction research project; these case files are the shop-level detail behind it.

  • ../directions_from_data.md - 22 qualified lanes, 14 aesthetics, 120 qualified directions, with units, revenue proxy, yield per listing and price bands for each
  • ../directions.json / ../directions_qualified.json - the same aggregates as data

Reading a case file through the direction lens: section 2 of each profile gives that shop's three axes, and section 5 shows which listings sit in which shape cell. Where a shop's numbers beat the direction average, the profile says so in the verdict.

What the set says, in one pass##

  • Bundle share splits the folder. Across the 73 shops with listing data, bundle-titled listings range from 0.0% to 93.6% of the catalogue, and carry from 0.0% to 100.0% of units. Bundle-led and single-print-led shops sit in the same folder and both work - the mechanic to copy is the bundle ladder, not the bundle share.
  • Tag discipline splits just as hard as the engines do. Top-10 tag coverage across the 72 shops with tag data runs 6.1% to 63.1%: 3 shops sit at 45%+ (a tight repeated vocabulary) and 47 sit under 20% (a diffuse one). The tight-cluster shops are the ones with a lane you could describe in a sentence, and the diffuse ones are spending 13 tag slots per listing without defending a phrase.
  • The dead tail is universal and should be budgeted for. Power-law shares here run from a few per cent of listings with zero sales to over 95%. A shop that tells you 90% of its listings sell nothing is not confessing, it is describing the model.
  • The four closed shops in the young sweep are the honest control. They ran the same mechanics as the live ones and still shut. Mechanics transfer; outcomes do not.
  • IP-riding revenue is present, quantified, and unusable. 5 shops carry licensed terms in at least 5% of their titles, the worst at 66.6% of units. It is the fastest money in the folder and the fastest way to lose the shop.

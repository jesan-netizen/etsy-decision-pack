9. What to copy#

  • Music/album lane, 94.0% of lane-keyword hits. Music/Album poster = 1,762 six-month units in the folder, concentrated in RadialPosters, where one personalised listing does the heavy lifting.
  • Bundles as the margin tier, not the whole shop. 26.8% of units from 22.9% of listings, with 30 bundles in the top 50 - the ladder is working without the tail being dead weight.
  • Count-anchored titles. 21.7% of the catalogue leads with a number plus a noun (9 of the top 20 do). The count hook is the most consistent title formula across all 17 shops in the folder.
  • $106.8 median - physical-tier pricing on the catalogue. The folder's evidence is that physical prints carry roughly 7.5x the revenue per unit of digital (RadialPosters), so this is the high-AOV shape, not a discounting shape.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 73.1% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 87.6% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Personalisation is the AOV engine. 1.0% of listings are custom/personalised and they take 60.0% of units. The folder's read: personalised digital is the bridge between cheap printables and the physical endgame.
  • Two-family lane: music / album leads, nursery / kids second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • 87.6% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.
  • Conversion reads 9.96, far above the group. That is a denominator artefact on a small view base (61,633 lifetime views), not a real efficiency edge.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Bundles are the margin tier here, not the whole shop (26.8% of units from 22.9% of listings). Build a $5-8 single tier for traffic and a $17-36 set tier for margin, and check monthly that the set tier is taking unit share.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • This shop earns $125.60 per listing without committing to an aesthetic. The corpus-wide comparison: shops with no named aesthetic earn far less per listing than shops that name one. Its lane (music / album) is the borrowable part, not its undefined style.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 87.6% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 87.6% dead tail on 420 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 73.1% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $20+ high-ticket band (this shop's median is $106.8), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 117 units per month of age over 16 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

bundle-assisted, physical-tier pricing, personalisation-led, hero-concentrated - AUD 17,453 in 30 days (Everbee, AUD). Engine D (hero listing + high-ticket hybrid) in the folder framework. The endgame shape in the folder: physical-tier pricing, 65.7% of units in the top 5 listings, and personalisation doing the AOV work. This is where a digital shop grows into, not where it launches.

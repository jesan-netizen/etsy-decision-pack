9. What to copy#

  • Seasonal lane, 25.6% of lane-keyword hits. Seasonality is a reskin engine, not a lane: the same template re-dated for the next occasion is how a flat catalogue keeps a revenue line without new design work.
  • Bundles as the margin tier, not the whole shop. 45.5% of units from 10.9% of listings, with 20 bundles in the top 50 - the ladder is working without the tail being dead weight.
  • $12.5 median - above the volume band. Buyers here are paying for a specific room, a specific style, or personalisation. The $17-36 tier is the money tier in the folder's price ladder.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 42.7% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 96.6% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: holiday / seasonal leads, vintage / retro second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • 96.6% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.
  • Catalogue inflation without refresh. 1,950 listings at 0.52 units per listing is volume that is not converting. Expansion is not the lever here; refresh and curation are.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Bundles are the margin tier here, not the whole shop (45.5% of units from 10.9% of listings). Build a $5-8 single tier for traffic and a $17-36 set tier for margin, and check monthly that the set tier is taking unit share.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads coastal / coastal-grandmother (18.6% of its style markers) inside the holiday / seasonal lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 96.6% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 96.6% dead tail on 1,500 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 42.7% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $9-13 band (this shop's median is $12.5), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 65 units per month of age over 12 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • Do not mistake catalogue size for progress. 1,500 listings at 0.52 units each is expansion without conversion.

13. Sources and caveats#

  • Listing units here are 6-month (Est. Sales Last 6 Mo) from yw_listings.csv. The master CSV's Sales 6 mo and Rev / mo (est) columns are the shop-level versions of the same window.
  • Prices are the CSV's Price (hidden) column. Everbee estimates, ~1 month lag; the folder notes ~95% accuracy.
  • Row-count gap, disclosed: master_shops_final.csv reports 1,950 listings for this shop, but the listing CSV carries 1,500 rows (-450). Every listing-level share in this file uses the 1,500-row denominator, because that is what the listing columns actually cover. Treat the master figure as the shop-level count and the row figure as the analysable sample - the two are not the same population.
  • Bundle share, count-anchoring, duplicate-title and IP figures are computed here from the title and tag columns of the listing CSVs using the classifiers stated in each section. They are not Everbee fields, so they are estimates with a stated rule, not ground truth.

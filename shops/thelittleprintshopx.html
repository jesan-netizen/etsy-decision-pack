9. What to copy#

  • Humour lane, 71.2% of lane-keyword hits. Kitchen/Humour plus Bathroom/Humour = 2,542 + 2,299 six-month units in the folder, and both are room-specific, so titles can name the room and stay honest.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 4.7% of listings and 4.2% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $13.36 median - above the volume band. Buyers here are paying for a specific room, a specific style, or personalisation. The $17-36 tier is the money tier in the folder's price ladder.
  • Worked power law. Top 50 = 30.0% of units, top 5 = 22.7%. A handful of listings carry the shop while the catalogue does the discoverability work.
  • 11.7% of listings have zero measured sales - the lowest dead-tail share in this set. That is the sign of a catalogue that is being refreshed, not merely expanded.
  • Two-family lane: humour / kitchen-bathroom leads, humour / typography quote second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Nothing in this shop's own numbers trips a structural warning. The risks here are the generic ones: fee stack on low price points, and the fact that a single-lane shop has no second leg when the lane cools.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (4.2% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads humour / pun novelty (52.1% of its style markers) inside the humour / kitchen-bathroom lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 11.7% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 11.7% dead tail on 549 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 30.0% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $14-20 money band (this shop's median is $13.36), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 471 units per month of age over 74 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

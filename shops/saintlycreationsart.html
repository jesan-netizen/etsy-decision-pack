9. What to copy#

  • Faith lane, 95.9% of lane-keyword hits. The lane is evergreen in this folder - it holds a 6-month family share of 1,542 units across the 17 starred shops - and it personalises at a higher price than the median print.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 0.0% of listings and 0.0% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $3.99 median as a deliberate floor. Everything sits at the bottom of the ladder; the folder's own teardown puts digital-only at a $5.5-15k/mo ceiling in this set, so this is the traffic model, not the money model.
  • Flat but dead. Top 50 = 11.8% of units, top 5 = 7.3%, and 81.0% of listings sell nothing. The distribution is flat because there is little to distribute - this is a thin shop with a long tail of unsold listings, not a healthy many-winners catalogue.
  • Two-family lane: faith / christian leads, botanical / floral second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Catalogue duplication: 51.1% of listings sit in near-duplicate title groups (168 groups, 599 listings; same first 45 characters of the title). Whatever this does to ranking, it is not something to copy, and near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit.
  • Catalogue inflation without refresh. 1,172 listings at 1.77 units per listing is volume that is not converting. Expansion is not the lever here; refresh and curation are.
  • No bundle ladder and a young catalogue. Bundles are 0.0% of units and 34.6% of listings are under 3 months old - the listings are too new to have been refreshed and there is no set tier to catch the buyers who want more than one print.
  • Cheap-single trap. $3.99 median across 1,172 listings is the slowest, most decay-prone model in the folder (NorthPrints took 73 months and 1,795 listings to reach what LoveDictionary does with 1,205 in 46). Volume feels productive and is the weakest shape in the data.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (0.0% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads vintage / antique oil painting (97.8% of its style markers) inside the faith / christian lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 81.0% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 81.0% dead tail on 1,172 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 11.8% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a sub-$3.50 traffic floor (this shop's median is $3.99), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 414 units per month of age over 5 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • Do not copy the listing duplication (51.1% of listings in near-identical title groups). Near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit, not a ranking trick.
  • Do not mistake catalogue size for progress. 1,172 listings at 1.77 units each is expansion without conversion.

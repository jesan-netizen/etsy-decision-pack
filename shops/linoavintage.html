9. What to copy#

  • Nursery/kids lane, the dominant family at 56.7% of lane-keyword hits. Nursery is the second-deepest proven family in the folder (2,572 six-month units across the 17) and it takes a price premium because buyers are decorating a room, not buying one print.
  • Bundles as the margin tier, not the whole shop. 29.2% of units from 39.1% of listings, with 26 bundles in the top 50 - the ladder is working without the tail being dead weight.
  • $9.46 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 76.7% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 93.6% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: nursery / kids leads, botanical / floral second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • This shop is closed (status: DEAD (closed, verified 30 Sep), verified 30 Sep). Treat it as a post-mortem, not a model. 468 listings and 1,101 six-month sales say the playbook ran and the shop still did not survive - read the what was copied column for the mechanics and ignore the outcome.
  • 93.6% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Bundles are the margin tier here, not the whole shop (29.2% of units from 39.1% of listings). Build a $5-8 single tier for traffic and a $17-36 set tier for margin, and check monthly that the set tier is taking unit share.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads botanical / floral study (38.7% of its style markers) inside the nursery / kids lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 93.6% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 93.6% dead tail on 468 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 76.7% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $9-13 band (this shop's median is $9.46), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 110 units per month of age over 10 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The biggest lesson is that it closed. Mechanics transfer between shops; the outcome does not. Copy the shape, not the size, and monitor refresh rate rather than listing count.
  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

bundle-assisted, hero-concentrated, CLOSED - $3,284/mo (master CSV). Hero-plus-dead-tail: one runaway listing over a catalogue that mostly sells nothing. Mechanics worth lifting: the nursery / kids lane discipline and the 29.2%-of-units bundle ratio. Do not lift the catalogue size - 468 listings and 1,101 six-month sales did not keep the shop open.

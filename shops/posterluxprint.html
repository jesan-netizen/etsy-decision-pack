9. What to copy#

  • Y2K / leopard aesthetic, 54.7% of lane-keyword hits. Leopard/Y2K = 1,133 six-month units in the folder, but treat as one operator: the folder's own note records PixeluxArt and PosterluxPrint as near-clones on the same Stockholm/leopard/Y2K titles, so do not double-count the aesthetic.
  • Bundle-backward, executed. 80.2% of measured units come from the 34.0% of listings that are bundles, and 20 of the top 50 listings by units are bundles. That is the Lumiora / LoveDictionary / HarmonyPixels shape: design the mega set first, let the singles feed it.
  • Count-anchored titles. 18.6% of the catalogue leads with a number plus a noun (10 of the top 20 do). The count hook is the most consistent title formula across all 17 shops in the folder.
  • $6.28 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 100.0% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 94.9% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: y2k / leopard / fashion leads, college / dorm second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • 94.9% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Build the mega bundle first and let the singles feed it. This shop gets 80.2% of its units from the 34.0% of listings that are sets. The order matters: design the 200-print set, then extract the 20 component singles from it, then bundle again at 5.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads y2k / it-girl / leopard (64.0% of its style markers) inside the y2k / leopard / fashion lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 94.9% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 94.9% dead tail on 156 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 100.0% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $6.28), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 235 units per month of age over 2 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.

12. Verdict#

bundle-led, hero-concentrated - EUR 5,877 in 30 days (Everbee, EUR). Engine A (bundle-led digital) in the folder framework. A hero shop, not a catalogue shop: the top 50 listings carry 100.0% of units while 94.9% of the catalogue sells nothing. Design for the hero, run the rest as discovery.

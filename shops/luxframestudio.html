9. What to copy#

  • Frame-TV art lane, 59.5% of lane-keyword hits. Frame TV / TV art = 3,176 six-month units in the folder. At a $5.01 median it is a traffic floor, and it works because the buyer already owns the frame.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 7.9% of listings and 16.4% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $5.01 median as a deliberate floor. Everything sits at the bottom of the ladder; the folder's own teardown puts digital-only at a $5.5-15k/mo ceiling in this set, so this is the traffic model, not the money model.
  • Worked power law. Top 50 = 34.7% of units, top 5 = 24.5%. A handful of listings carry the shop while the catalogue does the discoverability work.
  • Two-family lane: frame tv art leads, holiday / seasonal second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • 88.3% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.
  • Catalogue inflation without refresh. 1,424 listings at 0.23 units per listing is volume that is not converting. Expansion is not the lever here; refresh and curation are.
  • Cheap-single trap. $5.01 median across 1,424 listings is the slowest, most decay-prone model in the folder (NorthPrints took 73 months and 1,795 listings to reach what LoveDictionary does with 1,205 in 46). Volume feels productive and is the weakest shape in the data.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (16.4% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads landscape / nature vista (23.8% of its style markers) inside the frame tv art lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 88.3% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 88.3% dead tail on 1,424 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 34.7% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $5.01), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 29 units per month of age over 11 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • Do not mistake catalogue size for progress. 1,424 listings at 0.23 units each is expansion without conversion.

12. Verdict#

single-print - $294/mo (master CSV). Engine B (single-print volume digital) in the folder framework. Catalogue size is not the achievement here (1,424 listings at 0.23 units each); refresh rate is. Treat the listing count as a warning.

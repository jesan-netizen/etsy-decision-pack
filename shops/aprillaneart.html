9. What to copy#

  • Seasonal lane, 33.1% of lane-keyword hits. Seasonality is a reskin engine, not a lane: the same template re-dated for the next occasion is how a flat catalogue keeps a revenue line without new design work.
  • Bundles as the margin tier, not the whole shop. 20.7% of units from 10.9% of listings, with 12 bundles in the top 50 - the ladder is working without the tail being dead weight.
  • $8 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Flat distribution. Top 50 = 13.3% of units, top 5 = 7.5%, with only 10.2% of listings dead - no hero carries the shop, which is the healthiest shape in the folder and the hardest to build on purpose.
  • 10.2% of listings have zero measured sales - the lowest dead-tail share in this set. That is the sign of a catalogue that is being refreshed, not merely expanded.
  • Aged, seasoned catalogue. Median listing age 35.0 months across 1,650 listings; the winners have had years to compound.
  • Two-family lane: holiday / seasonal leads, abstract / minimal second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • Catalogue duplication: 45.3% of listings sit in near-duplicate title groups (246 groups, 726 listings; same first 45 characters of the title). Whatever this does to ranking, it is not something to copy, and near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit.
  • Tag diffusion. Only 9.5% of filled tag slots fall in the top 10 tags across 1,650 listings - 3,149 distinct tags for 1,650 listings. No phrase is being defended, so the shop is paying for 13 slots per listing and defending none of them.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • Bundles are the margin tier here, not the whole shop (20.7% of units from 10.9% of listings). Build a $5-8 single tier for traffic and a $17-36 set tier for margin, and check monthly that the set tier is taking unit share.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads botanical / floral study (20.0% of its style markers) inside the holiday / seasonal lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 10.2% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 10.2% dead tail on 1,601 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 13.3% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $8), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 537 units per month of age over 75 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • Do not copy the listing duplication (45.3% of listings in near-identical title groups). Near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit, not a ranking trick.

  • Cross-reference (28 Sep 2026 Momentum Layer note, not from these CSVs): 1,841 units in 6 months, 12-month 4,595, state mixed, recent-family split Gallery Wall 466 + Other 255.
  • Prices are the CSV's Price (hidden) column. Everbee estimates, ~1 month lag; the folder notes ~95% accuracy.
  • Row-count gap, disclosed: master_shops_final.csv reports 1,650 listings for this shop, but the listing CSV carries 1,601 rows (-49). Every listing-level share in this file uses the 1,601-row denominator, because that is what the listing columns actually cover. Treat the master figure as the shop-level count and the row figure as the analysable sample - the two are not the same population.
  • Bundle share, count-anchoring, duplicate-title and IP figures are computed here from the title and tag columns of the listing CSVs using the classifiers stated in each section. They are not Everbee fields, so they are estimates with a stated rule, not ground truth.

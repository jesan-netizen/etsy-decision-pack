9. What to copy#

  • A 5-listing catalogue, not a catalogue model. At this size the whole shop is a handful of products and every share below is a share of a tiny denominator. Read the top-5 listings and the price point; ignore any 'catalogue strategy' framing.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 0.0% of listings and 0.0% of units - this shop sells singles only, with no set tier at all.
  • $7.22 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 5 listings = 100.0% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 60.0% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.

10. What to avoid#

  • Catalogue duplication: 60.0% of listings sit in near-duplicate title groups (1 group, 3 listings; same first 45 characters of the title). Whatever this does to ranking, it is not something to copy, and near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit.
  • No bundle ladder and a young catalogue. Bundles are 0.0% of units and 40.0% of listings are under 3 months old - the listings are too new to have been refreshed and there is no set tier to catch the buyers who want more than one print.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (0.0% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • This shop earns $1,663.49 per listing without committing to an aesthetic. The corpus-wide comparison: shops with no named aesthetic earn far less per listing than shops that name one. Its lane (personalised) is the borrowable part, not its undefined style.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 60.0% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 60.0% dead tail on 5 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 5 listings take 100.0% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $7.22), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 144 units per month of age over 8 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • Do not copy the listing duplication (60.0% of listings in near-identical title groups). Near-duplicate listing at scale is a takedown-adjacent habit, not a ranking trick.

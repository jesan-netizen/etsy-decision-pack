9. What to copy#

  • Seasonal lane, 21.8% of lane-keyword hits. Seasonality is a reskin engine, not a lane: the same template re-dated for the next occasion is how a flat catalogue keeps a revenue line without new design work.
  • Single-print model. Bundles are 9.4% of listings and 6.0% of units - this shop competes on catalogue breadth and price point, not on set size.
  • $6 median in the volume band. Cheap enough to convert on impulse, high enough that the fee stack still clears. This is the folder's proven launch band.
  • Concentration is the design, not an accident. Top 50 listings = 66.4% of units. Plan for heroes, budget for 89.9% of the catalogue selling nothing, and do not read the dead tail as failure.
  • Two-family lane: holiday / seasonal leads, botanical / floral second. That is breadth inside one aesthetic, not style-hopping - the distinction the folder's Lumiora teardown turns on. The folder's own note on Lumiora is that the style experiments mostly died while one aesthetic kept paying, so treat the second family as a reskin wing, not a second business.

10. What to avoid#

  • 89.9% of the catalogue sold zero in the measured window. Breadth without curation is churn - the folder's own note on DigitalStudioArtUA puts it plainly: its volume came from a few hundred listings, not 1,678.

11. What a new shop could learn from this one#

The mechanic to take.

  • This shop sells almost entirely singles (6.0% of units from bundles), which the corpus says is the weakest money shape per listing. If you copy its lane, do not copy this part: add a set tier in month one.

The lane and aesthetic to take.

  • Commit to the aesthetic before the catalogue: this shop reads botanical / floral study (19.6% of its style markers) inside the holiday / seasonal lane. Name one style, then serve several niches inside it.

The operating numbers to plan against.

  • Expect 89.9% of your listings to sell zero and budget for it. This shop carries a 89.9% dead tail on 287 listings, which is the going rate in this corpus - not a sign of a mistake.
  • Design for a hero, not a uniform catalogue. This shop's top 50 listings take 66.4% of units. One good listing carries the month; the rest is discoverability cost.
  • Price into a $5-8 volume band (this shop's median is $6), then climb the ladder with set size rather than with per-unit price. The corpus's own reading: money is made on bundles, not low-ticket singles.
  • Use velocity per month of trading as the early signal, not raw units. This shop did 117 units per month of age over 8 months. A new shop should be able to say what its own number is at month 3.

The trap in this shop specifically.

  • The generic traps this shop does not fall into: no IP exposure, no near-duplicate listing pile, no catalogue inflation. Copy the discipline as much as the product.
